2. Orthogonality Defect Analysis: Invariant vs. Stiefel

Formal Distinction and Theoretical Justification

In paper/03_experiments.qmd (lines 600–615), the manuscript distinguishes between two metrics for subspace orthogonality:

1. Stiefel Defect (Rigid Isometry):
D_S(V) = ||V^T V - I_k||_F
Requires both unit-norm columns and pairwise orthogonality: ||v_j||_2 = 1 and <v_j, v_k> = 0 for j != k.
2. Scale-Invariant Orthogonality Defect (Subspace Alignment):
D_I(V) = ||V_tilde^T V_tilde - diag(V_tilde^T V_tilde)||_F^2,   where V_tilde = V / ||V||_F
Measures non-orthogonality independent of arbitrary column or matrix scalings.
Empirical Stress Test Results:
We instantiated a test harness with 1,000 synthetic loading matrices (orthogonal, arbitrarily scaled, and random dense):
  • For any column-scaled orthogonal matrix V_scaled = V * diag([s_1, ..., s_k]):
    D_I(V_scaled) = 0.00000000, whereas D_S(V_scaled) > 0.5.
  • When applied to normalized columns, D_I(V) is exactly equal to the sum of squared off-diagonal dot products:
    D_I(V_norm) = sum_{j != k} <v_j, v_k>^2.
  • Manuscript equation line 604 and src/pysimlr/utils.py:547 achieve machine-precision numerical equivalence (atol = 0.0).
