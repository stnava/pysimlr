3-Stage Support-Masked Riemannian Polish Pipeline

Standard $L_1$ lasso penalties shrink all coefficients simultaneously, flattening predictive signals and introducing coordinate shear. PySiMLR decouples direction finding from sparsity enforcement via three sequential stages:
Stage 1
Full-Support Riemannian Flow
Solves Riemannian flow on the Stiefel manifold at convex weight w = 0.5:
Ew(Vm) = (1 − w)ℒfid + w D(Vm)
Produces well-conditioned, orthogonal candidate loadings without shrinkage bias.
→
Stage 2
Order-Statistic Support Mask
Identifies (sm + 1)-th largest element τm,l per column for target quantile q:
Mm,jl = 1(|Vm,jl| > τm,l)
Applies only_if_denser=True and resolves column sign-gauge (vj ← −vj) before non-negative clamping.
→
Stage 3
Masked Stiefel Polish
Freezes zero entries and re-optimizes exclusively on the pinned active support:
Vm ← ΠStiefel(Mm ⊙ Vm)
Guarantees exact support recovery while driving Stiefel defect D(Vm) ≤ 10−4.

Three Probes: Subspace Fidelity (Linear XV) vs. Single-Axis Interpretability (Random Forest XV)

Core Methodological Discovery: Ordinary least squares linear regression on $\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}$ is invariant under orthogonal rotations $\mathbf{R} \in \mathrm{O}(k)$, making it blind to diagonal coordinate mixing. In contrast, decision tree ensembles perform axis-orthogonal splits ($z_j \le \theta$). On ADNI-MCI, PCA suffers an axis drop of −0.1081 (>70% collapse from 0.1522 to 0.0441), while Flow-SiMR-V drops by only −0.0342 (0.2311 to 0.1969). Across all 12 benchmark cohorts, PySiMLR architectures cut the axis drop in half relative to PCA (−0.0217 to −0.0283 vs. −0.0439).
