\chapter*{Abstract}\label{abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

Integrating structural neuroimaging, cognitive assessments, and
multi-omic profiles requires latent representations that remain both
clinically predictive and anatomically or molecularly localizable.
Classical linear factorizations such as principal component analysis and
canonical correlation analysis disperse biological variance across
dense, sign-mixed combinations of features, whereas end-to-end deep
neural networks obscure how individual brain regions or genes drive
patient-level predictions. We introduce the Similarity-driven Multi-view
Representation (SiMR) framework, which couples a sparse, non-negative,
approximately orthogonal linear input projection (\texttt{nsa\_flow})
with interchangeable linear (\texttt{SiMLR}), non-linear decoder
(\texttt{LEND}), and invertible normalizing-flow (\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V})
alignment modules. Across \(1,084\) participants with Mild Cognitive
Impairment from the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative
(\texttt{ADNI-MCI}) and eleven additional multi-omic, clinical, and
synthetic benchmarks, aligning baseline cortical thickness and
subcortical volumes with eleven neuropsychological scales during
training improves out-of-sample cognitive prediction from structural
magnetic resonance imaging alone from \(R^2 = 0.152\) to \(R^2 = 0.252\)
while isolating focal temporoparietal, medial temporal, and
frontocingulate networks that recapitulate Braak neurofibrillary staging
and spare primary sensorimotor cortex. Crucially, evaluating learned
representations across both multi-variable linear probes and single-axis
decision trees demonstrates that while linear regression is
algebraically invariant to post-hoc column mixing, coordinate-aligned
SiMR projections preserve more than fourfold higher single-factor
predictive utility (\(R^2 = 0.197\) versus \(0.044\)) and recover up to
\(84.7\%\) of ground-truth biological pathways under moderate (\(50\%\))
feature sparsity.

\chapter{Introduction and Background}\label{introduction-and-background}

\section{Clinical and Scientific Motivation: Why Multi-View Integration
Must Be
Auditable}\label{clinical-and-scientific-motivation-why-multi-view-integration-must-be-auditable}

Modern biomedical cohorts characterize disease across distinct physical,
physiological, and behavioral scales. In neurodegenerative disorders
such as Alzheimer's disease, structural magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)
quantifies millimeter-scale regional cortical thinning and subcortical
volume loss ({Jack et al.} 2018; {Dickerson et al.} 2009; Fischl and
Dale 2000; Tustison et al. 2014, 2021; Avants, Tustison, Song, et al.
2011; Das et al. 2009), whereas clinical diagnosis and trial monitoring
rely on multi-domain neuropsychological batteries measuring episodic
memory, executive function, language, and daily functional independence
(Rosen et al. 1984; Folstein et al. 1975; Morris 1993; Pfeffer et al.
1982; Reitan 1958; Rey 1964). In clinical oncology, projects such as The
Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) profile primary tumors simultaneously across
messenger RNA (mRNA) transcriptomics, somatic DNA copy-number variation
(CNV), reverse-phase protein arrays (RPPA), and DNA methylation
(Weinstein et al. 2013; The Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; The Cancer
Genome Atlas Research Network 2013; {Hoadley et al.} 2018). Across both
settings, clinicians and translational scientists require analytical
methods that connect structural or molecular vulnerability to clinical
phenotype without sacrificing anatomical or biological localization
(Avants et al. 2010, 2021; Avants, Libon, et al. 2014; Seeley et al.
2009; Rudin 2019).

A recurring tension in multi-modal data integration separates predictive
accuracy from mechanistic interpretability. For a neurologist evaluating
a patient with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), predicting an aggregate
cognitive decline score from a black-box neural network is insufficient
for clinical decision-making or clinical trial stratification (Rudin
2019; Doshi-Velez and Kim 2017; Lipton 2018). The clinician must verify
whether the predictive signal arises from established disease-vulnerable
substrates---such as the entorhinal-hippocampal memory circuit of early
Braak stages I--III (Braak and Braak 1991; {Jack et al.} 2018; Racine et
al. 2018) or the temporoparietal and posterior cingulate association
hubs of Braak stages IV--V ({Dickerson et al.} 2009; Buckner et al.
2005; Seeley et al. 2009; Cuingnet et al. 2011)---rather than from
scanner artifacts, ventricular enlargement, or global brain atrophy.
Similarly, a molecular tumor board cannot design a targeted assay from a
dense principal component combining \(20,000\) transcripts; actionable
translation requires isolating compact, non-redundant driver pathways
such as estrogen-receptor transcriptional networks (\texttt{ESR1},
\texttt{GATA3}, \texttt{PGR}) or focal chromosomal amplifications
(\texttt{17q23}, \texttt{8p11-p12}) ({Perou et al.} 2000; {Parker et
al.} 2009; The Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; {Curtis et al.} 2012;
Prat and Parker 2020; Cohen et al. 2014).

Existing multi-view factorizations force an uncomfortable compromise
between these clinical and computational goals:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Dense Linear Factorizations Disperse Anatomical Signal}:
  Principal Component Analysis (PCA) (Hotelling 1933), Canonical
  Correlation Analysis (CCA) (Hotelling 1936; Hardoon et al. 2004;
  Chaudhuri et al. 2009), Partial Least Squares (PLS) (Krishnan et al.
  2011; Tenenhaus and Tenenhaus 2011; Tenenhaus et al. 2014), Joint and
  Individual Variation Explained (JIVE) (Lock et al. 2013), and
  Multi-Omics Factor Analysis (MOFA) (Argelaguet et al. 2020; Cantini et
  al. 2021; Abdi et al. 2013) estimate dense loading matrices in which
  every brain region or gene receives a non-zero weight of mixed
  positive and negative sign. In neuroimaging, the dominant principal
  component captures global brain size and diffuse cross-cortical
  covariance, obscuring focal disease networks behind sign cancellations
  (Zou et al. 2006; Witten et al. 2009; Kandel et al. 2015).
\item
  \textbf{Rigid Orthogonality vs.~Rank Collapse in Sparse Matrix
  Decompositions}: Non-negative Matrix Factorization (NMF) (Lee and
  Seung 1999, 2001; Hoyer 2004; Ding et al. 2006; Cichocki et al. 2009)
  and Sparse PCA / Sparse CCA (Zou et al. 2006; Witten et al. 2009;
  Parkhomenko et al. 2009) promote parts-based or localized weights.
  However, without explicit cross-column orthogonality control, sparse
  non-negative projections frequently suffer from rank collapse---where
  multiple latent components converge onto the same high-variance
  hub---whereas enforcing strict Stiefel manifold orthogonality
  (\(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k\)) (Edelman et al.
  1998; Absil et al. 2008; Wen and Yin 2013; Boumal 2023) forbids
  overlapping biological pathways from sharing pleiotropic hub genes or
  transmodal cortical hubs (Buckner et al. 2009; Menon and Uddin 2010;
  Avants et al. 2021, 2025).
\item
  \textbf{Opaque Deep Multi-Modal Encoders}: Deep Canonical Correlation
  Analysis (DCCA) (Andrew et al. 2013; Wang et al. 2015), multi-modal
  Variational Autoencoders (Wu and Goodman 2018; Shi et al. 2019;
  Higgins et al. 2017; Hinton and Salakhutdinov 2006; Vincent et al.
  2008; Bengio et al. 2013), and contrastive multi-view transformers
  ({Radford et al.} 2021; Baltrušaitis et al. 2019) route raw
  measurements through multilayer non-linear encoders before aligning
  modalities. This composition destroys the direct linear mapping
  between input features and latent axes, forcing investigators to rely
  on post-hoc attribution approximations (\texttt{SHAP}, \texttt{LIME},
  \texttt{Integrated\ Gradients}) (Lundberg and Lee 2017; Ribeiro et al.
  2016; Sundararajan et al. 2017; Alvarez-Melis and Jaakkola 2018) that
  can be unstable across correlated anatomical or genomic features
  (Rudin 2019; Hooker et al. 2019).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The SiMR Framework: A Linear Entrypoint Paired with Flexible
Multi-View
Alignment}\label{the-simr-framework-a-linear-entrypoint-paired-with-flexible-multi-view-alignment}

To satisfy both statistical learning requirements and clinical
auditability, we formulate the Similarity-driven Multi-view
Representation (SiMR) framework around a strict architectural boundary:
the Linear Entrypoint Principle (Figure~\ref{fig-scientific-overview}).
Regardless of how complex the downstream cross-modal alignment or
generative modeling becomes, every modality \(m \in \{1, \dots, M\}\)
enters the model exclusively through an explicit, non-negative, sparse,
and approximately orthogonal linear projection matrix
\(\mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{p_m \times k}\):

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-linear-entrypoint}{
\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m
}\end{equation}

This architectural separation provides three foundational properties
that appeal simultaneously to machine learning researchers and clinical
domain specialists:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Exact Feature-to-Factor and Patient-Level Attribution}:
  Because each latent coordinate \(l \in \{1, \dots, k\}\) is an
  additive linear combination of non-negative weights
  (\(\mathbf{V}_m \ge 0\)), every column \(\mathbf{v}_{m,l}\) represents
  an immediately inspectable anatomical map or molecular module without
  sign-cancellation ambiguity (Lee and Seung 1999; Avants et al. 2021).
  At the individual patient level \(i\), the exact contribution of
  feature \(j\) to component \(l\) is the simple elementwise product
  \(c_{m,i,j,l} = x_{m,i,j} v_{m,j,l}\).
\item
  \textbf{Continuous Balance Between Pathway Overlap and Factor
  Redundancy (\texttt{nsa\_flow})}: Rather than choosing between
  unconstrained sparsity (which permits duplicate components) and exact
  Stiefel manifold projection (which prohibits shared biological hubs),
  we constrain \(\mathbf{V}_m\) via Non-Negative Stiefel Approximating
  Flow (\texttt{nsa\_flow}) (Edelman et al. 1998; Absil et al. 2008;
  Avants et al. 2021, 2025). A single trade-off weight \(w \in (0,1)\)
  continuously balances cross-modal alignment fidelity against
  scale-invariant column decorrelation and norm balance, while
  post-optimization quantile thresholding (\(q \in [0, 1)\)) allows
  investigators to dial continuously between pathway-wide discovery
  (\(q = 0.5\)) and ultra-compact diagnostic panels (\(q = 0.8\)).
\item
  \textbf{Interchangeable Downstream Alignment Topologies}: Holding the
  linear entrypoint
  \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) fixed, SiMR
  supports three modular alignment paradigms: (i) classical linear
  consensus optimization (\texttt{SiMLR}) (Avants et al. 2021), (ii)
  non-linear multilayer perceptron decoding (\texttt{LEND}, alongside
  shared-private variants \texttt{NED} and \texttt{NEDPP} (Bousmalis et
  al. 2016; Salzmann et al. 2010)) that accommodates curved manifold
  structure in the decoders while keeping the encoder strictly linear,
  and (iii) invertible RealNVP normalizing flows (\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V})
  (Dinh et al. 2017; Rezende and Mohamed 2015; Papamakarios et al. 2021)
  that align non-Gaussian biological distributions in an exact bijective
  latent space.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Subspace Preservation vs.~Coordinate Axis
Disentanglement}\label{subspace-preservation-vs.-coordinate-axis-disentanglement}

A central contribution of this work is resolving a widespread
methodological confusion in representation learning benchmarks: why
post-hoc rotation or mixing of latent variables often leaves
multi-variable linear regression unchanged while destroying clinical
interpretability and single-factor utility.

Suppose an algorithm learns a sparse, neuroanatomically localized
projection \(\mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\), and a
post-processing step multiplies \(\mathbf{V}_m\) on the right by any
invertible \(k \times k\) mixing matrix
\(\mathbf{M} \in \mathrm{GL}(k)\) (for example, an orthogonal Procrustes
rotation (Schönemann 1966; Davis and Kahan 1970) or independent
component unmixing matrix (Hyvärinen and Oja 2000)). For any downstream
model that depends only on the column space of the projected
features---such as ordinary least squares or lightly penalized ridge
regression (Hoerl and Kennard 1970; Hastie et al. 2009)---the column
space is mathematically invariant to \(\mathbf{M}\):

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-subspace-invariance}{
\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m \mathbf{M}) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m) \quad \forall \, \mathbf{M} \in \mathrm{GL}(k)
}\end{equation}

Consequently, evaluating learned representations solely with
multi-variable linear probes measures only overall subspace capacity,
masking whether individual columns \(\mathbf{v}_{m,l}\) correspond to
coherent biological systems or arbitrary diagonal mixtures. In contrast,
coordinate-wise evaluations---such as axis-aligned decision trees
(\texttt{RandomForest} (Breiman 2001)), single-biomarker clinical
thresholds, and anatomical loading maps---depend directly on the
orientation and sparsity of the individual coordinate axes. To separate
these properties rigorously, our benchmark evaluates all nine candidate
architectures across four complementary probes
(Table~\ref{tbl-evaluation-probes}).

\section{Unified Multi-View Objective and the Nine Benchmark
Architectures}\label{unified-multi-view-objective-and-the-nine-benchmark-architectures}

Let \(\{\mathbf{X}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times p_m}\}_{m=1}^M\) denote
\(M\) data modalities acquired on the same \(N\) subjects during
training. Every architecture within the SiMR family maps each view \(m\)
into a \(k\)-dimensional latent space exclusively through the linear
entrypoint \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\)
while optimizing a composite objective combining cross-modal alignment
\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\), within-view geometric or reconstruction
preservation \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{within}}\), and manifold
regularization \(\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{V}_m)\):

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-unified-simr}{
\min_{\{\mathbf{V}_m\}_{m=1}^M, \, \Theta} \; \sum_{m=1}^M \mathcal{L}_{\text{within}}(\mathbf{X}_m, \mathbf{V}_m; \Theta) + \lambda_{\text{align}} \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\!\left(\{\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\}_{m=1}^M; \Theta\right) + \sum_{m=1}^M \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{V}_m)
}\end{equation}

Here \(\Theta\) denotes architecture-specific alignment parameters: the
shared orthonormal subject basis
\(\mathbf{U} \in \operatorname{St}(N, k)\) in \texttt{SiMLR} (Avants et
al. 2021), the non-linear decoder weights \(\{\phi_m\}\) in
\texttt{LEND}, or the invertible coupling network parameters
\(\{\psi_m\}\) in \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} (Dinh et al. 2017). To evaluate
which mathematical components drive predictive performance and
biological localization, we compare nine architectures spanning

\section{\texorpdfstring{Non-Negative Stiefel Approximating Flow
(\texttt{nsa\_flow}) and Operating
Points}{Non-Negative Stiefel Approximating Flow (nsa\_flow) and Operating Points}}\label{non-negative-stiefel-approximating-flow-nsa_flow-and-operating-points}

In high-dimensional neuroimaging (\(p_m \sim 10^2\)--\(10^5\)) and
transcriptomics (\(p_m \sim 10^4\)), updating \(\mathbf{V}_m\) along a
cross-modal covariance gradient
\(\mathbf{V}_{m,0} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\) quickly causes
multiple latent components to collapse onto the dominant variance axis.
Conversely, projecting \(\mathbf{V}_m\) onto the strict Stiefel manifold
\(\operatorname{St}(p_m, k)\) (Edelman et al. 1998) forces exact zero
inner products between columns, preventing distinct biological networks
from sharing pleiotropic hub genes or transmodal association cortices
(Buckner et al. 2009; Avants et al. 2021, 2025).

To provide a continuous, scale-invariant bridge between these extremes,
each update of \(\mathbf{V}_m\) minimizes the dimensionless Non-Negative
Stiefel Approximating Flow (\texttt{nsa\_flow}) energy
\(E_w(\mathbf{V}_m; \mathbf{V}_{m,0})\) over the non-negative orthant
\(\mathbf{V}_m \ge 0\) (Avants et al. 2025; Birgin et al. 2000; Barzilai
and Borwein 1988; Armijo 1966; Parikh and Boyd 2014; Bolte et al. 2014;
Ablin and Peyré 2022) (Figure~\ref{fig-simr-architecture-flow}):

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-nsa-flow-energy}{
E_w(\mathbf{V}_m; \mathbf{V}_{m,0}) = (1 - w) \, \mathcal{L}_{\text{fid}}(\mathbf{V}_m; \mathbf{V}_{m,0}) + w \, D(\mathbf{V}_m), \quad w \in (0, 1)
}\end{equation}

where the scale-normalized fidelity loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{fid}}\)
and the trace-normalized Gram defect \(D(\mathbf{V}_m)\) are both
dimensionless and \(\mathcal{O}(1)\) by construction:

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-gram-defect}{
\mathcal{L}_{\text{fid}}(\mathbf{V}_m; \mathbf{V}_{m,0}) = \frac{\|\mathbf{V}_m - \mathbf{V}_{m,0}\|_F^2}{2 \|\mathbf{V}_{m,0}\|_F^2}, \qquad D(\mathbf{V}_m) = \left\| \mathbf{G}(\mathbf{V}_m) - \frac{1}{k}\mathbf{I}_k \right\|_F^2, \qquad \mathbf{G}(\mathbf{V}_m) = \frac{\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m}{\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m)}
}\end{equation}

The Gram defect \(D(\mathbf{V}_m)\) decomposes orthogonally into
off-diagonal column correlation plus diagonal column norm imbalance:

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-defect-decomposition}{
D(\mathbf{V}_m) = \sum_{l \neq r} G_{lr}^2 + \sum_{l=1}^k \left(G_{ll} - \frac{1}{k}\right)^2
}\end{equation}

Minimizing \(D(\mathbf{V}_m)\) therefore prevents both duplicate factors
(\(G_{lr} \to 0\) for \(l \neq r\)) and dead components
(\(G_{ll} \to 1/k\)), while the Euclidean gradient
\(\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} D(\mathbf{V}_m)\) is identically orthogonal to
\(\mathbf{V}_m\)
(\(\langle \nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} D(\mathbf{V}_m), \mathbf{V}_m \rangle_F = 0\)),
ensuring that orthogonality regularization never artificially inflates
or shrinks matrix scale. Moreover, when features within a modality share
a positive correlation floor (such as cortical thickness parcels across
the brain), the leading singular vector of \(\mathbf{V}_{m,0}\) acts as
a dense global mean while components \(2\dots k\) are signed contrasts;
directly projecting onto \(\mathbf{V}_m \ge 0\) would leave Component 1
dense. To prevent this without altering the spanned subspace
\(\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{V}_{m,0})\), \texttt{nsa\_flow} applies an
exact orthogonal Varimax rotation
\(\mathbf{R}^* = \arg\max_{\mathbf{R} \in \mathrm{O}(k)} \mathcal{V}_{\text{varimax}}(\mathbf{V}_{m,0}\mathbf{R})\)
(\texttt{varimax=True} by default in \texttt{pysimlr\ v0.2.17}) prior to
non-negative retraction, distributing variance evenly across all \(k\)
columns. Following gradient polish, column-wise quantile thresholding at
level \(q \in [0, 1)\) zeros out entries below the \(q\)-th percentile
of each column's positive weights and normalizes columns to unit
\(\ell_2\) norm. This yields two primary clinical operating points:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Discovery Operating Point (\(q = 0.5\))}: Retains the top
  \(50\%\) of active weights per component, capturing complete
  multi-gene pathways or distributed functional-anatomical brain
  systems.
\item
  \textbf{Focal Biomarker Panel Operating Point
  (\(q = 0.8\)--\(0.85\))}: Retains only the top \(15\%\)--\(20\%\) of
  weights per component, isolating compact regional MRI signatures or
  minimal gene panels suitable for targeted clinical assays.
\end{itemize}

\section{Linear, Non-Linear Decoder, and Normalizing-Flow Alignment
Topologies}\label{linear-non-linear-decoder-and-normalizing-flow-alignment-topologies}

Holding the linear entrypoint
\(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) fixed, SiMR
provides three alignment topologies tailored to different cross-modal
relationships:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Linear Consensus Alignment (\texttt{SiMLR})}: Alternates
  between updating \(\mathbf{V}_m\) via cross-modal covariance
  projection
  \(\mathbf{V}_{m,0} = \mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{S}_m \mathbf{U}\)
  followed by \texttt{nsa\_flow} polish (\texttt{L-BFGS-B} (Byrd et al.
  1995; Nocedal and Wright 2006) or Lookahead (Zhang et al. 2019)), and
  updating the shared subject basis
  \(\mathbf{U} = \operatorname{polar}\!\left(\sum_{m=1}^M \tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_m\right)\)
  via exact Sylvester-differentiable polar decomposition (Higham 2008;
  Avants et al. 2021).
\item
  \textbf{Linear Entrypoint with Non-Linear Decoders (\texttt{LEND})}:
  Replaces the linear reconstruction model with modality-specific
  multilayer perceptron decoders
  \(g_{\phi_m} : \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\) trained to
  reconstruct \(\mathbf{X}_m\) from \(\mathbf{U}\) while penalizing
  encoder-consensus divergence
  \(\gamma \|\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m - \mathbf{U}\|_F^2\). Because
  non-linearity is restricted to the generative decoders \(g_{\phi_m}\),
  out-of-sample inference
  \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_{m,\text{test}} \mathbf{V}_m\)
  remains strictly linear and auditable.
\item
  \textbf{Detached-Prior Normalizing Flows (\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V})}:
  Routes each linear projection
  \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) through a
  volume-preserving RealNVP flow
  \(f_{\psi_m} : \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k\) (Dinh et al. 2017;
  Papamakarios et al. 2021) toward an isotropic Gaussian prior. To
  prevent the single-modality density term from pulling \(\mathbf{V}_m\)
  toward high-variance noise directions, gradients from the Gaussian
  prior are detached before reaching \(\mathbf{V}_m\) (proved in
  Appendix C), ensuring that \(\mathbf{V}_m\) updates exclusively along
  cross-modal flow agreement:
\end{enumerate}

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-flow-simr-v}{
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Flow-SiMR-V}} = \sum_{m=1}^M \frac{1}{2N} \left\| f_{\psi_m}\!\left(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m)\right) \right\|_F^2 + \lambda_{\text{align}} \sum_{m < m'} \frac{1}{N} \left\| f_{\psi_m}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m) - f_{\psi_{m'}}(\mathbf{X}_{m'} \mathbf{V}_{m'}) \right\|_F^2
}\end{equation}

\section{Clinical Deployment Protocols: Multi-Modal Fusion
vs.~Single-View Bipartite
Translation}\label{clinical-deployment-protocols-multi-modal-fusion-vs.-single-view-bipartite-translation}

A critical distinction in clinical translation is which data views are
available when evaluating a new patient (Stone 1974):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Protocol A (Multi-Modal Biomarker Discovery)}: All \(M\)
  predictor modalities (for example, mRNA, CNV, and proteomics in TCGA)
  are measured on both training and test patients, and models predict an
  external clinical outcome \(\mathbf{y}\) (such as survival or
  histological subtype) from either modality-specific projections
  \(\mathbf{X}_{m,\text{test}}\mathbf{V}_m\) or the fused consensus
  embedding \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{test}}\).
\item
  \textbf{Protocol B (Single-View Clinical Translation in
  \texttt{ADNI-MCI})}: During training, the algorithm aligns structural
  MRI (\(\mathbf{X}_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 62}\), DKT cortical
  thickness and subcortical volumes (Desikan et al. 2006; Klein and
  Tourville 2012; Tustison et al. 2014, 2010; Avants, Tustison, Wu, et
  al. 2011; Avants, Tustison, et al. 2014)) with an 11-scale
  neuropsychological battery
  (\(\mathbf{X}_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 11}\)). At test time,
  cognitive test scores \(\mathbf{X}_{2,\text{test}}\) are completely
  withheld; predictions of composite cognitive status (\texttt{CogPC1})
  and individual clinical scales are generated solely from the patient's
  baseline structural MRI scan via
  \(\mathbf{Z}_{1,\text{test}} = \mathbf{X}_{1,\text{test}} \mathbf{V}_1\).
  This protocol tests whether supervising anatomical representation
  learning with multidimensional cognitive batteries during training
  extracts a more clinically informative neuroanatomical basis than
  unsupervised MRI decomposition.
\end{itemize}

Three empirical principles emerge from
Table~\ref{tbl-global-leaderboard} and Figures
\ref{fig-cd-diagram}--\ref{fig-three-probes}:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Multi-View Alignment Outperforms Single-Matrix Factorization
  When Views Differ in Scale or Private Variance}: \texttt{LEND} (mean
  Friedman rank \(3.79\)), \texttt{NED} (\(3.88\)), \texttt{NEDPP}
  (\(3.92\)), and \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} (\(4.25\)) lead the linear
  entrypoint ranking ahead of concatenated \texttt{PCA} (\(7.75\)) and
  \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} (\(7.67\)). On heterogeneous cohorts such as
  \texttt{mfeat} (\(p = [76, 64, 6, 47]\)) and \texttt{Shared+Private}
  (\(p = [160, 60, 30]\)), concatenated \texttt{PCA} rotates toward the
  highest-dimensional view (\texttt{mfeat} linear accuracy \(0.727\),
  \texttt{Shared+Private} \(R^2 = 0.618\)), whereas cross-view alignment
  equalizes view contributions (\texttt{mfeat} accuracy \(0.964\) for
  \texttt{NEDPP} and \(0.948\) for \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB};
  \texttt{Shared+Private} \(R^2 = 0.956\) for \texttt{LEND}).
\item
  \textbf{Coordinate-Aligned \texttt{nsa\_flow} Projections Prevent the
  Random Forest Axis Drop}: As established in
  Equation~\ref{eq-subspace-invariance}, ridge regression on
  \(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) is insensitive to orthogonal or oblique
  column mixing \(\mathbf{V}_m \mathbf{M}\), hiding the fact that dense
  \texttt{PCA} mixes distinct biological mechanisms across columns. When
  evaluated with coordinate-splitting decision trees
  (\texttt{RandomForest}), \texttt{PCA} suffers an average score drop of
  \(\Delta = -0.044\) across the 12 cohorts---collapsing completely on
  weak sparse factors (\texttt{NonnegPartsWeak} linear
  \(R^2 = 0.125 \to\) Random Forest \(R^2 = 0.000\)) and dropping by
  \(71\%\) on \texttt{ADNI-MCI} (\(0.152 \to 0.044\)). In contrast,
  \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} (\(\Delta = -0.022\)) and \texttt{LEND}
  (\(\Delta = -0.023\)) cut the axis-mixing penalty in half.
\item
  \textbf{Operating Points Match Intrinsic Biological Density
  (\(q = 0.5\) vs.~\(q = 0.8\))}:
  Figure~\ref{fig-operating-point-and-support} demonstrates how the
  quantile threshold \(q\) acts as a tunable lens on feature support
  recovery. Across the \(1,215\)-fit operating-point evaluation,
  moderate sparsity (\(q = 0.5\)) achieves the highest overall linear
  predictive score (\(0.680\)) and highest Random Forest axis score
  (\(0.651\)) while recovering \(F_1 = 0.989\) (\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} on
  \texttt{NonnegParts}) and \(F_1 = 0.709\)--\(1.000\) on
  \(50\%\)-sparse ground-truth pathways (\texttt{Linear}). When the
  underlying biology is concentrated in focal \(20\%\)-active modules
  (\texttt{NonnegPartsWeak} and \texttt{Shared+Private}), increasing the
  quantile threshold to \(q = 0.8\) raises support recovery to
  \(F_1 = 0.797\) while preserving \(99.1\%\) of peak subspace accuracy
  (\(0.674\) vs.~\(0.680\)).
\end{enumerate}

    heteromodal temporoparietal and inferior frontal hubs marks the
    clinical transition from isolated amnestic impairment to language,
    semantic, and praxis decline (Braak and Braak 1991; {Dickerson et
    al.} 2009; Buckner et al. 2005).
  \item
    \textbf{Component 2 (\(\mathbf{v}_2\), Medial Temporal Amnestic Core
    --- Braak Stages I--III)}: Isolates bilateral entorhinal cortex
    (\texttt{left\_entorhinal} \(+0.680\), \texttt{right\_entorhinal}
    \(+0.226\)) and precuneal/cuneal posterior hubs
    (\texttt{right\_cuneus} \(+0.698\)), coupled with subcortical
    hippocampal volume loss in View 0 (\texttt{right\_hippocampus}
    \(+0.369\)). This circuit directly reflects early transentorhinal
    tau neurofibrillary pathology responsible for episodic memory
    encoding failure on \texttt{RAVLT} and \texttt{ADASQ4} (Braak and
    Braak 1991; {Jack et al.} 2018; Rey 1964).
  \item
    \textbf{Component 3 (\(\mathbf{v}_3\), Frontocingulate Salience
    Network)}: Targets \texttt{left\_rostral\_middle\_frontal}
    (\(+0.631\)), \texttt{right\_caudal\_anterior\_cingulate}
    (\(+0.478\)), and bilateral insular cortex (\texttt{left\_insula}
    \(+0.434\), \texttt{right\_insula} \(+0.429\)). Atrophy in this
    anterior cingulate--insular salience network (Seeley et al. 2009;
    Menon and Uddin 2010) governs executive set-shifting deficits
    (\texttt{TRABSCOR} (Reitan 1958)) and everyday functional decline
    (\texttt{FAQ} (Pfeffer et al. 1982)).
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Spared Primary Sensorimotor Cortex and Support-Shuffled /
  PCA-Rotated Score Controls Confirm True Anatomical Localization}:
  Across all three components, primary motor cortex
  (\texttt{right\_precentral} \(+0.0016\), \texttt{left\_precentral}
  \(\le 10^{-5}\)) and primary somatosensory cortex
  (\texttt{left\_postcentral} \(\le 10^{-5}\),
  \texttt{right\_postcentral} \(\le 10^{-5}\)) receive effectively zero
  weight (Figure~\ref{fig-adni-neuroanatomy-flagship} B). Because
  primary sensorimotor neocortex is pathologically spared until
  end-stage Alzheimer's disease (Braak and Braak 1991; {Dickerson et
  al.} 2009), its automatic exclusion provides an internal
  neurobiological control. To further separate coordinate-level axis
  alignment and anatomical support localization from pure subspace
  capacity, we evaluate two algorithmic negative controls across all 3
  seeds on \texttt{ADNI-MCI}
  (\texttt{adni\_mci\_axis\_alignment\_controls.csv} via
  \texttt{axis\_alignment\_control\_metrics}): (i) a \textbf{PCA-Rotated
  Score Control} that rotates the learned scores
  \(\mathbf{Z} = [\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m]\) by their in-fold
  principal-component eigenvectors \(\mathbf{Q} \in \mathrm{O}(Mk)\)
  (\(\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{Z}\mathbf{Q}) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{Z})\)),
  which leaves the linear probe identically unchanged to machine
  precision (\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} \(0.2403 \to 0.2403\); \texttt{LEND}
  \(0.2418 \to 0.2418\); \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} \(0.1650 \to 0.1650\))
  while reducing single-axis Random Forest accuracy when localized axes
  are mixed (\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} \(0.1123 \to 0.0879\), \texttt{LEND}
  non-negative \(0.1617 \to 0.1416\)); and (ii) a
  \textbf{Support-Shuffled Basis Control} that permutes the row indices
  of each \(\mathbf{V}_m\) within view \(m\)
  (\(\mathbf{V}_m[\pi_m, :]\), preserving exact sparsity, weight
  magnitudes, and Stiefel orthogonality
  \(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m\)), which collapses the Random Forest
  \texttt{CogPC1} \(R^2\) by \(60\%\)--\(65\%\) (\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V}
  \(0.1798 \to 0.0627\), \(\Delta = -0.1171\); \texttt{LEND}
  \(0.1725 \to 0.0690\), \(\Delta = -0.1035\); \texttt{NEDPP}
  \(0.1563 \to 0.0668\), \(\Delta = -0.0895\)) and linear \(R^2\) from
  \(0.240\)--\(0.242\) to \(0.151\)--\(0.157\) (the unstructured
  \texttt{PCA} baseline floor of \(0.151\)). This confirms that the
  \(+3.8\times\) to \(+4.5\times\) Random Forest advantage on
  \texttt{ADNI-MCI} arises directly from localizing true neuroanatomical
  circuits rather than generic sparsity or basis conditioning.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Synthesizing Clinical Neurobiology and Statistical
Representation
Learning}\label{synthesizing-clinical-neurobiology-and-statistical-representation-learning}

The empirical results across \texttt{ADNI-MCI}, \texttt{TCGA-BRCA},
\texttt{TCGA-KIRC}, and the synthetic ground-truth benchmarks
demonstrate that predictive accuracy and mechanistic localization do not
need to be traded against one another when multi-view integration is
organized around an explicit linear entrypoint
(\(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\)). For the
clinical neuroscientist or neuropsychologist, the primary value of
\texttt{SiMR} lies in how cross-modal supervision shapes the learned
anatomical basis. When structural MRI is decomposed in isolation via
\texttt{PCA}, the dominant axis reflects diffuse global cortical
thickness and intracranial scaling across all \(62\) DKT parcels
(\(0\%\) sparsity), explaining only \(R^2 = 0.152\) of composite
cognitive severity (\texttt{CogPC1}) and collapsing to \(R^2 = 0.044\)
under single-axis decision tree splits. By coupling structural MRI views
to an 11-scale cognitive battery during training (\texttt{Protocol\ B})
and applying non-negative approximate Stiefel regularization
(\texttt{nsa\_flow}, \(w = 0.50, q = 0.85\)), the learned projection
matrices isolate three non-redundant, \(88\%\)-sparse anatomical systems
that mirror classical Braak neurofibrillary staging (Braak and Braak
1991; {Dickerson et al.} 2009; {Jack et al.} 2018): the lateral
temporoparietal association circuit (Braak IV--V), the
entorhinal--hippocampal amnestic core (Braak I--III), and the
fronto-insular-cingulate salience network (Seeley et al. 2009; Menon and
Uddin 2010), while automatically zeroing out pathologically spared
primary motor and somatosensory cortex.

For the machine learning researcher, our four-probe evaluation resolves
why post-hoc rotation or unmixing steps (\texttt{ICA} (Hyvärinen and Oja
2000), orthogonal Procrustes rotations (Schönemann 1966), or dense SVD
factors) often appear competitive under ridge regression yet fail when
individual coordinates are inspected or thresholded. Because
\(\operatorname{span}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m \mathbf{M}) = \operatorname{span}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m)\)
for any invertible \(\mathbf{M} \in \mathrm{GL}(k)\), linear subspace
probes measure only aggregate subspace capacity. Pairing linear subspace
probes with coordinate-aligned Random Forest probes (\texttt{RF(XV)})
and ground-truth support recovery (\(F_1\)) exposes diagonal coordinate
mixing directly: across all twelve benchmarks,
\texttt{nsa\_flow}-regularized architectures cut the axis-sensitivity
drop in half (\(\Delta = -0.022\) vs.~\(-0.044\) for \texttt{PCA}) while
recovering up to \(84.7\%\) of true pathway supports.

\section{Limitations and Negative
Findings}\label{limitations-and-negative-findings}

Rigorous experimental reporting requires highlighting the regimes where
\texttt{SiMR} architectures underperform, incur computational
trade-offs, or require careful preprocessing:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{No Universal Advantage Over PCA on Unaligned Single-View
  Unsupervised Tasks}: When a single homogeneous data matrix is analyzed
  without cross-modal supervision or when views share identical
  signal-to-noise ratios and dense Gaussian loadings, standard
  \texttt{PCA} remains optimal for variance explanation in
  \(\mathcal{O}(\min(N p^2, p N^2))\) time (\(0.004\text{ s}\)).
  Similarly, on low-dimensional clinical tables such as \texttt{Heart}
  (\(p = [7, 6]\)), \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} (\(0.848\)) slightly exceeds
  multi-view \texttt{SiMLR} (\(0.831\)) because cross-view graph
  regularization adds variance when \(p_m\) is very small.
\item
  \textbf{Global Cortical Thickness Confounding Under Unrotated
  Initialization (\texttt{varimax\ =\ False,\ q\ =\ None})}: On
  \texttt{ADNI-MCI}, running unrotated non-negative \texttt{SiMLR}
  (\texttt{varimax\ =\ False,\ sparseness\ =\ None}) leaves Component 1
  \(85.5\%\) dense (\(53/62\) active DKT regions, \(15\%\) overall
  sparsity) because cortical thickness parcels share a positive global
  correlation floor across the brain
  (Figure~\ref{fig-adni-neuroanatomy-flagship} A2). In
  \texttt{pysimlr\ v0.2.17}, default orthogonal Varimax subspace
  rotation (\texttt{varimax\ =\ True}) resolves this directly in
  standard column-centered space by rotating the \(k\)-dimensional
  subspace before non-negative projection, raising unthresholded
  sparsity from \(15\%\) to \(66\%\) (\([24, 12, 26]\) active parcels,
  Figure~\ref{fig-adni-neuroanatomy-flagship} A3) without discarding
  global atrophy severity (unlike manual subject row-mean centering,
  which yields \(61\%\)--\(68\%\) sparsity but reduces \texttt{CogPC1}
  \(R^2\) from \(0.189\) to \(0.153\), as shown in Appendix B).
  Combining \texttt{varimax\ =\ True} with quantile thresholding
  \(q \in [0.50, 0.85]\) further isolates focal 9-region Braak networks
  (\(88\%\) sparse, \texttt{CogPC1} \(R^2 = 0.192\),
  Figure~\ref{fig-adni-neuroanatomy-flagship} A4).
\item
  \textbf{Stiffness and Accuracy Degradation at Extreme Orthogonality
  Weights (\(w \ge 0.80\))}: Pushing the \texttt{nsa\_flow} trade-off
  weight toward strict Stiefel projection (\(w = 0.95\)) increases
  wall-clock optimization time by \(2.5\times\)--\(3.8\times\) due to
  boundary stiffness on the non-negative orthant and reduces
  \texttt{ADNI-MCI} \texttt{CogPC1} \(R^2\) from \(0.159\)
  (\(w = 0.50\)) to \(0.132\) (\(w = 0.95\)). Conversely, setting
  \(w \le 0.10\) triggers severe rank collapse
  (\(\text{EffRank} = 2.02\) of \(k = 3\), Gram defect \(D = 0.244\)).
  The balanced default \(w = 0.50\) avoids both failure modes across all
  tested cohorts.
\item
  \textbf{Hyperparameter Sensitivity of Deep Multi-View Models on Small
  Sample Sizes}: When sample sizes are moderate (\(N \le 400\) in
  \texttt{NonnegParts} and \texttt{Heart}), deep autoencoder variants
  (\texttt{NED}, \texttt{NEDPP}) exhibit higher fold-to-fold variance
  and lower feature support recovery (\(F_1 = 0.587\)--\(0.642\)) than
  quasi-Newton linear solvers (\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB},
  \(F_1 = 0.861\)--\(0.991\)).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

By enforcing an explicit, sparse, non-negative, and approximately
orthogonal linear projection at the input boundary of every modality,
the \texttt{SiMR} framework bridges classical multivariate biostatistics
and modern multi-modal representation learning. Clinicians and domain
scientists gain immediately auditable neuroanatomical and molecular
networks with exact patient-level risk attributions, while machine
learning practitioners gain a flexible family of linear, non-linear
decoder, and normalizing-flow alignment modules that preserve both
subspace capacity and single-axis disentanglement.

\chapter{Normalizing Flow SiMR with Linear Encoder Bottleneck
(Flow-SiMR-V)}\label{normalizing-flow-simr-with-linear-encoder-bottleneck-flow-simr-v}

\section{Model Formulation}\label{model-formulation}

\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} prepends a linear entrypoint projection matrix
\(\mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\) to each view's
volume-preserving Normalizing Flow (Dinh et al. 2017; Rezende and
Mohamed 2015; Kingma and Dhariwal 2018; Papamakarios et al. 2021;
Kobyzev et al. 2020). This architecture provides a linear bottleneck at
the input boundary while retaining bijective density mapping,
conditional cross-view inference, and latent trajectory interpolation on
the \(k\)-dimensional subspace. The normalizing flows are implemented in
PyTorch ({Paszke et al.} 2019), adapting coupling structures from the
Latent-Aligned Multiview Normalizing Flows (\texttt{LAMNrFlows})
framework
(\href{https://github.com/ntustison/LAMNrFlows}{ntustison/LAMNrFlows}).

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}\label{mathematical-formulation}

For each input modality \(\mathbf{X}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p_m}\)
(\(m = 1, \dots, M\)):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Linear entrypoint bottleneck:} \[
  \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \qquad \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k},
  \] where \(\mathbf{V}_m\) is regularized via \texttt{nsa\_flow}
  (\(w \in (0, 1)\), default \(w = 0.5\)) or polar retraction (Higham
  2008; Absil et al. 2008).
\item
  \textbf{Low-dimensional bijective normalizing flow:} \[
  \mathbf{Z}_m = f_{\text{flow}, m}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}),
  \] where \(f_{\text{flow}, m}: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k\) is a
  composition of RealNVP affine coupling layers (Dinh et al. 2017) with
  exact inverse \(f_{\text{flow}, m}^{-1}\) and tractable
  log-determinant
  \(\log |\det J_{f_{\text{flow}, m}}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}})|\).
\item
  \textbf{Consensus mixing and inverse reconstruction:} \[
  \mathbf{U}_m = \operatorname{Consensus}\big(\{\mathbf{Z}_j\}_{j \in \mathcal{N}(m)}\big), \qquad \hat{\mathbf{X}}_m = f_{\text{flow}, m}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_m) \mathbf{V}_m^\top.
  \]
\end{enumerate}

\begin{center}\rule{0.5\linewidth}{0.5pt}\end{center}

\section{Rectangular OLEE Prior Collapse and Detached-Prior
Optimization}\label{rectangular-olee-prior-collapse-and-detached-prior-optimization}

A structural distinction separates square ambient-space normalizing
flows (\texttt{Flow-SiMR}, where
\(f_{\text{flow},m}: \mathbb{R}^{p_m} \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\)) from
rectangular OLEE-bottlenecked flows (\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V}, where
\(\mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\) with \(k \ll p_m\)
precedes \(f_{\text{flow},m}: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k\)).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Why End-to-End Gaussian Prior Gradients
Collapse
\(\mathbf{V}_m\)}{Why End-to-End Gaussian Prior Gradients Collapse \textbackslash mathbf\{V\}\_m}}\label{why-end-to-end-gaussian-prior-gradients-collapse-mathbfv_m}

In standard normalizing flows, maximum likelihood training minimizes the
negative log-likelihood under a standard isotropic Gaussian prior
\(p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_k)\): \[
\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}; f_{\text{flow},m}) = \frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n \|f_{\text{flow},m}(\mathbf{z}_{m,\text{lin},i})\|_2^2 - \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \log \left|\det \frac{\partial f_{\text{flow},m}}{\partial \mathbf{z}_{m,\text{lin}}}(\mathbf{z}_{m,\text{lin},i})\right|.
\] When the rectangular projection
\(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\)
(\(k \ll p_m\)) precedes the flow, the change-of-variables determinant
\(\log |\det J_{f_{\text{flow},m}}|\) accounts only for volume changes
inside the \(k\)-dimensional subspace and contains no log-determinant
term for the rectangular projection \(\mathbf{V}_m\). If
\(\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}\) is backpropagated
into \(\mathbf{V}_m\), the quadratic penalty
\(\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{Z}_m\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m) + \mathcal{O}(\|f_{\text{flow},m} - \mathrm{id}\|)\)
is minimized by rotating \(\mathbf{V}_m\) onto the trailing eigenvectors
of \(\mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m\) with the smallest eigenvalues
\(\lambda_{p_m-k+1}, \dots, \lambda_{p_m}\).

\subsection{Detached-Prior Subspace-Decoupled
Optimization}\label{detached-prior-subspace-decoupled-optimization}

To prevent rectangular prior collapse, \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} detaches
\(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\) at the input to the Gaussian prior
penalty:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{OLEE basis update (\(\mathbf{V}_m\)):} Updated exclusively by
  the cross-view alignment energy \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\) and
  within-view relative reconstruction loss
  \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}\), while detaching
  \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\) in the prior term
  (\(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}) = \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}.\text{detach}()\)).
\item
  \textbf{Bijective flow update (\(f_{\text{flow},m}\)):} Trained via
  \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(f_{\text{flow},m}(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}))) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}\),
  initialized with zero scale and translation weights
  (\(s_\ell = 0, t_\ell = 0\)) at epoch \(0\) so
  \(f_{\text{flow},m}^{(0)}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}) = \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\).
\end{enumerate}

In the \texttt{ADNI-MCI} 1,214-configuration sweep (\(N = 1,084\),
\(k = 3\), \texttt{Protocol\ B}), \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} with
detached-prior optimization reaches an out-of-sample \texttt{CogPC1}
\(R^2\) of \(0.2341\) (\texttt{Mean11\_RidgeCV} \(= 0.1054\), and
\(0.1930 \pm 0.0048\) across the 3-seed benchmark), ranking third
overall across all 12 benchmark datasets.

\subsection{Block-Level Sign-Flip Permutation
Floor}\label{block-level-sign-flip-permutation-floor}

In paired comparisons across \(B = 12\) independent dataset blocks
(where each block cell is the mean across \(N_{\text{seeds}} = 3\)
replicates (Demšar 2006)), the exact distribution-free sign-flip null
distribution across the \(B = 12\) blocks consists of \(2^B = 4,096\)
sign assignments:

\[
p_{\text{perm}} = \frac{1}{2^B} \sum_{\mathbf{s} \in \{-1, +1\}^B} \mathbb{I}\left(\left|\frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B s_i \Delta_i\right| \ge |\bar{\Delta}|\right), \qquad p_{\min, B=12} = \frac{2}{2^{12}} = \frac{2}{4096} \approx 0.000488
\]
