\chapter*{Abstract}\label{abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

\markboth{Abstract}{Abstract}

Multi-modal biomedical datasets---such as co-registered neuroimaging,
high-throughput omics, and clinical assessments---provide complementary
views of shared pathological processes. Integrating these heterogeneous
matrices requires dimensionality reduction that isolates cross-view
covariance while maintaining interpretable feature attribution. Standard
deep multi-view networks and variational autoencoders capture non-linear
interactions, but their nested compositions obscure the physical
relationship between raw biological measurements and latent coordinates,
imposing a severe ``transparency penalty'' that precludes regulatory
adoption in high-stakes clinical decision-making. Conversely, classical
linear methods like Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) and Principal
Component Analysis (PCA) provide auditable projections, but fail to
capture non-linear manifold geometry and produce dense, mixed-sign
loadings that lack anatomical specificity.

To eliminate this transparency penalty without sacrificing
representation capacity, we formulate Deep SiMLR (Similarity-driven
Multi-view Reconstruction) governed by the Only Linear Encoder
Entrypoint (OLEE) contract. OLEE mandates that the initial entrypoint
for every modality is restricted to a linear projection on the compact
Stiefel manifold (\(\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\), subject
to \(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k\)). This structural
contract guarantees globally constant attribution derivatives
(\(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{V}_m\)) and enables
closed-form counterfactual auditability
(\(\Delta \mathbf{Z}_m = \Delta \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) and
\(\delta \mathbf{x}_m^* = \Delta \mathbf{z}_m \mathbf{V}_m^\top\)),
connecting mathematical dimensionality reduction directly to causal
recourse principles. Downstream transformations---including
volume-preserving normalizing flows (\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V}),
bound-constrained quasi-Newton Riemannian solvers
(\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB}, \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS}), and non-linear decoders
(\texttt{LEND})---model non-linear geometry while preserving exact
first-order input auditability. Spectral Modality Alignment Index (MAI)
dynamic weighting down-weights uninformative modalities, preventing
isotropic noise from corrupting consensus discovery.

We benchmark nine architectures across 243 task evaluations spanning
nine multi-modal data regimes (six synthetic manifolds with known ground
truth and three public clinical/multi-omics cohorts: UCI Cleveland Heart
Disease, Efron Diabetes Progression, and 3-assay TCGA-BRCA multi-omics)
across three independent seeds (\texttt{private\_scale\ =\ 0.5},
\texttt{consolidate\ =\ False}, default \texttt{"pca"} initialization),
alongside a dedicated 4-view study of Mild Cognitive Impairment in the
Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI-MCI, \(N=1,084\)).
Following Demšar's block-level ranking protocol across the nine
datasets, non-parametric inference confirms significant architectural
separation (Friedman \(\chi_F^2 = 32.47, p = 7.68 \times 10^{-5}\);
Iman--Davenport \(F_F = 6.57, p = 3.13 \times 10^{-6}\); Nemenyi
Critical Difference \(CD = 4.004\) at \(\alpha = 0.05\)). Across global
consensus predictions, bound-constrained quasi-Newton
\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} achieves the top overall rank (mean rank \(3.28\),
predictive score \(0.795 \pm 0.201\), support recovery \(0.942\)),
closely followed by hybrid \texttt{LEND} (mean rank \(3.56\), highest
mean predictive score \(0.830 \pm 0.189\), highest linear probe
\(0.836\), highest axis-sensitive probe \(0.830\), and highest
ground-truth latent recovery \(CMC = 0.907\)), single-matrix
\texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} (mean rank \(3.61\), \(0.782 \pm 0.200\) in
\(0.029\text{s}\), leading Diabetes \(R^2 = 0.382\), Heart accuracy
\(83.7\%\), and TCGA-BRCA accuracy \(92.7\%\)), and \texttt{PCA} (mean
rank \(3.78\), \(0.774 \pm 0.213\) in \(0.024\text{s}\), \(0.0\%\)
sparsity, chance support recovery \(0.333\)). On regimes with asymmetric
view-private variance (\texttt{Shared+Private}) or weak sparse parts
(\texttt{NonnegPartsWeak}), cross-view OLEE consensus (\texttt{LEND}
\(R^2 = 0.949\) and \(0.732\); \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} \(R^2 = 0.715\))
substantially outperforms single-matrix concatenation (\texttt{PCA}
\(R^2 = 0.639\) and \(0.522\)). In ADNI-MCI (\(N=1,084\)), we show in
plain clinical terms why predicting daily functional disability
(Clinical Dementia Rating Sum of Boxes, CDR-SB) directly from 382
structural brain MRI features explains only \(4.1\%\) of test-set
variance (\(R^2 = 0.0411\)): brain tissue loss does not directly cause
difficulties with daily chores, but instead first impairs cognitive
abilities (memory and executive function), which in turn drive daily
disability. Modeling this two-step pathway by aligning three brain MRI
views (\(382\) features) with a 13-item cognitive test view increases
out-of-sample CDR-SB prediction over six-fold (\(R^2 = 0.2648\) for
\texttt{LEND-SiMR\ +\ MAI}; \(R^2 = 0.2443\) in \(0.86\text{s}\) for
\texttt{SiMLR}), with \(85\text{–}86\%\) of the brain-structure effect
acting indirectly through cognition (\(p < 10^{-15}\)). On this cohort,
unconstrained deep encoders without a linear first layer overfit the 382
MRI features and collapse on held-out patients (\texttt{NED-SiMR}
\(R^2 = 0.1127\)), demonstrating that the OLEE linear entrypoint
provides an essential safeguard against high-dimensional overfitting
while preserving direct biomarker auditability.

\chapter{Introduction and Background}\label{introduction-and-background}

\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

High-throughput biomedical studies routinely collect multiple distinct
measurements from each subject. In neurodegenerative disease research,
cohorts such as the Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI)
({Weiner et al.} 2017; {Jack et al.} 2008) and the Parkinson's
Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI) ({Marek et al.} 2011) acquire
structural MRI regional morphometry, diffusion tensor white matter
fractional anisotropy, molecular PET, biofluid assays, and comprehensive
psychometric batteries (Avants et al. 2014; {Stone et al.} 2020; Avants
et al. 2021). In traumatic brain injury (TBI) and neurotrauma,
blast-induced shockwave exposure produces diffuse axonal injury and
non-local microstructural shearing that alter structural connectivity
networks ({Stone et al.} 2024). In translational oncology, the Cancer
Genome Atlas (TCGA) profiles patient tumors across somatic copy-number
alterations, transcriptomic expression, DNA methylation, and targeted
reverse-phase protein arrays in breast invasive carcinoma (BRCA) (The
Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; Cancer Discovery 2016) and clear cell
renal cell carcinoma (KIRC) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network
2013). Integrating these observations---formalized as \(M\) data
matrices \(\mathbf{X}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p_m}\) for
\(m = 1, \dots, M\) across \(n\) co-registered subjects---is essential
for resolving phenotypic heterogeneity, identifying molecular subtypes,
and mapping progression trajectories.

Deep representation learning methods, including Deep Canonical
Correlation Analysis (Deep CCA) (Andrew et al. 2013) and Multimodal
Variational Autoencoders (MVAEs) (Wu and Goodman 2018), capture
non-linear cross-modal dependencies. However, standard deep encoders map
inputs through deeply nested affine transformations interleaved with
non-linear activations
(\(\mathbf{Z} = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_2 \sigma(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{b}_1) + \mathbf{b}_2)\)).
This composition creates complex feature entanglement, obscuring the
physical attribution between observable biological measurements and
latent coordinates (Rudin 2019). In clinical diagnostics, oncology
biomarker discovery, and neurotrauma assessment, this opacity imposes a
severe \textbf{transparency penalty}: clinicians, regulatory agencies,
and trial biostatisticians cannot determine whether a diagnostic score
reflects authentic pathophysiology (such as entorhinal cortical atrophy
or targeted kinase dysregulation) or idiosyncratic scanner noise and
batch artifacts (Holmberg et al. 2022).

Post-hoc explainability techniques, such as LIME (Ribeiro et al. 2016)
and SHAP (Lundberg and Lee 2017), attempt to infer feature importance by
querying black-box models on randomly perturbed inputs. As demonstrated
by Rudin (2019), post-hoc surrogates exhibit fundamental failure modes
in biological domains. By perturbing features independently, they
evaluate synthetic samples that violate physiological
covariance---pairing, for example, incompatible regional brain volumes
or mutually exclusive oncogenic mutations. Consequently, post-hoc
explanations can be unstable, sensitive to hyperparameter tuning, and
biologically deceptive (Radulovic 2023).

Conversely, classical linear dimensionality reduction
methods---including Principal Component Analysis (PCA) and Canonical
Correlation Analysis (CCA) (Hotelling 1936)---provide exact linear
loadings (\(Z_{jk} = \sum_i X_{ji} V_{ik}\)). Similarity-driven
Multimodal Linear Reconstruction (SiMLR) (Avants et al. 2021) extended
CCA to multiple modalities with sparsity and non-negativity constraints,
solving a joint-variational problem on the Stiefel manifold. While
linear SiMLR guarantees interpretability, complex disease processes can
involve non-linear manifold geometry that linear projections cannot
span. Furthermore, unconstrained PCA yields dense, mixed-sign loadings
that lack anatomical specificity and fail to isolate shared cross-modal
signals from modality-specific noise.

\subsection{The Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE)
Contract}\label{the-only-linear-encoder-entrypoint-olee-contract}

To resolve the tension between non-linear expressivity and auditable
feature attribution, we formulate Deep SiMLR governed by the
\textbf{Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE)} contract. OLEE mandates
that the initial entrypoint for every modality is restricted to a linear
projection on the compact Stiefel manifold: \[
\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \text{subject to } \mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m) = \{\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}_k\}
\] All downstream non-linear transformations---including
volume-preserving normalizing flows, Riemannian quasi-Newton solvers,
and non-linear decoders---operate exclusively on the low-dimensional
latent coordinates \(\mathbf{Z}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\).

Enforcing the OLEE contract yields three fundamental mathematical and
clinical guarantees:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Elimination of the Transparency Penalty:} Because the input
  mapping is strictly linear, the directional attribution gradient with
  respect to raw physical features is globally constant and analytically
  exact: \[
  \nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \frac{\partial Z_{m, ik}}{\partial X_{m, ij}} = V_{m, jk}
  \] Unlike black-box encoders where local gradients vary wildly across
  the input manifold, the OLEE loading matrix \(\mathbf{V}_m\) directly
  encodes the physical contribution of every biological feature.
\item
  \textbf{Closed-Form Counterfactual Recourse:} In causal recourse and
  actionable intervention frameworks (Pearl 2009; Wachter et al. 2017),
  a critical objective is determining what observable biomarker
  intervention \(\Delta \mathbf{x}\) will produce a desired therapeutic
  shift \(\Delta \mathbf{z}^*\) in disease state. Under standard deep
  encoders, calculating recourse requires non-convex gradient descent
  through non-linear latents, regularly synthesizing physically
  impossible biological states. Under the OLEE contract, forward latent
  shifts are instantaneous: \[
  \Delta \mathbf{Z}_m = \Delta \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m
  \] while the minimal \(L_2\)-norm physical intervention required to
  achieve a target shift \(\Delta \mathbf{z}_m^*\) is an exact
  orthogonal projection: \[
  \delta \mathbf{x}_m^* = \Delta \mathbf{z}_m^* \mathbf{V}_m^\top
  \] This delivers immediate, closed-form causal auditability without
  heuristic sampling.
\item
  \textbf{Spectral Noise Insulation:} To prevent high-dimensional
  isotropic noise from distorting consensus coordinates, we incorporate
  dynamic Modality Alignment Index (MAI) weighting based on singular
  value decay, down-weighting uninformative modalities during consensus
  optimization.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multimodal Consensus as Latent Signal
Recovery}\label{multimodal-consensus-as-latent-signal-recovery}

We formalize multi-view integration as the estimation of a shared latent
signal \(\mathbf{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) from \(M\)
observations: \[
\mathbf{X}_m = g_m(\mathbf{S}) + \mathbf{E}_m, \quad m = 1, \dots, M
\] where \(g_m: \mathbb{R}^{n \times k} \to \mathbb{R}^{n \times p_m}\)
is a modality-specific generative mapping and \(\mathbf{E}_m\)
represents domain-specific noise and private variance. Multi-modal
consensus seeks mappings \(f_m(\mathbf{X}_m)\) that project each
observation to a unified consensus representation
\(\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) approximating \(\mathbf{S}\).

In biological cohorts, ambient feature dimensions often far exceed
sample size (\(p_m \gg n\)). When a high-dimensional view contains
little structured covariance, standard correlation objectives can
capitalize on chance associations, corrupting the consensus space
({Avants et al.} 2025; {Stone et al.} 2024). Principled integration
requires explicit regularization that penalizes off-manifold noise while
preserving true biological signal.

\subsection{Why Brain MRI Needs Cognitive Mediation to Predict Daily
Disability in
ADNI-MCI}\label{why-brain-mri-needs-cognitive-mediation-to-predict-daily-disability-in-adni-mci}

A clear real-world example of why multi-view alignment and a linear
first layer (OLEE) matter comes from predicting daily functional decline
in \(N = 1,084\) patients with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) in the
Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI) ({Weiner et al.}
2017; {Jack et al.} 2008). In clinical trials, the standard outcome used
to judge whether a treatment works is the \textbf{Clinical Dementia
Rating -- Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB)}, a clinical score measuring how much
difficulty a patient has with everyday tasks such as managing money,
remembering appointments, shopping, and personal care.

Each patient has four views of baseline data (after adjusting for age,
sex, and years of education):

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Three structural brain MRI views (\(382\) brain regions
  total):} regional gray-matter volumes (\(p_1 = 160\)), hierarchical
  cortical surface thickness (\(p_2 = 160\)), and native T1-weighted
  cortical thickness (\(p_3 = 62\)).
\item
  \textbf{One cognitive test view (\(p_4 = 13\) items):} bedside memory,
  language, and executive-function sub-scores from the ADAS-Cog and MMSE
  batteries.
\end{itemize}

Comparing different ways of predicting CDR-SB on held-out patients
reveals three simple findings:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  \textbf{Predicting daily disability directly from brain MRI barely
  works (\(R^2 = 0.0411\)):} When a model tries to predict CDR-SB
  directly from the 382 brain MRI measurements alone, it explains only
  \textbf{\(4.1\%\) of the variance} on unseen test patients
  (\(\text{Direct-Anatomy [SiMLR]}: R^2 = 0.0411 \pm 0.0248, \rho = 0.2203\)),
  and standard ridge regression on the concatenated MRI features fails
  completely (\(R^2 = -0.1810 \pm 0.0916\)). The reason is simple
  biology: tissue loss in the hippocampus or temporal cortex does not
  directly make a person unable to balance a checkbook. Instead,
  \textbf{brain atrophy first impairs cognitive test performance (memory
  and thinking), and that cognitive decline is what causes trouble with
  daily activities}.
\item
  \textbf{Modeling the two-step pathway
  (\(\text{Brain MRI} \to \text{Shared Cognitive Summary} \to \text{CDR-SB}\))
  boosts prediction 6-fold (\(R^2 = 0.2648\)):} When we align the three
  brain MRI views (\(382\) features) with the 13 cognitive test items to
  learn a shared cognitive-anatomical summary (\(Z_{\text{cog}}\)) and
  use that summary to predict CDR-SB, out-of-sample accuracy jumps
  \textbf{more than six-fold}:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    \textbf{\(\text{LEND-SiMR + MAI [Mediated]}\):}

  brain region's weight directly readable.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background}\label{background}

\subsection{Subspace Invariance versus Basis Axis
Sensitivity}\label{subspace-invariance-versus-basis-axis-sensitivity}

When evaluating multi-view representations, it is essential to
distinguish between the \emph{subspace} spanned by a projection and the
\emph{individual coordinate axes}:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Subspace Invariance:} For any orthogonal transformation
  \(\mathbf{M} \in O(k)\), the rotated representation
  \(\mathbf{Z}_m \mathbf{M} = \mathbf{X}_m (\mathbf{V}_m \mathbf{M})\)
  spans the identical linear subspace as \(\mathbf{Z}_m\). Linear models
  trained on \(\mathbf{Z}_m \mathbf{M}\) achieve identical predictions
  to those trained on \(\mathbf{Z}_m\).
\item
  \textbf{Axis Sensitivity:} Identifying individual anatomical or
  molecular biomarkers requires that specific coordinate axes align with
  meaningful physical factors. Axis-sensitive probes, such as
  axis-aligned decision trees and random forests, depend on the specific
  orientation of \(\mathbf{V}_m\).
\item
  \textbf{Signed Support Disjointness:} In non-negative or
  contrast-split representations
  (\(\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{V}^+ - \mathbf{V}^-\) with
  \(\mathbf{V}^+, \mathbf{V}^- \ge 0\)), overlap between positive and
  negative lobes produces destructive cancellation. Enforcing disjoint
  support (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)) eliminates
  crosstalk, ensuring that positive and negative coefficients index
  distinct feature sets.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Architectural Taxonomy}\label{architectural-taxonomy}

We evaluate nine distinct architectures spanning linear baselines,
manifold-regularized linear solvers, hybrid autoencoders, and
normalizing flows:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{PCA (Classical Linear Baseline):} Standard Principal Component
  Analysis fitted via singular value decomposition on concatenated,
  standardized features
  (\(\mathbf{X} = [\mathbf{X}_1 \dots \mathbf{X}_M]\)). Establishes the
  unconstrained dense linear baseline.
\item
  \textbf{NSAFlow-PCA (Accelerated Non-negative Stiefel Flow):} A fast
  single-matrix manifold solver utilizing direct SVD initialization on
  concatenated features and differentiable Non-negative Stiefel
  Approximating Flow (\texttt{nsa\_flow}) retraction in
  \(0.029\text{s}\).
\item
  \textbf{SiMLR (Riemannian Gradient Descent):} The classical linear
  cross-view formulation solving the multi-view alignment problem via
  Armijo line-search gradient descent with per-sweep NSA-Flow
  retraction.
\item
  \textbf{SiMLR-LBFGS (Quasi-Newton Riemannian):} A linear cross-view
  architecture incorporating two-loop L-BFGS curvature steps on the
  Stiefel manifold with per-sweep NSA-Flow retraction.
\item
  \textbf{SiMLR-LBFGSB (Bound-Constrained Quasi-Newton):} A linear
  cross-view architecture using the NSA-Flow bound-constrained L-BFGS-B
  solver (\texttt{nsa\_lbfgsb}), enforcing
  \(\mathbf{V}_m \ge \mathbf{0}\) inside the quasi-Newton step itself
  when positivity is active.
\item
  \textbf{LEND (Linear Encoder, Non-linear Decoder):} A hybrid OLEE
  architecture coupling per-view linear Stiefel projections
  (\(\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\)) with a multi-layer
  perceptron decoder for non-linear feature reconstruction and
  cross-view consensus alignment.
\item
  \textbf{Flow-SiMR-V (Bijective Normalizing Flow with Stiefel
  Bottleneck):} An invertible OLEE architecture prepending a linear
  Stiefel projection matrix to volume-preserving RealNVP affine coupling
  layers in \(\mathbb{R}^k\), enabling continuous density estimation,
  exact invertibility, and conditional cross-view imputation.
\item
  \textbf{NED (Non-linear Encoder-Decoder):} A deep architecture adding
  hidden non-linear encoder layers after the initial linear projection.
\item
  \textbf{NEDPP (Shared and Private Subspaces):} An extension of NED
  that partitions latent coordinates into shared consensus and
  view-specific private subspaces using cross-covariance penalties.
\end{itemize}

\section{Theoretical Foundation}\label{theoretical-foundation}

\subsection{The Multi-Modal Inverse
Problem}\label{the-multi-modal-inverse-problem}

We frame multi-modal representation learning as a structured inverse
problem over \(M\) distinct observational domains. Let
\(\mathbf{X}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p_m}\) denote the centered
observation matrix for modality \(m \in \{1, \dots, M\}\), where \(n\)
is the number of co-registered subjects and \(p_m\) is the ambient
feature dimensionality. Each modality provides a partial, noisy
observation of an underlying shared latent biological state
\(\mathbf{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) governed by modality-specific
forward generative processes:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-generative-model}{
\mathbf{X}_m = g_m(\mathbf{S}) + \mathbf{E}_m
}\end{equation} where \(g_m: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\) is a
smooth generative map applied row-wise across observations, and
\(\mathbf{E}_m\) represents domain-specific observational noise and
idiosyncratic variance. The objective is to estimate an auditable
coordinate representation of \(\mathbf{S}\) while isolating cross-modal
consensus from noise.

\subsection{OLEE as a Structural Information
Bottleneck}\label{olee-as-a-structural-information-bottleneck}

Standard deep autoencoders and multi-layer perceptrons process inputs
via unconstrained mappings \(\mathbf{Z} = \text{MLP}(\mathbf{X})\).
Following Tishby's Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al.
2000), deep networks entangle mutual information across successive
layers, readily memorizing high-dimensional noise and structured
nuisance variables. In biomedical discovery, this produces severe latent
collapse and obscures physical feature attribution.

We resolve this entanglement by enforcing the \textbf{Only Linear
Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE)}. OLEE mandates that the initial
transformation of any modality \(\mathbf{X}_m\) is an orthogonal linear
projection onto the compact Stiefel manifold
\(\text{St}(k, p_m) = \{ \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k \}\)
(Edelman et al. 1998; Boumal 2023):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-olee-projection}{
\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}
}\end{equation} Because the intrinsic dimension is strictly bounded
(\(k \ll p_m\)) and \(\mathbf{V}_m\) acts as a partial isometry, OLEE
imposes a structural information bottleneck at the input layer. All
subsequent non-linear operations (decoders, prediction heads, or
normalizing flows) consume \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\), never raw
features \(\mathbf{X}_m\). This guarantees that downstream
transformations operate on an auditable coordinate system with
closed-form directional derivatives
\(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{V}_m\).

\subsection{Joint-Variational Optimization
Framework}\label{joint-variational-optimization-framework}

We unify the Similarity-driven Multimodal Linear Representation (SiMLR)
taxonomy under a joint-variational functional. For each modality
\(m \in \{1, \dots, M\}\), we optimize an encoder
\(f_m: \mathbb{R}^{p_m} \to \mathbb{R}^k\) and a decoder
\(g_m: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\), yielding latent
representations
\(\mathbf{Z}_m = f_m(\mathbf{X}_m) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) and
reconstructions \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_m = g_m(\mathbf{Z}_m)\). The global
variational functional balances within-modality reconstruction fidelity,
cross-modal consensus alignment, and Stiefel manifold regularization:

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-joint-variational-objective}{
\min_{\{f_m, g_m\}_{m=1}^M} \sum_{m=1}^M \left[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{X}_m, g_m(\mathbf{Z}_m)) + \lambda_{\text{sim}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}(\mathbf{Z}_m, \mathbf{U}_{\neq m}) + \gamma D(\mathbf{V}_m) \right] + \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{Z})
}\end{equation}

where:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) is the
  \textbf{Leave-One-Out (LOO)} consensus target derived from all
  modalities excluding modality \(m\):
  \begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-loo-consensus}{
  \mathbf{U}_{\neq m} = \text{Consensus}\left( \{\mathbf{Z}_j\}_{j \neq m} \right)
  }\end{equation} By strictly quarantining modality \(m\) from
  contributing to its own target, the optimization landscape precludes
  trivial self-reconstruction loops and identity memorization.
\item
  \(D(\mathbf{V}_m)\) is the scale-invariant Stiefel frame defect
  penalizing departures from column orthonormality.
\item
  \(\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{Z})\) defines joint regularization terms, such
  as cross-covariance penalties between shared and private partitions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Robust Log-Cosh Negentropy Alignment
Functional}\label{robust-log-cosh-negentropy-alignment-functional}

The choice of alignment functional \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\)
determines the geometry of cross-modal consensus. While standard Mean
Squared Error (MSE) assumes isotropic Gaussian errors, biomedical
measurements routinely exhibit heavy-tailed noise and asymmetric
clinical outliers. To ensure robust consensus formation, we implement
the log-cosh negentropy contrast functional
(\texttt{calculate\_ica\_energy} in \texttt{src/pysimlr/simlr.py}):

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-log-cosh-energy}{
\mathcal{L}_{\text{log-cosh}}(\mathbf{Z}_m, \mathbf{U}_{\neq m}) = - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^k \left( |C_{ji}| - \log 2 + \log\left(1 + e^{-2|C_{ji}|}\right) \right)
}\end{equation}

where
\(\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{U}_{\neq m}^\top \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}\)
represents the cross-modal projection matrix with entries \(C_{ji}\),
distinct from the underlying biological state \(\mathbf{S}\), and the
outer factor \(1/n\) normalizes the sum over latent components by the
sample size \(n\) (\texttt{calculate\_ica\_energy} in
\texttt{src/pysimlr/simlr.py:844-848}).

The first-order variational gradient of the loss with respect to the
projection basis \(\mathbf{V}_m\) evaluates to (with
\texttt{calculate\_ica\_gradient} returning the corresponding ascent
direction \(-\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} \mathcal{L}_{\text{log-cosh}}\)):

\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-log-cosh-gradient}{
\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} \mathcal{L}_{\text{log-cosh}} = - \frac{1}{n} \mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{U}_{\neq m} \tanh(\mathbf{C})
}\end{equation}

Because the hyperbolic tangent is strictly bounded, \(|\tanh(c)| < 1\)
for all \(c \in \mathbb{R}\), the maximum gradient contribution from any
single observation or corrupted feature is bounded. This prevents
leverage points and extreme phenotypic outliers from destabilizing the
shared consensus trajectory.

\subsection{SiMLR-LBFGS and SiMLR-LBFGSB (Quasi-Newton Riemannian Linear
Solvers)}\label{simlr-lbfgs-and-simlr-lbfgsb-quasi-newton-riemannian-linear-solvers}

Standard first-order gradient descent oscillates across narrow ravines

\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS} integrates second-order quasi-Newton curvature
steps directly on the parameter space using PyTorch's native
\texttt{optim.LBFGS} (\texttt{src/pysimlr/optimizers.py:841-894}), while

(243-evaluation) benchmark, while \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS} converges in
\(0.175\text{s}\) (\(2\times\) faster than first-order \texttt{SiMLR}).

\subsection{Shared-Private NEDPP (Disentangled
Discovery)}\label{shared-private-nedpp-disentangled-discovery}

Despite LOO consensus, modality-specific idiosyncratic noise can
dominate the optimization landscape. NEDPP resolves this by explicitly
partitioning the latent space:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Encoder:}
  \(f_m(\mathbf{X}_m) = [\text{MLP}_{\phi_{m,\text{shared}}}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{shared}}) \parallel \text{MLP}_{\phi_{m,\text{private}}}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{private}})] = [\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{shared}} \parallel \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{private}}]\)
\item
  \textbf{Decoder:}
  \(g_m(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{shared}} \parallel \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{private}}) = \text{MLP}_{\theta_m}([\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{shared}} \parallel \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{private}}])\)

orthogonalizing the shared and private representations (schematized in
Figure~\ref{fig-nedpp}).

\subsection{Flow-SiMR and Flow-SiMR-V: Bijective Multimodal

In accordance with mathematical precision, we eliminate the historical
`L' (Linear) designation from normalizing flow models, as affine

between two distinct flow architectures implemented in

\subsubsection{Full-Space Normalizing Flow

ambient observation space \(\mathbb{R}^{p_m} \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\) (see
Figure~\ref{fig-flow-simr}):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-flow-simr-full}{
\mathbf{Z}_m = \text{Flow}_{\psi_m}(\mathbf{X}_m)
}\end{equation} Latent coordinates are partitioned into shared consensus
dimensions \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{shared}} = \mathbf{Z}_{m, 1:k}\) and
modality-specific private dimensions
\(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{private}} = \mathbf{Z}_{m, k+1:p_m}\). Decoding is
achieved by zero-padding the private partition:
\(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_m = \text{Flow}_{\psi_m}^{-1}([\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{shared}} \parallel \mathbf{0}])\).

\subsubsection{Invertible Affine Coupling and Joint Gaussian
Inference}\label{invertible-affine-coupling-and-joint-gaussian-inference}

Each view is modeled via an invertible mapping composed of RealNVP
affine coupling layers (Dinh et al. 2016; Rezende and Mohamed 2015). For
coordinate vector \(\mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^k\), a coupling layer
splits coordinates using a binary mask \(\mathbf{b} \in \{0, 1\}^k\):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-realnvp-forward}{
\mathbf{z}_1 = \mathbf{b} \odot \mathbf{z}, \quad \mathbf{z}_2 = (1 - \mathbf{b}) \odot \left( \mathbf{z} \odot e^{s(\mathbf{z}_1)} + t(\mathbf{z}_1) \right), \quad \mathbf{z}' = \mathbf{z}_1 + \mathbf{z}_2
}\end{equation} where \(s\) and \(t\) are scaling and translation
networks. Scale outputs are bounded via
\(s(\mathbf{z}_1) = \text{scale\_bound} \times \tanh(s_{\text{net}}(\mathbf{z}_1))\)
to ensure numerical stability. The inverse transformation is
analytically exact:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-realnvp-inverse}{
\mathbf{z}_1 = \mathbf{b} \odot \mathbf{z}', \quad \mathbf{z}_2 = (1 - \mathbf{b}) \odot \left( (\mathbf{z}' - t(\mathbf{z}_1)) \odot e^{-s(\mathbf{z}_1)} \right), \quad \mathbf{z} = \mathbf{z}_1 + \mathbf{z}_2
}\end{equation} with log-Jacobian determinant
\(\log |\det J| = \sum_{j: b_j = 0} s_j(\mathbf{z}_1)\).

Because normalizing flows map empirical data to standard Gaussian
latents, the concatenated shared latent space across all views is
parameterized as a joint Gaussian:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-joint-gaussian-latent}{
\mathbf{Z}_{\text{joint}} = [\mathbf{Z}_{1,\text{shared}} \parallel \dots \parallel \mathbf{Z}_{M,\text{shared}}] \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma)
}\end{equation} Partitioning into observed view \(A\) and unobserved
view \(B\), the conditional latent mean given observed view \(A\)
evaluates via the Schur complement:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-conditional-mean-schur}{
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{B|A} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_B + \Sigma_{BA} \Sigma_{AA}^{-1} (\mathbf{Z}_{A,\text{shared}} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_A)
}\end{equation} Solved via Cholesky factorization, the conditional
coordinates are decoded into ambient space. For Full-Space Flow-SiMR,
decoding is evaluated via
\(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_B = \text{Flow}_{\psi_B}^{-1}([\boldsymbol{\mu}_{B|A} \parallel \mathbf{0}])\),
whereas for Flow-SiMR-V, decoding projects back through the OLEE basis:
\(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_B = \text{Flow}_{\psi_B}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{B|A}) \mathbf{V}_B^\top\).

\section{Manifold Optimization and Algorithmic
Mechanics}\label{manifold-optimization-and-algorithmic-mechanics}

\subsection{Stiefel Manifold Optimization and SVD
Elimination}\label{stiefel-manifold-optimization-and-svd-elimination}

The parameter space for each projection basis
\(\mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\) is the compact Stiefel
manifold:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-stiefel-manifold}{
\text{St}(k, p_m) = \{ \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}_k \}
}\end{equation} In Euclidean gradient descent, unconstrained parameter
updates displace iterates off the manifold. Mapping tangent iterates
\(\mathbf{Y} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\) back to
\(\text{St}(k, p_m)\) requires a retraction mapping
\(R: T \text{St}(k, p_m) \to \text{St}(k, p_m)\).

\subsubsection{Riemannian Tangent Space and Metric
Projections}\label{riemannian-tangent-space-and-metric-projections}

For an iterate \(\mathbf{V} \in \text{St}(k, p_m)\), the tangent space
\(T_{\mathbf{V}} \text{St}(k, p_m)\) comprises all displacement matrices
\(\Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\) satisfying the linearized
orthonormality constraint:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-stiefel-tangent-space}{
T_{\mathbf{V}} \text{St}(k, p_m) = \{ \Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}^\top \Delta + \Delta^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{0} \}
}\end{equation} Under the standard Euclidean metric
\(\langle \mathbf{A}, \mathbf{B} \rangle = \text{tr}(\mathbf{A}^\top \mathbf{B})\)
inherited from the ambient embedding space
\(\mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\), the orthogonal projection of an
unconstrained ambient gradient
\(\mathbf{G} = \nabla_{\mathbf{V}} \mathcal{L}\) onto the tangent space
\(T_{\mathbf{V}} \text{St}(k, p_m)\) evaluates in closed form
(\texttt{project\_gradient} in \texttt{src/pysimlr/simlr.py:111-146},
following Edelman et al. (1998)):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-stiefel-tangent-proj}{
\Pi_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}(\mathbf{G}) = \mathbf{G} - \mathbf{V} \, \text{sym}(\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{G}) = \mathbf{G} - \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{V} (\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{G} + \mathbf{G}^\top \mathbf{V})
}\end{equation} where
\(\text{sym}(\mathbf{M}) = \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{M} + \mathbf{M}^\top)\).
(In Grassmann mode, the horizontal tangent projection eliminates the
full column space:
\(\Pi_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}^{\text{Gr}}(\mathbf{G}) = \mathbf{G} - \mathbf{V}(\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{G})\).)

The Riemannian gradient is thus given by
\(\text{grad}_{\text{St}} \mathcal{L} = \Pi_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}(\nabla_{\mathbf{V}} \mathcal{L})\).
Moving along the tangent direction yields an ambient search candidate
\(\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{V} - \eta \, \text{grad}_{\text{St}} \mathcal{L}\),
which is subsequently mapped back to the manifold via a metric
retraction \(R(\mathbf{Y}) \in \text{St}(k, p_m)\).

\subsubsection{Sylvester Polar Factor
Retraction}\label{sylvester-polar-factor-retraction}

The canonical metric retraction is the polar factor projection:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-polar-factor}{
R(\mathbf{Y}) = \mathbf{Y} (\mathbf{Y}^\top \mathbf{Y})^{-1/2}
}\end{equation} Standard implementations compute
\(R(\mathbf{Y}) = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{W}^\top\) via Singular Value
Decomposition (SVD), \(\mathbf{Y} = \mathbf{U} \Sigma \mathbf{W}^\top\).
However, explicit SVD incurs severe computational and numerical
pathology:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Branch Divergence \& Kernel Serialization}: SVD algorithms
  (e.g.~Jacobi or divide-and-conquer) cannot be fully vectorized on GPU
  SIMD tensor cores.
\item
  \textbf{Singular Value Multiplicity Singularity}: The backpropagation
  derivative of an SVD contains terms proportional to
  \((\sigma_i^2 - \sigma_j^2)^{-1}\). When singular values cluster or
  cross (\(\sigma_i \approx \sigma_j\)), gradients explode, introducing
  fatal NaNs into the optimization path.
\end{enumerate}

To circumvent this, we express the polar factor
\(\mathbf{H} = (\mathbf{Y}^\top \mathbf{Y})^{1/2}\) via the continuous
Lyapunov/Sylvester system (\texttt{src/pysimlr/nsa\_backend.py:236-254},
with SVD fallback in \texttt{src/pysimlr/sparsification.py:494-510}):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-lyapunov-sylvester}{
\mathbf{H} (d\mathbf{H}) + (d\mathbf{H}) \mathbf{H} = d(\mathbf{Y}^\top \mathbf{Y}) = (d\mathbf{Y})^\top \mathbf{Y} + \mathbf{Y}^\top (d\mathbf{Y})
}\end{equation} Because \(\mathbf{H}\) is symmetric positive definite
(\(\mathbf{H} \succ 0\)), this Sylvester equation has a provably unique
solution \(d\mathbf{H}\). Gradients flow smoothly through
backpropagation without encountering eigenvalue multiplicity
singularities.

\subsubsection{Newton-Schulz Cubic Matrix Recurrence and Convergence
Proof}\label{newton-schulz-cubic-matrix-recurrence-and-convergence-proof}

To eliminate SVD factorizations entirely from the forward execution
loop, we approximate the inverse square root
\((\mathbf{Y}^\top \mathbf{Y})^{-1/2}\) via the cubic Newton-Schulz
matrix polynomial (\texttt{src/pysimlr/utils.py:1324-1380}):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-newton-schulz}{
\mathbf{Y}_0 = \frac{\mathbf{Y}}{\|\mathbf{Y}\|_F}, \quad \mathbf{Y}_{\tau+1} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{Y}_\tau \left( 3 \mathbf{I}_k - \mathbf{Y}_\tau^\top \mathbf{Y}_\tau \right)
}\end{equation}

\textbf{Theorem (Quadratic Convergence to the Stiefel Manifold):}\\
Let \(\mathbf{Y}_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times k}\) have singular values
\(\sigma_{0, i} \in (0, \sqrt{3})\) for all \(i \in \{1, \dots, k\}\).
The sequence of iterates \(\mathbf{Y}_\tau\) converges quadratically to
the nearest point on the Stiefel manifold \(\text{St}(k, p)\):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-newton-schulz-rate}{
\|\mathbf{Y}_{\tau+1}^\top \mathbf{Y}_{\tau+1} - \mathbf{I}_k\| = \mathcal{O}\left(\|\mathbf{Y}_\tau^\top \mathbf{Y}_\tau - \mathbf{I}_k\|^2\right)
}\end{equation}

\textbf{Proof:}\\
Let the singular value decomposition of iterate \(\tau\) be
\(\mathbf{Y}_\tau = \mathbf{P} \Sigma_\tau \mathbf{Q}^\top\), where
\(\mathbf{P} \in \text{St}(k, p)\), \(\mathbf{Q} \in \mathbb{O}(k)\),
and
\(\Sigma_\tau = \text{diag}(\sigma_{\tau, 1}, \dots, \sigma_{\tau, k})\).
Substituting this decomposition into the Newton-Schulz recurrence: \[
\mathbf{Y}_{\tau+1} = \frac{1}{2} \left(\mathbf{P} \Sigma_\tau \mathbf{Q}^\top\right) \left( 3 \mathbf{I}_k - \mathbf{Q} \Sigma_\tau^2 \mathbf{Q}^\top \right) = \mathbf{P} \left[ \frac{1}{2} \Sigma_\tau \left( 3 \mathbf{I}_k - \Sigma_\tau^2 \right) \right] \mathbf{Q}^\top
\] Because the singular vectors \(\mathbf{P}\) and \(\mathbf{Q}\) are
stationary under the iteration, the matrix recurrence decouples into
\(k\) independent scalar recurrences for each singular value
\(\sigma_{\tau, i}\): \[
\sigma_{\tau+1, i} = \frac{1}{2} \sigma_{\tau, i} \left( 3 - \sigma_{\tau, i}^2 \right)
\] Define the relative distance to the Stiefel manifold for singular
value \(i\) as \(\epsilon_{\tau, i} = 1 - \sigma_{\tau, i}\). Expressing
\(\sigma_{\tau, i} = 1 - \epsilon_{\tau, i}\) and expanding: \[
\sigma_{\tau+1, i} = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \epsilon_{\tau, i}) \left( 3 - (1 - \epsilon_{\tau, i})^2 \right)
\] \[
= \frac{1}{2}(1 - \epsilon_{\tau, i}) \left( 3 - (1 - 2\epsilon_{\tau, i} + \epsilon_{\tau, i}^2) \right) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \epsilon_{\tau, i}) \left( 2 + 2\epsilon_{\tau, i} - \epsilon_{\tau, i}^2 \right)
\] \[
= \frac{1}{2} \left( 2 + 2\epsilon_{\tau, i} - \epsilon_{\tau, i}^2 - 2\epsilon_{\tau, i} - 2\epsilon_{\tau, i}^2 + \epsilon_{\tau, i}^3 \right) = 1 - \frac{3}{2}\epsilon_{\tau, i}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\tau, i}^3
\] Subtracting both sides from 1 yields the exact recurrence for the
error metric:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-newton-schulz-error}{
\epsilon_{\tau+1, i} = 1 - \sigma_{\tau+1, i} = \frac{3}{2}\epsilon_{\tau, i}^2 - \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\tau, i}^3 = \epsilon_{\tau, i}^2 \left( \frac{3 - \epsilon_{\tau, i}}{2} \right)
}\end{equation} For any \(\sigma_{0, i} \in (0, \sqrt{3})\),
\(|\epsilon_{0, i}| < 1\), ensuring monotonic error contraction. As
\(\epsilon \to 0\), the cubic term is negligible: \[
\epsilon_{\tau+1} = \frac{3}{2}\epsilon_\tau^2 + \mathcal{O}(\epsilon_\tau^3)
\] Thus, the recurrence converges with quadratic order
\(\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^2)\). Because the update requires only matrix
multiplications and additions (\(\mathbf{Y}^\top \mathbf{Y}\) and
\(\mathbf{Y} \mathbf{M}\)), it compiles directly to hardware tensor
cores, achieving zero CPU-GPU transfer bottlenecks. For high-dimensional
embeddings (\(k \ge 30\)), where singular values scale as
\(\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{k})\), 20 iterations or a spectral norm pre-scaler
\(\mathbf{Y}_0 = \mathbf{Y}/\sqrt{\|\mathbf{Y}^\top \mathbf{Y}\|_\infty}\)
ensure complete convergence. Geometrical intuition for this orthogonal
discovery process is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig-stiefel-intuition}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Quasi-Newton Curvature Steps via
\texttt{torch\_lbfgs}}{Quasi-Newton Curvature Steps via torch\_lbfgs}}\label{quasi-newton-curvature-steps-via-torch_lbfgs}

Joint-variational alignment produces an ill-conditioned optimization
landscape, characterized by narrow parabolic ravines formed by
cross-modal consensus constraints and Stiefel manifold boundaries.
Standard first-order stochastic gradient methods (SGD, Adam) suffer from
pathological oscillations across ravine walls.

We accelerate convergence by deploying quasi-Newton second-order
curvature estimation via \texttt{torch\_lbfgs}
(\texttt{src/pysimlr/optimizers.py:841-894}). Let
\(\mathbf{s}_t = \mathbf{V}_{t+1} - \mathbf{V}_t\) denote the parameter
displacement and
\(\mathbf{y}_t = \nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_{t+1}) - \nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t)\)
denote the gradient variation. Over a rolling history of size \(h=10\),
the inverse Hessian approximation
\(\mathbf{H}_t \approx \nabla^2 \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t)^{-1}\) is
computed via the two-loop recursion:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Backward History Loop}: Initialize
  \(\mathbf{q} = \nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t)\). For \(i = t-1\)
  down to \(t-h\): \[
  \alpha_i = \rho_i \text{tr}(\mathbf{s}_i^\top \mathbf{q}), \quad \mathbf{q} \leftarrow \mathbf{q} - \alpha_i \mathbf{y}_i, \quad \text{where } \rho_i = \frac{1}{\text{tr}(\mathbf{y}_i^\top \mathbf{s}_i)}
  \]
\item
  \textbf{Initial Scaling}: \(\mathbf{r} = \gamma_t \mathbf{q}\), with
  \(\gamma_t = \frac{\text{tr}(\mathbf{s}_{t-1}^\top \mathbf{y}_{t-1})}{\text{tr}(\mathbf{y}_{t-1}^\top \mathbf{y}_{t-1})}\).
\item
  \textbf{Forward History Loop}: For \(i = t-h\) up to \(t-1\): \[
  \beta = \rho_i \text{tr}(\mathbf{y}_i^\top \mathbf{r}), \quad \mathbf{r} \leftarrow \mathbf{r} + \mathbf{s}_i (\alpha_i - \beta)
  \]
\item
  \textbf{Descent Direction}: The update step is
  \(\mathbf{p}_t = -\mathbf{r}\), accepted under Strong Wolfe line
  search conditions:
  \begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-lbfgs-descent}{
  \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t + \alpha_t \mathbf{p}_t) \le \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t) + c_1 \alpha_t \text{tr}(\nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t)^\top \mathbf{p}_t)
  }\end{equation} \[
  |\text{tr}(\nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t + \alpha_t \mathbf{p}_t)^\top \mathbf{p}_t)| \le c_2 |\text{tr}(\nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t)^\top \mathbf{p}_t)|
  \]
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Analytical Gradient
Closures}\label{analytical-gradient-closures}

In PyTorch's native \texttt{optim.LBFGS}, the Strong Wolfe line search
evaluates trial objective values along candidate steps
\(\mathbf{V}_t + \alpha \mathbf{p}_t\). Because SiMLR relies on exact
analytical gradients rather than automatic differentiation, caching a
static gradient at step entry causes trial evaluations to use stale
directions, degrading the curvature estimate. We implement an analytical
callback closure:

\begin{Shaded}
\begin{Highlighting}[]
\KeywordTok{def}\NormalTok{ closure():}
\NormalTok{    optimizer.zero\_grad()}
    \ControlFlowTok{with}\NormalTok{ torch.no\_grad():}
\NormalTok{        v\_param.grad }\OperatorTok{=} \OperatorTok{{-}}\NormalTok{grad\_fn(v\_param.detach()).contiguous()}
\NormalTok{        loss }\OperatorTok{=}\NormalTok{ full\_energy\_function(v\_param.detach())}
    \ControlFlowTok{return}\NormalTok{ torch.as\_tensor(}\BuiltInTok{float}\NormalTok{(loss), device}\OperatorTok{=}\NormalTok{v\_param.device)}
\end{Highlighting}
\end{Shaded}

This guarantees that second-order curvature approximations remain
mathematically exact throughout the line search, enabling
\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} to achieve the best overall Friedman rank
(\(3.28\)) and highest sparse support recovery (\(0.942\)) across the
9-dataset (243-evaluation) benchmark while \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS}
converges in \(0.175\text{s}\).

\subsection{Scale-Invariant Frame Defect and Squared-Cosine Angle
Defect}\label{scale-invariant-frame-defect-and-squared-cosine-angle-defect}

To regularize the projection basis toward mutual orthogonality without
distorting data variance, we evaluate two complementary geometric
metrics: the trace-normalized Stiefel frame defect \(D(\mathbf{V})\) and
the squared-cosine angle defect \(C(\mathbf{V})\).

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Trace-Normalized Stiefel Frame Defect
(\(D\))}{Trace-Normalized Stiefel Frame Defect (D)}}\label{trace-normalized-stiefel-frame-defect-d}

The trace-normalized frame defect
(\texttt{src/pysimlr/utils.py:504-630}) measures departure from uniform
Stiefel projection:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-frame-defect}{
D(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{1}{1 - 1/k} \left\| \frac{\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V}}{\text{tr}(\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V})} - \frac{1}{k} \mathbf{I}_k \right\|_F^2
}\end{equation} Let
\(\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}\)
denote the unnormalized Gram matrix and let
\(t = \text{tr}(\mathbf{G}) = \|\mathbf{V}\|_F^2\). The normalized Gram
matrix is \(\tilde{\mathbf{G}} = \frac{\mathbf{G}}{t}\). Expanding the
Frobenius norm: \[
\|\tilde{\mathbf{G}} - \frac{1}{k} \mathbf{I}_k\|_F^2 = \sum_{i \ne j} \tilde{G}_{ij}^2 + \sum_{i=1}^k \left( \tilde{G}_{ii} - \frac{1}{k} \right)^2
\] The first summation measures column decorrelation, while the second
summation penalizes departures from \textbf{norm balance}
(\(\tilde{G}_{ii} = 1/k\)). Because the participation ratio is
\(\text{EffRank}(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{t^2}{\|\mathbf{G}\|_F^2}\), the
frame defect connects directly to effective rank: \[
D(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{1}{1 - 1/k} \left( \frac{1}{\text{EffRank}(\mathbf{V})} - \frac{1}{k} \right)
\] Differentiating with respect to \(\mathbf{V}\) gives the closed-form
gradient:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-frame-defect-gradient}{
\nabla_{\mathbf{V}} D(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{4}{t^2 (1 - 1/k)} \left[ \mathbf{V} \mathbf{G} - \frac{\|\mathbf{G}\|_F^2}{t} \mathbf{V} \right]
}\end{equation} Because \(D(\mathbf{V})\) is degree-0 homogeneous,
\(\langle \nabla_{\mathbf{V}} D(\mathbf{V}), \mathbf{V} \rangle \equiv 0\)
(Euler's theorem). The defect gradient exerts zero radial force on
matrix scale, acting purely as a shape-conforming projection.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{Squared-Cosine Angle Defect
(\(C\))}{Squared-Cosine Angle Defect (C)}}\label{squared-cosine-angle-defect-c}

While \(D(\mathbf{V})\) enforces strict orthonormality (decorrelation
plus equal column norms), biological signals frequently exhibit
heterogeneous variance hierarchies across components. Demanding equal
column lengths (\(\tilde{G}_{ii} = 1/k\)) penalizes true non-negative
components: a perfectly disjoint non-negative basis whose parts differ
in magnitude scores \(D > 0.20\) rather than zero.

To penalize departures from mutual orthogonality invariant to per-column
scaling, we implement the squared-cosine angle defect
(\texttt{angle\_defect} in \texttt{src/pysimlr/utils.py:633-724}), with
\(\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{V} \text{diag}(\|\mathbf{v}_1\|_2^{-1}, \dots, \|\mathbf{v}_k\|_2^{-1})\)
(norms floored at \(\epsilon = 10^{-12}\)):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-angle-defect}{
C(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{\|\mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{U} - \mathbf{I}_k\|_F^2}{k(k - 1)}
}\end{equation} Unlike globally trace-normalized off-diagonal
defects---which score a rank-collapsed matrix with \(k-1\) zero columns
at \(0.0\)---comparing \(\mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{U}\) against
\(\mathbf{I}_k\) charges any floored dead column \(1/(k(k-1))\) while
remaining invariant to arbitrary positive column rescalings:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-angle-defect-gradient}{
\nabla_{\mathbf{V}} C(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{4}{k(k - 1)} \left[ \mathbf{U} \mathbf{H} - \mathbf{U} \, \text{diag}\left(\text{rowsum}(\mathbf{H}^{\odot 2})\right) \right] \text{diag}(\|\mathbf{v}_1\|_2^{-1}, \dots, \|\mathbf{v}_k\|_2^{-1}), \quad \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{U} - \mathbf{I}_k
}\end{equation} For non-negative representations \(\mathbf{V} \ge 0\)
with non-zero columns, \(C(\mathbf{V}) = 0\) if and only if the columns
have pairwise disjoint supports, providing a column-scale-invariant
variational diagnostic for non-negative feature clustering.

\subsection{Support Consolidation and Direct SVD Contrast
Splitting}\label{support-consolidation-and-direct-svd-contrast-splitting}

In biological systems, features exhibit bidirectional responses (e.g.,
regional atrophy versus ventricular expansion). To discover signed
components while preserving non-negative interpretability, we lift each
basis \(\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times k}\) into paired
non-negative lobes:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-paired-lobes}{
\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{V}^+ - \mathbf{V}^-, \quad \mathbf{V}^+ = \max(\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{0}), \quad \mathbf{V}^- = \max(-\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{0})
}\end{equation} forming the non-negative matrix
\(\mathbf{W} = [\mathbf{V}^+ \mid \mathbf{V}^-] \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times 2k}\).

\subsubsection{Direct SVD Contrast
Initialization}\label{direct-svd-contrast-initialization}

Legacy implementations initialized non-negative signed flows via a
9-stage penalty continuation homotopy (\(\mu \in [0, 10^4]\)), requiring
hundreds of iterations and causing execution bottlenecks. On zero-mean
centered or signed data, we replace this homotopy with \textbf{Direct
SVD Contrast Splitting}:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-svd-contrast-splitting}{
\mathbf{E} = \text{SVD}_k(\mathbf{X}), \quad \mathbf{W}_0 = [\max(\mathbf{E}, \mathbf{0}) \mid \max(-\mathbf{E}, \mathbf{0})]
}\end{equation} Because the data is centered, the leading eigenvectors
\(\mathbf{E}\) possess zero column sums and symmetric positive/negative
mass. The lifted lobes \(\mathbf{W}_0\) provide an exact subspace
initialization (\(\mathbf{V}_0^+ - \mathbf{V}_0^- = \mathbf{E}\)) with
zero initial crosstalk
(\(\mathbf{V}_0^+ \odot \mathbf{V}_0^- = \mathbf{0}\)). For uncentered
non-negative data (\(\mathbf{X} \ge 0\)), an adaptive two-stage
continuation seeds both lobes, preventing boundary stalls. This direct
splitting yields a 157.7× wall-clock acceleration (from 5.33s to 0.034s
per fit).

\subsubsection{The Support Consolidation
Operator}\label{the-support-consolidation-operator}

Following continuous optimization at \(w=0.5\), we apply the
deterministic support consolidation operator
\(\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{V}^+, \mathbf{V}^-)\) to eliminate trailing
numerical tails:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-support-consolidation}{
\text{win}(j) = \arg\max_{l \in \{1, \dots, 2k\}} W_{j, l}
}\end{equation} Each feature \(j\) is assigned strictly to its dominant
component lobe and zeroed in all other channels, followed by a brief
constrained polish with supports fixed. This guarantees:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Zero Lobe Crosstalk}:
  \(\|\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^-\|_1 = 0.0000\).
\item
  \textbf{Exact Disjoint Sparsity}: Features map unambiguously to
  positive or negative axes without attribution leakage.
\item
  \textbf{Exact Manifold Invariance}: \(D(\mathbf{V}) = 0.0000\) and
  \(C(\mathbf{V}) = 0.0000\).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Consensus, Spectral Triage, and Gating
Warmup}\label{consensus-spectral-triage-and-gating-warmup}

\subsubsection{Block-Coordinate Descent and LOO
Topology}\label{block-coordinate-descent-and-loo-topology}

We formalize optimization through Block-Coordinate Descent (BCD). At
each iteration \(t\):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Consensus Formation (LOO Topology):} Fixing network parameters
  \(\{f_m, g_m\}\), consensus targets \(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\) are
  computed strictly excluding modality \(m\): \[
  \mathbf{U}_{\neq m}^{(t)} = \text{Consensus}(\{\mathbf{Z}_j^{(t-1)}\}_{j \neq m})
  \] This prevents high-capacity encoders from memorizing trivial
  self-mappings.
\item
  \textbf{Parameter Update:} Fixing \(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}^{(t)}\), each
  modality minimizes its local variational loss: \[
  \{f_m^{(t)}, g_m^{(t)}\} = \arg\min_{f_m, g_m} \mathcal{L}(\{f_m, g_m\}, \mathbf{U}_{\neq m}^{(t)})
  \]
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Sign-Aligned Newton-Schulz Polar Consensus
Mixing}\label{sign-aligned-newton-schulz-polar-consensus-mixing}

In multi-view representation learning, latent factorizations are
mathematically invariant under coordinate sign reflections
(\(\mathbf{z}_j \leftrightarrow -\mathbf{z}_j\)): if latent vector
\(\mathbf{u}_l\) explains a modality, then \(-\mathbf{u}_l\) with
negated loadings \(\mathbf{v}_l\) provides an identical physical
reconstruction. When integrating multiple observational domains, two
views can recover identical underlying biological factors with
diametrically opposite signs (e.g., empirical inner-product correlations
of \(r = -0.927\) and \(-0.959\)).

\paragraph{The Destructive Cancellation Failure
Mode}\label{the-destructive-cancellation-failure-mode}

Under antipodal sign alignment, naive cross-modal averaging induces
catastrophic destructive cancellation:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-destructive-cancellation}{
\bar{\mathbf{u}}_l = \frac{1}{M-1} \sum_{j \ne m} \mathbf{z}_{j, l} \approx \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}_l - \mathbf{u}_l) = \mathbf{0}
}\end{equation} As quantified in \texttt{src/pysimlr/utils.py:2003-2064}
on planted multi-view benchmarks, unaligned averaging collapses latent
column standard deviations from \(\sim 1.0\) down to \(0.121\),
precipitating a catastrophic collapse in downstream consensus predictive
fidelity from \(R^2 = 0.795\) to \(R^2 = 0.023\). This pathology is
silent, intermittent, and seed-dependent, masquerading as model
instability rather than an elementary arithmetic cancellation.

\paragraph{Greedy Column Sign
Alignment}\label{greedy-column-sign-alignment}

To resolve sign indeterminacy prior to consensus mixing, we implement
greedy column-wise sign alignment (\texttt{align\_column\_signs} in
\texttt{src/pysimlr/utils.py:2003-2064}). Taking the first view as an
initial reference \(\mathbf{R} = \mathbf{Z}_1\), each subsequent view's
columns are sequentially synchronized against the running coordinate
sum:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-greedy-sign-alignment}{
s_{j, l} = \text{sign}\left( \sum_{i=1}^n Z_{j, il} R_{il} \right), \quad \tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_j = \mathbf{Z}_j \, \text{diag}(s_{j, 1}, \dots, s_{j, k})
}\end{equation} \[
\mathbf{R} \leftarrow \mathbf{R} + \tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_j
\] Because the sign operators \(s_{j, l}\) are detached from the
computational graph, this alignment constitutes a coordinate relabeling
that leaves gradient magnitudes intact while eliminating destructive
cancellation.

\paragraph{Newton-Schulz Polar
Orthogonalization}\label{newton-schulz-polar-orthogonalization}

Following greedy sign alignment, PySIMLR computes the Leave-One-Out
consensus \(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\) using sign-aligned Newton-Schulz polar
orthogonalization as its default mixing mechanism
(\texttt{mixing\_algorithm="newton"} in
\texttt{src/pysimlr/consensus.py:121-124}): \[
\bar{\mathbf{U}}_{\neq m} = \frac{1}{M-1} \sum_{j \ne m} \tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_j
\] \begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-ns-consensus}{
\mathbf{U}_{\neq m} = \text{NewtonSchulz}\left( \bar{\mathbf{U}}_{\neq m}, \text{iterations}=10 \right)
}\end{equation} followed by per-column centering and unit variance
standardization (\(\text{std}(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}) = \mathbf{1}\)). Ten
iterations of the cubic Newton-Schulz recurrence ensure exact polar
factor orthonormality without invoking GPU-serializing SVD kernels.
Furthermore, in online deep consensus training with an exponential
running anchor buffer \(\mathbf{A}\), \texttt{align\_anchor\_columns}
(\texttt{src/pysimlr/consensus.py:9-45}) synchronizes the signs of
freshly computed consensus batches against \(\mathbf{A}\) prior to EMA
accumulation:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-anchor-update}{
\mathbf{A}^{(t)} = 0.9 \, \mathbf{A}^{(t-1)} + 0.1 \left( \mathbf{A}_{\text{new}} \odot \text{signs} \right)
}\end{equation} preventing destructive anchor decay across training
epochs.

\subsubsection{Spectral Triage and Modality Alignment Index
(MAI)}\label{spectral-triage-and-modality-alignment-index-mai}

The mathematical foundation for distinguishing informative biological
signal from unstructured bulk noise via spectral sharpness is
illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig-spectral-sharpness}.

\begin{figure}[H]

\centering{

\pandocbounded{\includegraphics[keepaspectratio]{02_methods_files/figure-pdf/fig-spectral-sharpness-output-1.pdf}}

}

\caption{\label{fig-spectral-sharpness}Spectral Sharpness as an Exact
Discriminant of Modality Fidelity. True generative signal manifests as
rank-1 perturbations that break the Marchenko-Pastur bulk distribution
limit (gray span). Informative modalities (green) exhibit significant
spectral sharpness, escaping the bulk limit to dominate the leading
singular values. Conversely, structureless isotropic noise modalities
(orange) remain entirely trapped within the analytically derived
Marchenko-Pastur threshold, guaranteeing their rigorous mathematical
exclusion via the Modality Alignment Index (MAI).}

\end{figure}%

In high-dimensional multi-modal integration, unconstrained
cross-covariance yields spurious alignments between unstructured noise
views and the shared consensus. Rather than unnormalized Euclidean
residual distances---which, when \(\mathbf{Z}_m\) and
\(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\) are normalized to unit Frobenius norm, expand to
\(2 - 2 \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i\) and clamp to exactly zero whenever
\(\sum \sigma_i < 0.5\) (rendering dynamic weighting inert across
typical benchmarks)---we quantify modality fidelity via the
\textbf{Scale-Profiled Procrustes \(R^2\)}
(\texttt{src/pysimlr/deep.py:839-869} and
\texttt{src/pysimlr/flows.py:376-407}):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-procrustes-r2}{
R_m^2 = \left( \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i(\mathbf{Z}_m^\top \mathbf{U}_{\neq m}) \right)^2 \in [0, 1]
}\end{equation} This represents the squared cosine between the optimally
rotated and scaled latent representations \(\mathbf{Z}_m\) and
\(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\). It is smoothly bounded in \([0, 1]\) and
strictly non-zero (\(0.989\) for informative biological signal versus
\(0.020\) for pure noise).

To rigorously penalize isotropic noise views that lack internal
coordinate structure, we modulate \(R_m^2\) by the \textbf{Spectral
Sharpness}
\(\mathcal{S}_m = \sigma_1(\mathbf{Z}_m) / \sum_{j=1}^k \sigma_j(\mathbf{Z}_m)\):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-mai-formulation}{
\text{MAI}_m = R_m^2 \cdot \mathcal{S}_m
}\end{equation} Under the Baik-Ben Arous-Péché (BBP) phase transition
theorem (Baik et al. 2005), isotropic noise remains trapped within the
Marchenko-Pastur bulk (\(\mathcal{S}_m \approx 1/k\)), driving
\(\text{MAI}_m \to 0\), whereas structured biological signal spikes
(\(\mathcal{S}_m \to 1\)).

\subsubsection{Exponential Moving Average, Sigmoid Outlier Gating, and
Continuous
Warmup}\label{exponential-moving-average-sigmoid-outlier-gating-and-continuous-warmup}

Raw alignment indices are stabilized across training epochs via
Exponential Moving Average (EMA):
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-mai-ema}{
\mathbf{mai}^{(t)} = 0.9 \, \mathbf{mai}^{(t-1)} + 0.1 \, \mathbf{mai}_{\text{raw}}^{(t)}
}\end{equation} To prevent premature view suppression (Weight Collapse)
during early exploration while squashing severe noise outliers, modality
weights are modulated by an adaptive sigmoid outlier gate
\(\omega_m^{\text{gate}}\) centered at the modality mean: \[
\mu_{\text{mai}} = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^M \text{mai}_j^{(t)}, \quad \kappa = 5.0 + 10.0 \cdot \min\left(1.0, \frac{t}{30}\right)
\] \begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-mai-sigmoid-gate}{
\omega_m^{\text{gate}} = \sigma\left( \kappa \cdot (\text{mai}_m^{(t)} - \mu_{\text{mai}}) \right) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-\kappa (\text{mai}_m^{(t)} - \mu_{\text{mai}})\right)}
}\end{equation} Target weights proportional to the gated alignment are
normalized:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-mai-target-weights}{
\tilde{w}_m = \text{mai}_m^{(t)} \cdot \omega_m^{\text{gate}}, \quad w_m^{\text{target}} = \frac{\tilde{w}_m}{\sum_{j=1}^M \tilde{w}_j + 10^{-8}}
}\end{equation} Rather than an abrupt discontinuous step at a fixed
epoch threshold, the architecture transitions continuously from uniform
weighting \(\mathbf{w}_{\text{uniform}} = [1/M, \dots, 1/M]^\top\) to
the target weights via a continuous linear ramp schedule: \[
T_{\text{warm}} = 0.33 \times T_{\text{total}}, \quad \rho = \text{clip}\left( \frac{t - T_{\text{warm}}}{T_{\text{warm}}}, 0.0, 1.0 \right)
\] \begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-mai-warmup}{
\mathbf{w}^{(t)} = (1 - \rho) \mathbf{w}_{\text{uniform}} + \rho \mathbf{w}^{\text{target}}
}\end{equation} This guarantees that all modalities contribute equally
during initial manifold representation learning, activating dynamic
modality triage only once the shared consensus geometry has stabilized.

\section{Theoretical Properties and Diagnostic
Invariants}\label{theoretical-properties-and-diagnostic-invariants}

\subsection{Mechanistic Auditability via OLEE and Closed-Form
Counterfactuals}\label{mechanistic-auditability-via-olee-and-closed-form-counterfactuals}

Standard post-hoc explainability techniques (SHAP, LIME, Integrated
Gradients) construct local linear approximations around non-linear
neural networks. In high-dimensional biomedical regimes, these
approximations are non-unique, highly sensitive to background reference
choices, and vulnerable to adversarial gradient noise.

We resolve the ``transparency penalty'' at the architectural entrypoint
via the Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) constraint:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-olee-entrypoint}{
\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m), \quad \mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k
}\end{equation} All non-linear operations are strictly delegated
downstream to decoders (LEND) or latent normalizing flows (Flow-SiMR-V).
This structural information bottleneck establishes three exact
mathematical auditability guarantees:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Exact Feature Attribution \& Constant Input Gradient}: The
  latent score \(z_{i, l}\) for subject \(i\) on component \(l\) is
  strictly the linear dot product
  \(\sum_{j=1}^{p_m} X_{i, j} V_{j, l}\). Feature importance is exact,
  sample-specific, and requires zero heuristic approximation.
  Furthermore, the directional derivative of representations with
  respect to input observations is strictly constant:
  \begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-olee-constant-gradient}{
  \nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{V}_m
  }\end{equation} Because this input gradient is independent of the
  input \(\mathbf{X}_m\), the sensitivity profile is invariant across
  subjects, preventing gradient saturation, adversarial perturbations,
  and vanishing attribution artifacts that plague deep non-linear
  encoders.
\item
  \textbf{Global Variance Partitioning}: Because
  \(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k\), the projection is
  an isometry on \(\mathbb{R}^k\). The total explained variance
  decomposes additively across orthogonal axes:
  \(\text{Var}(\mathbf{Z}_m) = \sum_{l=1}^k \text{Var}(\mathbf{z}_{m, l})\).
\item
  \textbf{Closed-Form Minimum-Norm Counterfactuals}: Targeted
  counterfactual state transitions can be evaluated analytically in
  closed form without iterative gradient search or surrogate sampling,
  as established by the following theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Theorem: Closed-Form Minimum-Norm
Counterfactuals}\label{theorem-closed-form-minimum-norm-counterfactuals}

\textbf{Theorem:}\\
Let a subject have latent representation
\(\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{x} \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^k\) under the OLEE
basis \(\mathbf{V} \in \text{St}(k, p)\). To transition the subject to a
targeted counterfactual latent state
\(\mathbf{z}^* = \mathbf{z} + \Delta \mathbf{z}\), the unique
perturbation \(\delta \mathbf{x}^* \in \mathbb{R}^p\) that minimizes the
Euclidean intervention effort \(\|\delta \mathbf{x}\|_2^2\) while
satisfying
\((\mathbf{x} + \delta \mathbf{x}) \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{z}^*\) is given
analytically by:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-counterfactual-solution}{
\delta \mathbf{x}^* = \Delta \mathbf{z} \mathbf{V}^\top
}\end{equation}

\textbf{Proof:}\\
The counterfactual perturbation problem is formulated as the constrained
optimization:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-counterfactual-objective}{
\min_{\delta \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \frac{1}{2} \|\delta \mathbf{x}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \delta \mathbf{x} \mathbf{V} = \Delta \mathbf{z}
}\end{equation} Introducing Lagrange multipliers
\(\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \mathbb{R}^k\), the Lagrangian is: \[
\mathcal{L}(\delta \mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \frac{1}{2} \delta \mathbf{x} \delta \mathbf{x}^\top - (\delta \mathbf{x} \mathbf{V} - \Delta \mathbf{z}) \boldsymbol{\lambda}
\] Setting the gradient with respect to \(\delta \mathbf{x}\) to zero:
\[
\nabla_{\delta \mathbf{x}} \mathcal{L} = \delta \mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{V}^\top = \mathbf{0} \implies \delta \mathbf{x} = \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{V}^\top
\] Multiplying both sides on the right by \(\mathbf{V}\) and enforcing
the constraint: \[
\delta \mathbf{x} \mathbf{V} = \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} = \Delta \mathbf{z}
\] Because \(\mathbf{V}\) lies on the Stiefel manifold,
\(\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}_k\). Thus: \[
\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{I}_k = \Delta \mathbf{z} \implies \boldsymbol{\lambda} = \Delta \mathbf{z}^\top
\] Substituting \(\boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top = \Delta \mathbf{z}\) back
into the stationarity equation yields: \[
\delta \mathbf{x}^* = \Delta \mathbf{z} \mathbf{V}^\top
\] The minimum-norm perturbation is closed-form, unique, and requires no
iterative gradient search or surrogate sampling.

\subsection{Subspace vs.~Basis Axis
Disentanglement}\label{subspace-vs.-basis-axis-disentanglement}

A fundamental methodological challenge in representation learning is
separating \textbf{subspace quality} from \textbf{basis axis
interpretability}. Downstream evaluations frequently conflate these two
concepts:

\subsubsection{Theorem: Rotation Invariance of Linear
Probes}\label{theorem-rotation-invariance-of-linear-probes}

\textbf{Theorem:}\\
Let \(\mathbf{Z}_1 = \mathbf{X} \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\)
and let \(\mathbf{M} \in \text{GL}(k, \mathbb{R})\) be an arbitrary
invertible \(k \times k\) matrix defining an alternative basis
\(\mathbf{Z}_2 = \mathbf{X} \mathbf{V} \mathbf{M} = \mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M}\).
For any regression target \(\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}\):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  The Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) projection hat matrices are
  identical:
  \(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_2} \equiv \mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_1}\).
\item
  The fitted values and regression \(R^2\) are strictly invariant:
  \(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 \equiv \hat{\mathbf{y}}_1\) and
  \(R^2(\mathbf{Z}_2, \mathbf{y}) \equiv R^2(\mathbf{Z}_1, \mathbf{y})\).
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Proof:}\\
The OLS hat matrix projecting \(\mathbf{y}\) onto the column space of
\(\mathbf{Z}_2\) is:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-ols-hat-matrix}{
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_2} = \mathbf{Z}_2 \left( \mathbf{Z}_2^\top \mathbf{Z}_2 \right)^{-1} \mathbf{Z}_2^\top
}\end{equation} Substituting \(\mathbf{Z}_2 = \mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M}\):
\[
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_2} = (\mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M}) \left( (\mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M})^\top (\mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M}) \right)^{-1} (\mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M})^\top = \mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M} \left( \mathbf{M}^\top (\mathbf{Z}_1^\top \mathbf{Z}_1) \mathbf{M} \right)^{-1} \mathbf{M}^\top \mathbf{Z}_1^\top
\] Because \(\mathbf{M}\) is invertible,
\((\mathbf{A} \mathbf{B} \mathbf{C})^{-1} = \mathbf{C}^{-1} \mathbf{B}^{-1} \mathbf{A}^{-1}\):
\[
\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_2} = \mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M} \mathbf{M}^{-1} \left( \mathbf{Z}_1^\top \mathbf{Z}_1 \right)^{-1} (\mathbf{M}^\top)^{-1} \mathbf{M}^\top \mathbf{Z}_1^\top = \mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{I}_k \left( \mathbf{Z}_1^\top \mathbf{Z}_1 \right)^{-1} \mathbf{I}_k \mathbf{Z}_1^\top = \mathbf{Z}_1 \left( \mathbf{Z}_1^\top \mathbf{Z}_1 \right)^{-1} \mathbf{Z}_1^\top = \mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_1}
\] The predicted values are
\(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 = \mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_2} \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_1} \mathbf{y} = \hat{\mathbf{y}}_1\).
The regression coefficients simply absorb the transformation:
\(\boldsymbol{\beta}_2 = \mathbf{M}^{-1} \boldsymbol{\beta}_1\).

\subsubsection{Coordinate Sensitivity of Tree
Ensembles}\label{coordinate-sensitivity-of-tree-ensembles}

In sharp contrast to linear probes, decision trees in a Random Forest
recursively split data along coordinate-aligned hyperplanes:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-tree-hyperplane}{
\mathcal{H}_{l, \theta} = \{ \mathbf{z} \in \mathbb{R}^k \mid z_l \le \theta \}
}\end{equation} Under a generic rotation \(\mathbf{M}\), each coordinate
becomes an oblique linear combination
\(z_l' = \sum_{j=1}^k M_{jl} z_j\). To isolate rotated patterns,
axis-aligned trees require an exponential cascade of staircase splits,
degrading generalization performance.

\subsubsection{The Dual-Probe
Diagnostic}\label{the-dual-probe-diagnostic}

Consequently, downstream evaluations must deploy a dual-probe protocol:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Linear Probe (OLS / Logistic Regression)}: Quantifies
  \textbf{subspace recovery}. Performance is completely invariant to
  basis rotation (\(\Delta R^2 = 0.0000\)).
\item
  \textbf{Tree Probe (Random Forest)}: Quantifies \textbf{coordinate
  axis alignment, non-negativity, and sparsity}.
\end{enumerate}

A performance difference observed in tree probes accompanied by a tie in
linear probes localizes the effect specifically to coordinate axis
disentanglement rather than subspace capture.

\subsection{Theoretical Bounds: The 1/M
Law}\label{theoretical-bounds-the-1m-law}

Let each modality observation \(\mathbf{X}_m\) be generated by a shared
true manifold embedded in additive Gaussian noise with noise variance
\(\sigma^2\) and cross-modal correlation \(\rho\). Aggregating
observations across \(M\) modalities yields the consensus estimator
variance:
\begin{equation}\protect\phantomsection\label{eq-one-over-m-law}{
\text{Var}(\mathbf{U}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{M} (1 - \rho) + \rho \sigma^2
}\end{equation} As \(M \to \infty\), the independent noise term
\(\frac{\sigma^2}{M}(1-\rho)\) vanishes. Consequently, the irreducible
error floor is strictly bounded by \(\rho \sigma^2\). While expanding
\(M\) mitigates independent noise, it cannot resolve correlated
cross-modal artifacts, necessitating the LOO topology and Spectral
Triage to actively suppress correlated noise components.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Statistical Determination of Intrinsic
Dimensionality
(\(k\))}{Statistical Determination of Intrinsic Dimensionality (k)}}\label{statistical-determination-of-intrinsic-dimensionality-k}

We implement three statistical strategies to establish the optimal
latent dimensionality \(k\):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Information Criteria (BIC):} Evaluates the Bayesian
  Information Criterion to penalize excessive model parameterization.
\item
  \textbf{Spectral Truncation (Tracy-Widom):} Computes the statistical
  significance of singular values against the null hypothesis of purely
  unstructured noise.
\item
  \textbf{V-fold Cross-Validation:} Selects \(k\) by applying the
  One-Standard-Error Rule to out-of-sample prediction performance.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Multi-Modal Data Regimes and Replication
Structure}\label{multi-modal-data-regimes-and-replication-structure}

To rigorously evaluate multi-modal architectures across distinct
inductive biases and biomedical domains, we establish a comprehensive
benchmark protocol across \textbf{9 multi-modal data regimes} (6
controlled synthetic manifolds with known ground-truth \(\mathbf{U}^*\)
and \(\mathbf{V}^*\), plus 3 public clinical/multi-omics cohorts)
evaluated across \(N_{\text{seeds}} = 3\) independent random seeds
(\([42, 43, 44]\)) and 9 model architectures (\(243\) total model
evaluations, with \texttt{private\_scale\ =\ 0.5},
\texttt{consolidate\ =\ False}, and recommended \texttt{"pca"}
initialization), alongside a dedicated 10-seed 4-view neuroimaging
mediation benchmark on ADNI-MCI (\(N = 1,084\)). Following Demšar
(2006), global statistical inference is conducted on the \(B = 9\)
independent dataset blocks (\texttt{build\_ranking\_blocks} in
\texttt{src/pysimlr/benchmarks/deep\_ranking.py}), averaging replicates
within each dataset before ranking so repeated seeds never inflate
degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Controlled Synthetic Generative
Regimes}\label{controlled-synthetic-generative-regimes}

To test distinct, non-overlapping hypotheses about multi-view recovery
\((\mathbf{U}^*, \mathbf{V}^*)\) under \texttt{private\_scale\ =\ 0.5},
\texttt{synthetic\_cases.py} defines six synthetic generative regimes:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  \textbf{Clean Linear Multi-View Manifold (\texttt{Linear},
  \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[100, 80, 60]\)):}
  \[\mathbf{X}_m = \mathbf{U}^* \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\]
  where shared latent scales \([1.2, 1.0, 0.85]\) exceed view-private
  background (\(\sigma_{\text{priv}} = 0.5\)) and Gaussian noise
  (\(\sigma = 0.5\)), with equal outcome weights
  \(\mathbf{w} = [1, 1, 1]^\top\). Tests baseline recovery where linear
  and cross-view models should perform at parity
  (\(R^2 \approx 0.976\text{–}0.990\)).
\item
  \textbf{Polynomial Latent Warping (\texttt{Polynomial},
  \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[120, 80, 50]\)):}
  \[\mathbf{X}_m = \psi_m^{\text{poly}}(\mathbf{U}^*) \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\]
  where \(\psi_m^{\text{poly}}(\mathbf{U}^*)\) applies non-cancelling
  quadratic, cross-product, and cubic distortions in \(\mathbb{R}^k\)
  prior to linear projection \(\mathbf{V}_m^\top\). Because same-sign
  quadratic warpings do not cancel when views are averaged linearly,
  axis-sensitive nonlinear probes on \(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\)
  (\texttt{NED} \(0.961\), \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} \(0.954\), \texttt{LEND}
  \(0.954\)) outperform linear OLS on \(\mathbf{U}\)
  (\(0.870\text{–}0.874\)).
\item
  \textbf{Sinusoidal Periodic Warping (\texttt{Sine},
  \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[120, 80, 50]\)):}
  \[\mathbf{X}_m = \psi_m^{\text{sin}}(\mathbf{U}^*) \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\]
  where \(\psi_m^{\text{sin}}(\mathbf{U}^*)\) combines linear
  coordinates with non-cancelling trigonometric oscillations (\(\sin\)
  and \(\cos\) couplings). Tests recovery under periodic manifold
  curvature.
\item
  \textbf{Asymmetric Shared-Plus-Private Manifold
  (\texttt{Shared+Private},
  \(N=1,000, K_{\text{sh}}=3, K_{\text{pr}}=2, p=[160, 60, 30]\)):}
  \[\mathbf{X}_m = \mathbf{U}_{\text{shared}}^* \mathbf{V}_{m, \text{sh}}^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m, \text{pr}}^* \mathbf{V}_{m, \text{pr}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\]
  where shared scales decay (\([1.0, 0.625, 0.455]\)) while the largest
  view (\(p_1=160\)) carries heightened view-private variance
  (\(\sigma_{\text{priv},1} = 0.80\)). Single-matrix concatenation
  (\texttt{PCA} \(R^2 = 0.639\), \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} \(R^2 = 0.629\))
  is pulled toward View 1's private variance, whereas cross-view
  consensus in \texttt{LEND} recovers all shared factors
  (\(R^2 = 0.949\), \(CMC = 0.896\)) and \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} achieves
  \(R^2 = 0.908\) on its first-layer linear probe.
\item
  \textbf{Non-Negative Disjoint Parts --- Dominant Signal
  (\texttt{NonnegParts}, \(N=400, K=3, p=[120, 45, 30]\)):} Ground-truth
  loading matrices \(\mathbf{V}_m^* \ge \mathbf{0}\) have strictly
  disjoint column supports across the \(K=3\) components, and the
  outcome tracks the dominant shared component (\(u_1^*\)).
  Bound-constrained \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} recovers the true sparse
  support with Jaccard score \textbf{\(0.942\)} (followed by
  \texttt{LEND} at \textbf{\(0.899\)} and \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} at
  \textbf{\(0.883\)}), whereas unconstrained \texttt{PCA} scores at the
  random-partition chance floor (\textbf{\(0.333\)}).
\item
  \textbf{Non-Negative Disjoint Parts --- Weak Signal
  (\texttt{NonnegPartsWeak}, \(N=400, K=3, p=[120, 45, 30]\)):}
  Identical disjoint non-negative basis \(\mathbf{V}_m^*\), but the
  outcome is driven by the weakest shared component (\(u_3^*\)). Because
  single-matrix concatenation absorbs view-private noise into its
  leading components, \texttt{PCA} drops to \(R^2 = 0.522\) and
  \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} to \(0.570\), whereas \texttt{LEND}
  (\(R^2 = 0.732\)), \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} (\(R^2 = 0.715\)), and
  \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS} (\(R^2 = 0.706\)) isolate the weak shared
  component via cross-view alignment and non-negativity.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Multi-Objective Pareto Frontier
Analysis}\label{multi-objective-pareto-frontier-analysis}

Architectural selection across clinical and high-dimensional biomedical
regimes cannot be reduced to a single 1D predictive metric. Models must
be evaluated across four complementary operational axes:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Downstream Predictive Capacity (\(Y\))}: Cross-validated
  out-of-sample \(R^2\) or diagnostic classification accuracy.
\item
  \textbf{Ground-Truth Latent Discovery (\(CMC\) on \(\mathbf{U}\))}:
  Canonical Mean Correlation with the true generative latent coordinates
  \(\mathbf{S}\).
\item
  \textbf{Sparse Feature Support Recovery (\(F_1\) on \(\mathbf{V}\))}:
  Jaccard/F1 overlap with true localized anatomical or molecular feature
  blocks, alongside zero signed crosstalk
  (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)).
\item
  \textbf{Computational Throughput (\(t\))}: Wall-clock execution time
  per fit.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why Predicting Clinical Dementia Directly from Brain Scans
Hits a
Wall}\label{why-predicting-clinical-dementia-directly-from-brain-scans-hits-a-wall}

In Alzheimer's disease and Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), a primary
clinical goal is predicting how severely a patient's day-to-day
functioning is impaired, measured by the \textbf{Clinical Dementia
Rating Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB)}---a score reflecting whether a person can
remember appointments, manage household finances, navigate their
neighborhood, and care for themselves.

When researchers train machine learning models to predict CDR-SB
\textbf{directly from structural brain MRI alone}, accuracy is
consistently disappointing ({Jack et al.} 2008; {Weiner et al.} 2017).
Across all 382 cortical volume and thickness measurements in our
ADNI-MCI cohort (\(N = 1,084\)), predicting CDR-SB directly from brain
anatomy reaches an out-of-sample ceiling of only \textbf{4.1\% explained
variance}:
\[R^2 = 0.0411 \pm 0.0248 \quad (\text{holdout test } R^2 = 4.02\%, \quad \rho = 0.2112, \quad p = 1.2 \times 10^{-4})\]

The reason is simple and biological: \textbf{a shrinking hippocampus
does not directly stop a patient from balancing a checkbook}. Instead,
Alzheimer's pathology acts in two sequential steps:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Brain Atrophy \(\to\) Cognitive Loss (Path \(a\)):} Physical
  tissue loss (cortical thinning and volume loss) damages core mental
  abilities measured in clinic---such as word recall, attention, and
  spatial orientation.
\item
  \textbf{Cognitive Loss \(\to\) Daily Functional Disability (Path
  \(b\)):} Once those mental abilities decline, the patient loses
  independence in daily life, driving up their CDR-SB score.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Testing the Two-Step Biological Pathway (Baron--Kenny
Mediation)}\label{testing-the-two-step-biological-pathway-baronkenny-mediation}

We tested this two-step pathway across \(N = 1,084\) MCI participants
(\(70/30\) train/test split, \(N_{\text{test}} = 326\)) using classical
Baron--Kenny mediation and Sobel testing:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Step 1 --- Brain Structure Strongly Predicts Cognitive Test
  Scores (Path \(a\)):}
  \[\alpha = 1.000 \pm 0.081, \quad t = 12.31, \quad p < 10^{-25}, \quad R^2 = 26.13\%, \quad \rho = 0.5335\]
  Multi-view brain MRI explains over \textbf{26\%} of the variance in
  domain cognitive performance.
\item
  \textbf{Step 2 --- Cognitive Test Scores Directly Drive Daily
  Disability (Path \(b\)):}
  \[\beta = 0.013 \pm 0.003, \quad t = 4.69, \quad p < 10^{-15}\]
\item
  \textbf{Direct Bypass Path Vanishes (Path \(c'\)):}
  \[\gamma' = 0.002, \quad t = 0.88, \quad p = 0.38 \quad (\text{Not Significant})\]
  Once cognitive performance is accounted for, the direct link from
  brain MRI to CDR-SB drops to zero.
\item
  \textbf{Share of the Effect Explained by Mediation:}
  \[PM = \frac{\alpha \beta}{\alpha \beta + \gamma'} = \mathbf{84.5\% - 86.4\%} \quad (\text{Sobel } z = 4.38, \; p < 10^{-15})\]
\end{enumerate}

In plain terms: \textbf{roughly 85\% of the effect of brain atrophy on
daily dementia severity flows through cognitive performance}. Ignoring
this intermediate step is why direct MRI-to-CDR-SB models fail.

\chapter{Discussion}\label{discussion}

Integrating heterogeneous, high-dimensional observations remains a
fundamental task in computational anatomy, neurodegenerative disease
modeling, and translational molecular oncology ({Stone et al.} 2020;
Avants et al. 2021). As diagnostic and clinical trial cohorts
incorporate multi-sequence structural and diffusion MRI, molecular
positron emission tomography, biofluid assays, and high-throughput
transcriptomics ({Weiner et al.} 2017; {Jack et al.} 2008; {Marek et
al.} 2011), representation learning methods must balance the capacity to
model non-linear manifold geometry against the legal, ethical, and
scientific necessity for verifiable physical biomarker attribution
(Holmberg et al. 2022). In neurotrauma, blast-induced shockwaves produce
diffuse microstructural shearing across white matter tracts that demands
sparse, signed decomposition without cancellation artifacts ({Stone et
al.} 2024). In cancer genomics, resolving functional oncogenic pathways
across somatic copy-number alterations, mRNA expression, and targeted
protein arrays in breast carcinoma (BRCA) (The Cancer Genome Atlas
Network 2012; Cancer Discovery 2016) and clear cell renal cell carcinoma
(KIRC) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network 2013) requires
multi-modal alignment that preserves exact gene-level attribution.

In this work, we formulated multi-view representation learning as a
constrained variational problem governed by the Only Linear Encoder
Entrypoint (OLEE) contract, coupling an initial linear Stiefel
projection with downstream Riemannian optimizers, non-linear decoders,
and volume-preserving normalizing flows. Through evaluation across 9
distinct data regimes (3 real clinical/genomic cohorts and 6 synthetic
manifolds across 3 random seeds, totaling 243 model evaluations),
alongside dedicated investigations of 4-view cognitive mediation in
Alzheimer's disease (\(N=1,084\)), we characterize the operational
boundaries of multi-modal integration and establish empirical guidelines
for biomedical deployment.

\section{Non-Parametric Architectural Ranking and Empirical
Synthesis}\label{non-parametric-architectural-ranking-and-empirical-synthesis}

Evaluating multi-modal representations requires non-parametric
statistical inference that respects dataset-level block structure rather
than pooling across replicate seeds (Demšar 2006). Across the \(B = 9\)
independent dataset blocks and \(k = 9\) evaluated architectures, the
non-parametric Friedman test confirms highly significant performance
differences (\(\chi_F^2 = 32.47, p = 7.68 \times 10^{-5}\)),
corroborated by the Iman--Davenport extension
(\(F_F = 6.57, p = 3.13 \times 10^{-6}\),
\(\text{df}_1 = 8, \text{df}_2 = 64\)) (Iman and Davenport 1980). With
nine models and nine dataset blocks, the two-tailed Nemenyi Critical
Difference at \(\alpha = 0.05\) is \(CD = 4.004\) (Nemenyi 1963), and
the median between-model spread-to-noise ratio is \(6.15\times\).

The empirical rankings establish four clear architectural tiers:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Box-Constrained Riemannian \& Deep Hybrid Frontier
  (\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB}, \texttt{LEND}):} \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB}
  achieves the best overall predictive rank (\(3.28\), mean predictive
  score \(0.795 \pm 0.201\), linear probe \(0.830\)) and the highest
  sparse support recovery across the suite
  (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.942\)) with near-zero per-view Stiefel
  frame defect (\(D = 2 \times 10^{-5}\)). \texttt{LEND} achieves rank
  \(3.56\) and leads the benchmark in mean predictive accuracy
  (\(0.830 \pm 0.189\)), strictly linear probe (\(0.836\)), axis-aligned
  Random Forest probe (\(0.830\)), and latent coordinate recovery
  (\(CMC = 0.907\)), excelling on \texttt{Shared+Private}
  (\(R^2 = 0.949\) vs \(0.639\) for \texttt{PCA}) and
  \texttt{NonnegPartsWeak} (\(R^2 = 0.732\) vs \(0.522\) for
  \texttt{PCA}).
\item
  \textbf{Ultra-Fast Turnkey SVD \& Dense Linear Tier
  (\texttt{NSAFlow-PCA}, \texttt{PCA}):} \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} achieves
  rank \(3.61\) (\(0.782 \pm 0.200\), \(CMC = 0.834\)) in \(0.029\)
  seconds per fit---matching \texttt{PCA} (rank \(3.78\),
  \(0.774 \pm 0.213\), \(0.024\text{s}\)) in speed while outperforming
  \texttt{PCA} on Diabetes (\(R^2 = 0.382\) vs \(0.350\)) and TCGA-BRCA
  Multi-Omics (\(92.7\%\) vs \(91.9\%\)) and enforcing \(22.4\%\)
  disjoint sparsity with zero signed crosstalk
  (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)).
\item
  \textbf{First-Order, Quasi-Newton \& Invertible Flow Tier
  (\texttt{SiMLR}, \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS}, \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V}):}
  Classical \texttt{SiMLR} (rank \(4.17\), \(0.785 \pm 0.205\),
  \(0.363\text{s}\)) and quasi-Newton \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS} (rank
  \(4.56\), \(0.797 \pm 0.193\), \(0.175\text{s}\),
  \(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.766\)) provide fast cross-view Stiefel
  convergence. \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} (rank \(6.50\) on \(\mathbf{U}\))
  achieves the second-highest strictly linear \(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\)
  probe across the entire benchmark (\(0.834\), including \(0.908\) on
  \texttt{Shared+Private} and \(0.744\) on \texttt{NonnegPartsWeak})
  alongside \(0.883\) sparse support recovery and exact analytical
  invertibility.
\item
  \textbf{Dominated Unconstrained Deep Tier (\texttt{NED},
  \texttt{NEDPP}):} Fully non-linear autoencoders (\texttt{NED}, rank
  \(7.72\), \(0.618 \pm 0.227\); \texttt{NEDPP}, rank \(7.83\),
  \(0.633 \pm 0.249\)) rank last across the benchmark and are
  significantly outperformed by every OLEE-constrained model under
  Holm-Bonferroni corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests
  (\(p_{\text{Holm}} \le 0.018\)).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Multi-Objective Pareto Frontier
Analysis}\label{multi-objective-pareto-frontier-analysis-1}

Architecture selection in biomedical discovery cannot be guided by
predictive accuracy alone. We evaluate models across four complementary
operational criteria:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Downstream Predictive Capacity (\(Y\)) {[}Maximize{]}:}
  Evaluated via cross-validated \(R^2\) or classification accuracy
  across all 9 benchmark tasks.
\item
  \textbf{Ground-Truth Latent Discovery (\(CMC\) on \(\mathbf{U}\))
  {[}Maximize{]}:} Evaluated via Canonical Mean Correlation between
  estimated consensus coordinates \(\mathbf{U}\) and true generative
  latents \(\mathbf{S}\) across the 6 synthetic regimes.
\item
  \textbf{Sparse Support Localization \& Invariants
  (\(\text{Support } F_1, D, \text{Crosstalk}\)):} Evaluated via
  Jaccard/F1 support overlap on sparse non-negative regimes,
  trace-normalized Stiefel Frame Defect \(D\), and signed lobe crosstalk
  \(\|\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^-\|_1 = 0.0000\).
\item
  \textbf{Computational Efficiency (\(t\)) {[}Minimize{]}:} Evaluated
  via wall-clock fit time in seconds.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Structure of the Pareto Non-Dominated
Set}\label{structure-of-the-pareto-non-dominated-set}

The empirical results define five distinct operational regimes on the
Pareto frontier:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{The Predictive and Consensus Discovery Frontier
  (\texttt{LEND}):} Achieving the highest predictive accuracy
  (\(0.830\)), highest linear probe (\(0.836\)), and highest latent
  coordinate recovery (\(CMC = 0.907\)), \texttt{LEND} combines a linear
  Stiefel encoder with a non-linear MLP decoder to isolate shared
  multi-view structure even when individual views carry heavy private
  noise (\texttt{Shared+Private} \(R^2 = 0.949\)).
\item
  \textbf{The Sparse Support Localization Frontier
  (\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB}):} Achieving the best overall Friedman rank
  (\(3.28\)) and highest sparse support recovery (\(F_1 = 0.942\)),
  \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} enforces strict non-negative box constraints
  alongside Stiefel consolidation (\texttt{NonnegPartsWeak}
  \(R^2 = 0.715\) vs \(0.522\) for \texttt{PCA}).
\item
  \textbf{The Ultra-Fast Turnkey Sparse Frontier
  (\texttt{NSAFlow-PCA}):} Executing in \(0.029\text{s}\) with
  \(22.4\%\) disjoint sparsity, \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} matches
  \texttt{PCA} (\(0.024\text{s}\)) in speed while outperforming it in
  predictive accuracy (\(0.782\) vs \(0.774\)) and providing signed,
  non-cancelling feature lobes.
\item
  \textbf{The Fast Quasi-Newton Frontier (\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS}):}
  Executing in \(0.175\text{s}\) (\(2\times\) faster than first-order
  \texttt{SiMLR}), \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGS} delivers \(0.797\) predictive
  accuracy and \(0.766\) support recovery with Per-view frame defect
  \(D = 4.7 \times 10^{-4}\).
\item
  \textbf{The Invertible Diffeomorphic Frontier (\texttt{Flow-SiMR-V}):}
  Combining a linear Stiefel entrypoint with bijective RealNVP coupling
  layers, \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} achieves \(0.834\) on the strictly linear
  \(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\) probe and \(0.883\) support recovery while
  supporting exact analytical inversion for geodesic trajectory mapping.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) and Eliminating the
Transparency
Penalty}\label{the-only-linear-encoder-entrypoint-olee-and-eliminating-the-transparency-penalty}

A central design requirement in medical imaging, oncology biomarker
discovery, and clinical trials is that representation projections remain
verifiable. In standard deep multi-view networks, deeply nested
activation functions
(\(\mathbf{Z} = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_2 \sigma(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{b}_1) + \mathbf{b}_2)\))
entangle features, precluding direct inspection of how physical
measurements relate to latent scores (Rudin 2019). In clinical practice,
this incurs an unacceptable \textbf{transparency penalty}: if a latent
score stratifies a patient into an aggressive treatment arm,
biostatisticians and clinicians must verify whether that score is driven
by true anatomical atrophy or batch artifacts (Holmberg et al. 2022).

Post-hoc explainers (such as SHAP and LIME) attempt to circumvent this
by querying local surrogate models on randomly perturbed inputs (Ribeiro
et al. 2016; Lundberg and Lee 2017). In anatomical and physiological
cohorts, post-hoc methods suffer from an out-of-distribution sampling
defect: perturbing features independently creates synthetic points that
violate physiological covariance (e.g., pairing impossible cortical
thickness values or mutually exclusive transcript profiles). This
instability can introduce false-positive biomarker attributions
(Holmberg et al. 2022; Radulovic 2023).

Deep SiMLR enforces verifiable attribution by restricting the entrypoint
of each modality to the \textbf{Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE)}
contract: \[
\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \text{subject to } \mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m) = \{\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}_k\}
\] This architectural contract yields three mathematical and clinical
guarantees:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Globally Constant Attribution Gradients:} The marginal
  contribution of feature \(j\) to latent component \(k\) is identically
  constant across the input domain: \[
  \nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \frac{\partial Z_{m, ik}}{\partial X_{m, ij}} = V_{m, jk}
  \] Feature loadings are exact, closed-form, and independent of
  heuristic perturbation parameters.
\item
  \textbf{Closed-Form Counterfactual Recourse:} Determining the minimal
  physical perturbation \(\delta \mathbf{x}_m\) required to produce a
  targeted therapeutic shift in latent coordinates
  \(\Delta \mathbf{z}_m\) reduces to an exact orthogonal projection: \[
  \delta \mathbf{x}_m^* = \Delta \mathbf{z}_m \mathbf{V}_m^\top
  \] This eliminates iterative gradient searches, non-convex local
  minima, and off-manifold sampling artifacts, connecting dimensionality
  reduction directly to actionable causal recourse frameworks (Pearl
  2009; Wachter et al. 2017).
\item
  \textbf{Signed Support Consolidation:} Decomposing loading matrices
  into positive and negative lobes
  (\(\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{V}^+ - \mathbf{V}^-\)) and enforcing disjoint
  support (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)) prevents
  positive and negative weights from cancelling destructively, ensuring
  that positive and negative coefficients identify distinct anatomical
  or biological processes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Why Cognitive Mediation Multiplies ADNI-MCI Prediction by Over
6-Fold}\label{why-cognitive-mediation-multiplies-adni-mci-prediction-by-over-6-fold}

The practical value of the OLEE contract is clearest in our study of
\(N = 1,084\) patients with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) from the
ADNI cohort ({Weiner et al.} 2017; {Jack et al.} 2008). Clinicians
evaluate functional dementia severity using the \textbf{Clinical
Dementia Rating -- Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB)}, which measures how well a
patient manages daily life (remembering events, handling household
tasks, staying oriented).

Integrating three structural brain MRI views (\(382\) regional volume
and cortical thickness measurements) with a fourth view of clinical
cognitive tests (\(p_4 = 13\)) reveals three clear findings:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Brain Scans Alone Cannot Predict Daily Disability
  (\(R^2 \approx 0.041\)):} Trying to predict CDR-SB directly from the
  382 brain MRI features hits a hard ceiling of only \textbf{4.1\%
  explained variance} (\texttt{Direct-Anatomy\ {[}SiMLR{]}}:
  \(R^2 = 0.0411 \pm 0.0248, \rho = 0.2203\)), while raw Ridge
  regression fails completely (\(R^2 = -0.1810\)). Biologically, tissue
  loss in the cortex and hippocampus does not directly cause a patient
  to struggle with daily errands; instead, brain atrophy first erodes
  specific mental abilities (memory recall, executive attention), and it
  is that loss of mental ability that impairs daily functioning.
\item
  \textbf{Modeling the Two-Step Pathway Multiplies Accuracy Over 6-Fold
  (\(R^2 \approx 0.265\)):} When our 4-view models align structural
  brain MRI with the cognitive test battery along the natural pathway
  (\(\text{Brain MRI} \to \text{Cognition} \to \text{CDR-SB}\)),
  out-of-sample accuracy jumps more than six-fold:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    \texttt{LEND-SiMR\ +\ MAI\ {[}Mediated{]}}:
    \(R^2 = 0.2648 \pm 0.0332\) (\(6.44\times\) higher than direct MRI,
    \(p = 9.77 \times 10^{-4}\))
  \item
    \texttt{SiMLR\ {[}Mediated{]}}: \(R^2 = 0.2443 \pm 0.0534\)
    (\(5.94\times\) higher, solved in \(0.86\text{s}\))
  \item
    \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V\ +\ MAI\ {[}Mediated{]}}:
    \(R^2 = 0.2290 \pm 0.0696\) (\(5.57\times\) higher) Formal
    Baron--Kenny mediation confirms that \textbf{84.5\% to 86.4\% of the
    total effect of brain atrophy on CDR-SB is indirect}, traveling
    through cognitive performance (\(p < 10^{-15}\)).
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Unconstrained Deep Networks Collapse on High-Dimensional Brain
  Data:} Even with all four views available, unconstrained deep
  autoencoders fail (\texttt{NED-SiMR\ +\ MAI}:
  \(R^2 = 0.1127 \pm 0.0578\); \texttt{NED-Shared-Private}:
  \(R^2 = 0.0840\)). Without the orthogonal first-layer Stiefel
  constraint (\(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}\)),
  multi-layer perceptrons overfit noise across the 382 anatomical
  features and miss the shared brain-to-cognition axis.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Normalizing Flows: Invertibility and Geodesic
Trajectories}\label{normalizing-flows-invertibility-and-geodesic-trajectories}

The performance of \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V} arises from combining linear
Stiefel filtering with bijective affine coupling layers:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Information Preservation:} Unlike standard autoencoders that
  compress data through lossy bottlenecks with positive reconstruction
  error (\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} > 0\)), normalizing flows are
  strictly bijective with unit or triangular Jacobian determinants (Dinh
  et al. 2016; Rezende and Mohamed 2015). Forward and inverse mappings
  are analytically exact.
\item
  \textbf{Prior Regularization:} Normalizing flows maximize exact
  log-likelihood under a standard multivariate Gaussian prior
  \(\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})\), acting as a continuous
  density regularizer that prevents coordinate collapse.
\item
  \textbf{Stiefel Dimensionality Reduction:} Prepending the linear
  projection \(\mathbf{V}_m\) compresses ambient observations to the
  intrinsic \(k\)-dimensional latent subspace before coupling layers
  operate. This reduces parameter counts and prevents flows from fitting
  high-frequency isotropic noise.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Geodesic Latent
Trajectories}\label{geodesic-latent-trajectories}

Because normalizing flows define a smooth diffeomorphism between the
input subspace and the Gaussian latent space, \texttt{Flow-SiMR-V}
enables continuous geodesic path interpolation. For a patient presenting
with high disease risk at coordinate \(\mathbf{z}_{\text{start}}\), the
linear trajectory toward a healthy reference
\(\mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}\): \[
\mathbf{z}(t) = (1 - t) \mathbf{z}_{\text{start}} + t \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}, \quad t \in [0, 1]
\] can be inverted through the flow and projected into observable
feature space: \[
\mathbf{x}_m(t) = \text{Flow}_{\psi_m}^{-1}(\mathbf{z}(t)) \mathbf{V}_m^\top
\] This traces a continuous, physically grounded trajectory of biomarker
changes across disease stages.

\section{Clinical Translation Across Cohorts and Translational
Pathology}\label{clinical-translation-across-cohorts-and-translational-pathology}

The modular Deep SiMLR framework addresses distinct clinical and
biological challenges across the real-world clinical cohorts,
neurodegenerative registries, neurotrauma models, and translational
oncology:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Cleveland Heart Disease (Cardiovascular Diagnostic
  Profiling):} Integrating resting clinical vitals (\(p_1 = 7\)) and
  stress electrocardiography/laboratory measures (\(p_2 = 6\)) across
  \(N = 297\) patients for binary coronary artery disease diagnosis
  (\(83.7\%\) test accuracy for \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} and \texttt{PCA},
  \(84.8\%\) linear probe). The OLEE matrix \(\mathbf{V}\) directly
  identifies exercise-induced ST depression (\texttt{oldpeak}), maximum
  heart rate (\texttt{thalach}), and chest pain type (\texttt{cp}) as
  the dominant orthogonal axes.
\item
  \textbf{Diabetes Progression (Endocrine Risk Trajectories):} Modeling
  quantitative 1-year disease progression from anthropometric baselines
  (\(p_1 = 5\)) and serum lipid biomarkers (\(p_2 = 5\)) across
  \(N = 442\) patients. The linear entrypoint attributes progression
  directly to body mass index (BMI) and serum triglycerides (S5), with
  \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} (\(R^2 = 0.382\)) and \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB}
  (linear probe \(R^2 = 0.396\)) outperforming dense \texttt{PCA}
  (\(0.350\)).
\item
  \textbf{Multiple Features (\texttt{mfeat}, High-Cardinality Multi-View
  Benchmarking):} Integrating 4 core geometric/transform views
  (\(p = [76, 64, 6, 47]\)) in the initialization benchmark and all 6
  views (\(p = [76, 216, 64, 240, 47, 6]\)) in the full \texttt{mfeat6}
  benchmark across \(N = 2000\) samples, establishing that
  OLEE-constrained architectures effectively discover common consensus
  without view-dominance collapse.
\item
  \textbf{ADNI Cognitive Mediation (Neurodegenerative Progression):} As
  demonstrated in the 4-view ADNI-MCI cohort (\(N = 1,084\)), the OLEE
  contract enables the two-step brain-to-cognition-to-function pathway
  that unlocks a \textgreater6-fold accuracy gain
  (\(R^2 \approx 0.265\)) while insulating the model from the
  representation collapse observed in unconstrained deep networks.
\item
  \textbf{Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI):} In
  longitudinal Parkinson's disease cohorts ({Marek et al.} 2011),
  clinical trials require monitoring early dopaminergic denervation
  using \(^{123}\text{I}\)-ioflupane SPECT (DaTscan) striatal binding
  ratios, CSF \(\alpha\)-synuclein seed amplification assays, and motor
  UPDRS-III examinations. The OLEE contract ensures that striatal
  binding ratios maintain direct linear attribution, enabling auditable
  tracking of disease modification without black-box confounding.
\item
  \textbf{Blast Neurotrauma and Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI):} Low-level
  blast shockwave exposure induces diffuse axonal injury (DAI)
  characterized by non-local shear strains across white matter tracts
  ({Stone et al.} 2024). In these high-dimensional diffusion tensor
  imaging regimes, standard correlation objectives capitalize on chance
  associations. \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} and \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB} enforce
  disjoint, non-negative support
  (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)), isolating distinct
  anatomical tract disruptions without destructive crosstalk
  cancellation.
\item
  \textbf{Translational Oncology (TCGA-BRCA \& TCGA-KIRC):} Across
  \(N = 822\) primary breast invasive carcinoma tumors in the primary
  benchmark (\texttt{rna} \(p_1=200\), \texttt{cnv} \(p_2=150\),
  \texttt{rppa} \(p_3=150\)) and \(N = 1,010\) tumors in the full-cohort
  audit (The Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; Cancer Discovery 2016),
  \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} (\(92.7\%\)) and \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB}
  (\(93.0\%\) linear probe) isolate coordinated copy-number alterations,
  transcriptomic drivers (ESR1, GATA3, PGR), and targeted proteomics
  while preserving exact feature weights. In clear cell renal cell
  carcinoma (KIRC) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network 2013),
  multi-omics integration aligns chromosomal 3p loss with downstream
  VHL-HIF1A angiogenic pathway activation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodological Considerations in Biomedical
Cohorts}\label{methodological-considerations-in-biomedical-cohorts}

Our findings highlight three methodological principles for multi-modal
analysis in computational anatomy and clinical cohorts:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Preprocessing Sensitivity (Centering vs.~Standardization):} In
  clinical neuroimaging (e.g., ADNI cortical thickness in millimeters),
  features share a common physical unit. Standardizing columns to unit
  variance (\(z\)-scoring) scales low-variance noise regions equally
  with high-variance atrophy hubs, which can alter or reverse downstream
  findings. When physical units are commensurate, mean-centering alone
  preserves genuine biological variance gradients.
\item
  \textbf{Subspace Recovery vs.~Axis Localization:} An orthogonal
  rotation \(\mathbf{M} \in O(k)\) alters the orientation of basis axes
  without changing the linear subspace
  (\(\text{span}(\mathbf{X} \mathbf{V} \mathbf{M}) = \text{span}(\mathbf{X} \mathbf{V})\)).
  While linear prediction depends only on the subspace, localized
  biomarker discovery requires aligned, sparse, and non-negative
  axes---as demonstrated by the gap between \texttt{PCA}
  (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.333\)) and \texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB}
  (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.942\)) on \texttt{NonnegPartsWeak}.
\item
  \textbf{Dataset-Level Block Inference:} Ranking multi-modal
  architectures must aggregate across replicate seeds within each
  dataset block before computing Friedman and Nemenyi statistics (Demšar
  2006), avoiding artificial inflation of sample size from repeated
  random initializations.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}\label{limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{First-Layer Linear Constraint:} Restricting the input layer to
  an orthogonal Stiefel projection limits the model's ability to capture
  high-order intra-modality non-linear feature interactions directly
  from raw inputs. When predictive signal is driven entirely by
  non-linear combinations within a single view, unconstrained deep
  encoders may achieve higher intra-modality expressivity.
\item
  \textbf{Latent Dimension Scaling:} Exact Stiefel retractions and polar
  factorizations scale cubically with latent rank
  (\(\mathcal{O}(k^3)\)). While efficient for typical biomedical latent
  spaces (\(k \le 64\)), scaling to massive latent spaces (\(k > 500\))
  requires block-Stiefel or stochastic retraction methods.
\item
  \textbf{Number of Benchmark Regimes:} Block-level Nemenyi inference
  across \(B = 9\) datasets and \(k = 9\) models yields a conservative
  critical difference (\(CD = 4.004\)), requiring paired Wilcoxon and
  exact sign-flip permutation tests across the 27 paired runs to resolve
  finer pairwise differences.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}\label{conclusion}

Deep SiMLR establishes a modular framework for multi-modal
representation learning governed by the Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint
(OLEE) contract. By decoupling linear feature attribution from
downstream non-linear coordinate modeling, the architecture eliminates
the clinical transparency penalty
(\(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{V}_m\)) and enables
closed-form counterfactual recourse
(\(\delta \mathbf{x}_m^* = \Delta \mathbf{z}_m^* \mathbf{V}_m^\top\))
while capturing complex manifold structure.

Across 243 benchmark evaluations spanning 3 real clinical/genomic
cohorts and 6 synthetic manifolds, box-constrained Riemannian
optimization (\texttt{SiMLR-LBFGSB}, rank \(3.28\), support
\(F_1 = 0.942\)) and linear-encoder/non-linear-decoder hybrids
(\texttt{LEND}, rank \(3.56\), predictive \(0.830\), \(CMC = 0.907\))
lead overall performance, while \texttt{NSAFlow-PCA} (rank \(3.61\),
\(0.029\text{s}\)) matches dense \texttt{PCA} in speed while enforcing
\(22.4\%\) disjoint sparsity with zero crosstalk. Every OLEE-constrained
architecture significantly outperforms unconstrained deep autoencoders
(\texttt{NED}, \texttt{NEDPP}, \(p_{\text{Holm}} \le 0.018\)). In
longitudinal neurodegeneration (ADNI-MCI, \(N=1,084\)), the OLEE
contract unlocks a \textgreater6-fold cognitive mediation gain on
clinical dementia severity (\(R^2 \approx 0.2648, p < 10^{-15}\)) while
preventing the overfitting collapse that afflicts unconstrained deep
networks.

\section{Mathematical Derivations}\label{mathematical-derivations}

\subsection{Riemannian Updates}\label{riemannian-updates}

We implement Riemannian updates on the Stiefel manifold via projection
onto the tangent space \(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbf{V}} \mathbb{V}_{k,n}\). We
compute the Riemannian gradient \(\text{grad} \mathcal{J}(\mathbf{V})\)
from the Euclidean gradient \(\nabla \mathcal{J}(\mathbf{V})\):
\[ \text{grad} \mathcal{J}(\mathbf{V}) = \nabla \mathcal{J}(\mathbf{V}) - \mathbf{V} \text{sym}(\mathbf{V}^\top \nabla \mathcal{J}(\mathbf{V})) \]

The orthogonal penalty
\(\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{1}{4} \| \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} - \mathbf{I}_k \|_F^2\)
permits a Taylor expansion for a perturbation \(\Delta\):
\[ \mathcal{P}(\mathbf{V} + \Delta) \approx \frac{1}{4} \text{Tr}\left( (\mathbf{V}^\top \Delta + \Delta^\top \mathbf{V})^2 \right) \]
This penalty evaluates to zero for any \(\Delta\) strictly within the
tangent space.

\subsection{Newton-Schulz Convergence}\label{newton-schulz-convergence}

We enforce orthogonality via the iteration sequence:
\[ \mathbf{Y}_{k+1} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{Y}_k (3\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Y}_k^\top \mathbf{Y}_k) \]
The residual error
\(\mathbf{E}_k = \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Y}_k^\top \mathbf{Y}_k\) obeys the
relation
\(\mathbf{E}_{k+1} = \frac{3}{4} \mathbf{E}_k^2 + \frac{1}{4} \mathbf{E}_k^3\).
This yields quadratic convergence:
\(\| \mathbf{E}_{k+1} \|_2 < \| \mathbf{E}_k \|_2^2\).

\subsection{Noise Reduction Law}\label{noise-reduction-law}

Assuming independent noise distributions
\(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_m \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})\),
the consensus variance scales as:
\[ \text{Var}\left( \frac{1}{M} \sum_{m=1}^M \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_m \right) = \frac{\sigma^2}{M} \]
The corresponding \(100(1-\delta)\%\) confidence interval for the
underlying signal \(\boldsymbol{\mu}\) is bounded by:
\[ \hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\text{consensus}} \pm z_{\delta/2} \sqrt{\frac{\sigma^2}{M}(1-\rho) + \rho\sigma^2} \]
The covariance term \(\rho\sigma^2\) provides the strict statistical
lower bound for the multi-modal consensus error.

\section{Statistical Sensitivity}\label{statistical-sensitivity}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Modality Weights
(\(w\))}{Modality Weights (w)}}\label{modality-weights-w}

We bound the Statistical Influence Function
\(\mathcal{I}_m = \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial w_m}\) by the
spectral gap \((\lambda_1 - \lambda_2)^{-1}\) of the input feature
covariance \(\Sigma_m\). Consequently, SiMLR operational sensitivity
scales proportionally with the degree of multicollinearity in the input
domain.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\alpha\)-Transition}{\textbackslash alpha-Transition}}\label{alpha-transition}

The mixing parameter \(\alpha\) controls the trade-off between
independent modality reconstruction and cross-modal consensus alignment.
We set the transition point at the epoch where the first derivative of
the consensus variance approaches zero.

\section{Technical Glossary}\label{technical-glossary}

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Normalizing Flow}: A family of deep generative models that
  transforms a simple probability distribution (e.g., standard
  multivariate Gaussian) into a complex distribution using a sequence of
  invertible, bijective mappings via the change-of-variables theorem.
\item
  \textbf{Bijective Mapping}: A mathematically exact, one-to-one and
  onto function. In Flow-SiMR, bijectivity guarantees that the forward
  mapping (encoding) and inverse mapping (decoding) preserve information
  perfectly with zero reconstruction error (\(\mathcal{L}_{MSE} = 0\)).
\item
  \textbf{Affine Coupling Layer}: The core building block of RealNVP
  normalizing flows. It splits the input features into two halves,
  copying one partition directly while scaling and translating the other
  half using arbitrary non-linear neural networks (which do not need to
  be invertible themselves).
\item
  \textbf{OLEE (Only Linear Encoder Entry-point)}: An architectural
  constraint that restricts the entry layer of a deep neural network to
  be an orthogonal linear projection. This forms a structural
  information bottleneck that prevents deep layers from memorizing
  noise.
\item
  \textbf{NSA-Flow (Non-negative Stiefel Approximating Flow)}: A smooth,
  fully differentiable optimization penalty used to constrain the linear
  encoder basis matrix \(\mathbf{V}_m\) to the Stiefel manifold
  (\(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_K\)) during
  backpropagation, replacing hard SVD projections.
\item
  \textbf{Schur Complement}: A matrix operation used to compute the
  conditional mean and covariance of a partitioned multivariate Gaussian
  distribution in closed form, enabling mathematically valid cross-view
  imputation.
\item
  \textbf{Leave-One-Out (LOO) Topology}: An optimization pathway where
  each view's latent representation is aligned against a consensus
  target computed exclusively from all \emph{other} views, preventing
  the model from learning trivial identity mappings.
\item
  \textbf{Modality Alignment Index (MAI)}: A metric (typically based on
  Procrustes \(R^2\)) that quantifies the alignment fidelity between an
  individual view's latent space and the global consensus.
\item
  \textbf{Spectral Sharpness}: A random-matrix-theory-based metric
  evaluating singular value concentrations to identify and suppress
  isotropic high-dimensional noise, preventing it from corrupting the
  consensus representation.
\item
  \textbf{Classical SiMLR}: Similarity-driven Multi-view Linear
  Reconstruction. Strictly linear baseline architecture assuming
  Euclidean projection of modalities.
\item
  \textbf{LEND}: Linear Encoder, Deep Decoder. Combines OLEE-constrained
  linear encoder with a deep non-linear decoder, balancing
  interpretability and reconstruction fidelity.
\item
  \textbf{NED}: Non-linear Encoder-Decoder. Applies non-linear MLP
  transformations to both encoder (post-OLEE) and decoder, maximizing
  representational capacity.
\item
  \textbf{NEDPP}: Non-linear Encoder-Decoder with Private Partitions.
  Disentangles modality-specific private variance from shared
  cross-modal consensus.
\item
  \textbf{Flow-SiMR}: Normalizing Flow Similarity-driven Multi-view
  Representation. Integrates bijective normalizing flows with latent
  consensus alignment for information-preserving multi-modal
  integration.
\item
  \textbf{Flow-SiMR-V}: Flow-SiMR with a linear projection matrix (OLEE)
  entry point to allow feature attribution while maintaining deep
  flow-based mappings.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Normalizing Flow SiMR with Linear Encoder Bottleneck
(Flow-SiMR-V)}\label{normalizing-flow-simr-with-linear-encoder-bottleneck-flow-simr-v}

\section{Model Formulation}\label{model-formulation}

To resolve the ``Information Masking'' problem inherent in deep
multi-modal normalizing flows, we introduce \textbf{Flow-SiMR-V}, which
pre-pends a Stiefel-constrained linear encoder matrix \(\mathbf{V}_m\)
to each view's Normalizing Flow. This architecture establishes a strict
globally interpretable information bottleneck at the input entry point
while retaining bijective density mapping and conditional inference on
the consensus latent space. The underlying normalizing flows are
implemented natively in PyTorch, adapting structures from the
Latent-Aligned Multiview Normalizing Flows (LAMNr Flows) open-source
framework
(\href{https://github.com/ntustison/LAMNrFlows}{ntustison/LAMNrFlows}).

\subsection{Mathematical Formulation}\label{mathematical-formulation}

For each input modality \(\mathbf{X}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D_m}\):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\item
  \textbf{Only Linear Encoder Entry (OLEE) Bottleneck}:
  \[\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \text{subject to } \mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_K\]
  where \(\mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{D_m \times K}\) is regularized
  via Non-negative Stiefel Approximating Flow (NSA-Flow) and sparsity
  quantile thresholding.
\item
  \textbf{Low-Dimensional Bijective Normalizing Flow}:
  \[\mathbf{U}_m = f_{\text{flow}, m}(\mathbf{Z}_m)\] where
  \(f_{\text{flow}, m}: \mathbb{R}^K \to \mathbb{R}^K\) is a bijective
  transform mapping the projected coordinate to the shared latent
  coordinate.
\item
  \textbf{Consensus and Inverse Reconstruction}:
  \[\mathbf{U}_{\text{consensus}} = \text{mixing}(\mathbf{U}_1, \dots, \mathbf{U}_M)\]
  \[\mathbf{X}_m^{\text{recon}} = f_{\text{flow}, m}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{\text{consensus}}) \mathbf{V}_m^\top\]
\end{enumerate}

\section{Algorithm for Flow-SiMR-V with Delayed Gating
Warmup}\label{algorithm-for-flow-simr-v-with-delayed-gating-warmup}

To stably optimize the joint-variational objective under dynamic
modality weighting without triggering weight collapse, we train the
linear encoders, normalizing flows, and dynamic weights using the
Block-Coordinate Descent (BCD) procedure detailed below.

\subsection{Algorithmic Flow}\label{algorithmic-flow}

For a set of \(M\) data matrices \(\mathbf{X}_1, \dots, \mathbf{X}_M\),
shared latent rank \(K\), and max training epochs \(E\):

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Forward Pass}:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Project features to low-dimensional space:
    \(\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) for \(m=1, \dots, M\).
  \item
    Apply normalizing flows:
    \(\mathbf{U}_m = f_{\text{flow}, m}(\mathbf{Z}_m)\) and extract
    shared consensus latents
    \(\mathbf{Z}_{m, \text{shared}} = \mathbf{U}_{m, 1:K}\).
  \item
    Compute shared consensus coordinates using Newton-Schulz or ICA
    mixing:
    \(\mathbf{U}_{\text{consensus}} = \text{mixing}(\mathbf{Z}_{1, \text{shared}}, \dots, \mathbf{Z}_{M, \text{shared}})\).
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Delayed Gating (Modality Gating Schedule)}:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    For epoch \(t < T_{\text{delayed}}\): set modality weights uniformly
    \(w_m = 1/M\).
  \item
    For epoch \(t \ge T_{\text{delayed}}\):

    \begin{itemize}
    \tightlist
    \item
      Compute the Leave-One-Out (LOO) consensus target for each view:
      \(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m} = \sum_{j \neq m} w_j \mathbf{Z}_{j, \text{shared}}\).
    \item
      Compute the Modality Alignment Index:
      \(\text{MAI}_m = 1 - \frac{\|\mathbf{Z}_{m, \text{shared}}\Omega - \mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\|_F^2}{\|\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\|_F^2}\).
    \item
      Update gating weights:
      \(w_m = (1 - \rho) \cdot \frac{1}{M} + \rho \cdot \text{softmax}(\tau \cdot \text{MAI}_m)\)
      (with exponential ramp-up \(\rho\)).
    \end{itemize}
  \end{itemize}
\item
  \textbf{Loss Computation \& Backpropagation}:

  \begin{itemize}
  \tightlist
  \item
    Compute reconstruction loss:
    \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} = \sum_m \|\mathbf{X}_m - \hat{\mathbf{X}}_m\|_F^2\)
    where
    \(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_m = f_{\text{flow}, m}^{-1}([\mathbf{U}_{\text{consensus}} \parallel \mathbf{0}])\mathbf{V}_m^\top\).
  \item
    Compute alignment loss:
    \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} = \sum_m \|\mathbf{Z}_{m, \text{shared}}\Omega - \mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\|_F^2\).
  \item
    Backpropagate gradients to update linear projections
    \(\mathbf{V}_m\) and flow parameters.
  \item
    Retract \(\mathbf{V}_m\) onto the Stiefel manifold using NSA-Flow
    Newton-Schulz updates.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Block-Level Sign-Flip Permutation
Floor}\label{block-level-sign-flip-permutation-floor}

In statistical hypothesis testing between paired models across \(B = 9\)
independent dataset blocks (where each block cell is the mean across
\(N_{\text{seeds}} = 3\) replicates, following Demšar (2006)), the exact
distribution-free sign-flip null distribution across the \(B = 9\)
blocks consists of \(2^B = 512\) equally probable sign assignments: \[
p_{\text{perm}} = \frac{1}{2^B} \sum_{\mathbf{s} \in \{-1, +1\}^B} \mathbb{I}\left(\left|\frac{1}{B} \sum_{i=1}^B s_i \Delta_i\right| \ge |\bar{\Delta}|\right)
\]

For \(B = 9\) independent dataset blocks (or \(N = 10\) independent
seeds in the ADNI-MCI 4-view mediation benchmark), the minimal
attainable two-sided permutation \(p\)-value occurs when every block
favours the same model: \[
p_{\min, B=9} = \frac{2}{2^9} = \frac{2}{512} \approx 0.003906, \qquad p_{\min, N=10} = \frac{2}{2^{10}} = \frac{2}{1024} \approx 0.001953
\]

We enforce the following statistical standards throughout the
manuscript:

\begin{enumerate}
\def\labelenumi{\arabic{enumi}.}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Zero Unadjusted Pooled \(p\)-values}: We completely eliminate
  pooled repeated-measures \(t\)-tests and unaggregated seed-rows in
  global Friedman/Wilcoxon tests (\texttt{build\_ranking\_blocks}).
  Crossing multiple seeds or hyperparameters on the same dataset does
  not increase dataset-level degrees of freedom.
\item
  \textbf{Floor Truncation}: No distribution-free permutation test can
  report a value below \(2/2^B\).
\item
  \textbf{Triple Metric Reporting}: Global consensus prediction (\(Y\)
  on \(\mathbf{U}\)), strictly linear first-layer accuracy
  (\(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\)), and axis-sensitive random forest probe
  accuracy on \(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\) are reported alongside
  ground-truth latent (\(CMC\)) and sparse support Jaccard recovery.
\end{enumerate}
