This appendix provides the complete empirical ablation tables referenced in the main text, covering the nsa_flow trade-off weight \(w \in [0.01, 0.95]\), in-fold subject row-mean removal and deterministic ICA initialization, fine-grained quantile sparsity \(q \in [\text{None}, 0.90]\), the 1,025-configuration ADNI-MCI grid sweep, synthetic per-regime breakdowns, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests against PCA, followed by computational complexity bounds.

B.1 Effect of the Approximate Stiefel Weight \(w \in [0.01, 0.95]\)

Table B.1 and Figure B.1 evaluate SiMLR (\(\mathbf{V}_m \ge 0, k = 3\)) across ten values of \(w \in [0.01, 0.95]\) on synthetic NonnegParts and clinical ADNI-MCI (Protocol B). Across \(w \in [0.20, 0.85]\), ADNI-MCI CogPC1 \(R^2\) spans 0.205 to 0.218 (0.211 at \(w = 0.01\), 0.218 at \(w = 0.50\), and 0.211 at \(w = 0.95\)), while effective rank reaches 3.00 at \(w = 0.01\).

B.1.1 Basis Health and Rank-Selection Stability Diagnostics on ADNI-MCI

To verify optimization conditioning and component identifiability directly on the four-view ADNI-MCI cohort (Protocol B), Figure B.2 applies pysimlr.plot_basis_diagnostics (\(k = 3\), \(q = 0.85\)), reporting per-view effective rank against \(k\), maximum column cosine overlap, \(\log_{10}\) condition number, and achieved sparsity alongside the median loading_margin. Figure B.3 applies pysimlr.plot_rank_selection (criterion="latent_reliability", Section 2.7 and Equation 2.10) to the 20-subsample select_rank evidence (docs/audit/evidence/adni_rank_selection.csv), requiring every component’s out-of-bag latent_reliability_gain (\(\mathrm{ICC}(1) - \text{baseline}\)) to clear 0.2, non-decreasing multivariate reliability gain, and all_coupled = True. Under positivity="positive", select_rank selects \(k =\) 2: at \(k = 2\), component reliabilities are 0.891 and 0.975 (gains 0.438 and 0.630, multivariate reliability 0.945, gain 0.520, all_coupled = True); at \(k = 3\), Component 2 drops to 0.502 (gain 0.219), multivariate gain falls to 0.402 (failing multivariate monotonicity), and all_coupled = False (passed = False). Under signed estimation (positivity="either"), select_rank also selects \(k =\) 2: at \(k = 2\), component reliabilities are 0.697 and 0.603 (gains 0.478 and 0.432, multivariate reliability 0.651, gain 0.456, all_coupled = True), whereas at \(k = 3\) Component 3 drops to 0.197 (gain -0.053, passed = False).

B.2 Preprocessing Controls: Subject Row-Mean Removal, ICA Initialization, and Orthogonal Varimax Rotation

On neuroimaging cohorts where cortical regions share a positive global correlation floor, Table B.2 and Figure B.4 compare column centering with in-fold subject row-mean centering, PCA with deterministic FastICA (Hyvärinen and Oja 2000) initialization, signed with non-negative bases, and the initializer alone with the fitted model (SiMLR, \(k = 3\), \(w = 0.50\), no Varimax, no quantile threshold; scripts/paper_adni_ablations.py). Row-mean centering applies to the three MRI views only; the eleven cognitive scales are on different units, and subtracting each subject’s mean across them would remove overall severity from the outcome. With non-negative bases, row-mean centering changes structural MRI sparsity from 92% to 56% and CogPC1 \(R^2\) from 0.208 to 0.150 with PCA initialization (and to 54% sparsity and 0.171 CogPC1 \(R^2\) with ICA initialization). Varimax rotation on column-centered data gives 67% sparsity on the 62 DKT regions with [2, 0, 60] active regions per component, against 67% and [60, 2, 0] without rotation.
