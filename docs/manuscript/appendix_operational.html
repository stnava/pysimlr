B.1.1 Basis Health and Rank-Selection Stability Diagnostics on ADNI-MCI

To verify optimization conditioning and component identifiability directly on the four-view ADNI-MCI cohort (Protocol B), Figure B.2 applies pysimlr.plot_basis_diagnostics (\(k = 3\), \(q = 0.85\)), reporting per-view effective rank against \(k\), maximum column cosine overlap, \(\log_{10}\) condition number, and achieved sparsity alongside the median loading_margin. Figure B.3 applies pysimlr.plot_rank_selection to the 20-subsample select_rank evidence (docs/audit/evidence/adni_rank_selection.csv), contrasting non-negative estimation (positivity="positive", where \(k = 2\) clears the \(0.90\) identification bar on all components while \(k = 3\) exposes an under-identified Component 2 at \(0.803\)) against unconstrained signed estimation (positivity="either", where no candidate \(k \in \{2, 3, 4, 5\}\) clears \(0.90\)).

B.2 Preprocessing Controls: Subject Row-Mean Removal, ICA Initialization, and Orthogonal Varimax Rotation

On neuroimaging cohorts where cortical regions share a positive global correlation floor, Table B.2 and Figure B.4 compare column centering with in-fold subject row-mean centering, PCA with deterministic FastICA (Hyvärinen and Oja 2000) initialization, signed with non-negative bases, and the initializer alone with the fitted model (SiMLR, \(k = 3\), \(w = 0.50\), no Varimax, no quantile threshold; scripts/paper_adni_ablations.py). Row-mean centering applies to the three MRI views only; the eleven cognitive scales are on different units, and subtracting each subject’s mean across them would remove overall severity from the outcome. With non-negative bases, row-mean centering changes cortical sparsity from 93% to 59% and CogPC1 \(R^2\) from 0.196 to 0.162. Varimax rotation on column-centered data gives 67% sparsity on the 62 DKT regions with [2, 0, 60] active regions per component, against 67% and [60, 1, 1] without rotation.
