3.1 Experimental Design, Cohorts, and Generative Regimes

3.1.1 Multi-Modal Data Regimes and Replication Structure

To rigorously evaluate multi-modal architectures across distinct inductive biases and biomedical domains, we establish a comprehensive benchmark protocol across 9 multi-modal data regimes (6 controlled synthetic manifolds with known ground-truth \(\mathbf{U}^*\) and \(\mathbf{V}^*\), plus 3 public clinical/multi-omics cohorts) evaluated across \(N_{\text{seeds}} = 3\) independent random seeds (\([42, 43, 44]\)) and 9 model architectures (\(243\) total model evaluations, with private_scale = 0.5, consolidate = False, and recommended "pca" initialization), alongside a dedicated 10-seed 4-view neuroimaging mediation benchmark on ADNI-MCI (\(N = 1,084\)). Following Demšar (2006), global statistical inference is conducted on the \(B = 9\) independent dataset blocks (build_ranking_blocks in src/pysimlr/benchmarks/deep_ranking.py), averaging replicates within each dataset before ranking so repeated seeds never inflate degrees of freedom.

3.1.2 Established Real-World Biomedical Cohorts

All real-world cohorts underwent strict in-fold standardization (StandardScaler fitted exclusively on the training partition) to prevent test-set leakage:

  1. Cleveland Heart Disease (Heart, \(N=297\), 2 views: \(p_1=7, p_2=6\)):
    • Cohort Source & Filtering: Drawn from the UCI Machine Learning Repository (processed.cleveland.data, cached locally at data/heart_cleveland.csv) (Detrano et al. 1989). From the 303 clinical records, six individuals with missing values (?) are removed, yielding \(N=297\) complete records.
    • View Partitioning: Clinical predictors are split into two diagnostic views:
      • \(V_1\) (\(p_1=7\)): Resting clinical history and vitals (age, sex, chest pain type cp, resting blood pressure trestbps, serum cholesterol chol, fasting blood sugar fbs, resting ECG restecg).
      • \(V_2\) (\(p_2=6\)): Exercise stress testing and fluoroscopy (maximum heart rate thalach, exercise-induced angina exang, ST depression oldpeak, peak exercise ST slope slope, major vessels colored by fluoroscopy ca, thallium scintigraphy thal).
    • Downstream Task & Results: Binary coronary artery disease diagnosis (num > 0, majority rate \(53.9\%\)). In the 3-seed benchmark, NSAFlow-PCA and PCA achieve \(83.7\%\) test accuracy (\(84.8\%\) strictly linear probe for NSAFlow-PCA), followed by LEND, NED, SiMLR, and SiMLR-LBFGSB at \(83.0\%\text{–}83.3\%\).
  2. Diabetes Progression (Diabetes, \(N=442\), 2 views: \(p_1=5, p_2=5\)):
    • Cohort Source: The Efron et al. clinical diabetes cohort (Efron et al. 2004) (sklearn.datasets.load_diabetes).
    • View Partitioning: Ten baseline predictors are partitioned into patient/hemodynamic measures and serum panel chemistries:
      • \(V_1\) (\(p_1=5\)): Patient demographics, anthropometrics, and total cholesterol (age, sex, bmi, mean blood pressure bp, total serum cholesterol s1).
      • \(V_2\) (\(p_2=5\)): Specialized blood serum panel (LDL s2, HDL s3, total/HDL ratio s4, log triglycerides s5, fasting glucose s6).
    • Downstream Task & Results: Continuous 1-year disease progression (\(R^2\)). In the 3-seed benchmark, NSAFlow-PCA leads global consensus prediction (\(R^2 = 0.382\)), followed by SiMLR-LBFGS (\(R^2 = 0.352\)), PCA (\(R^2 = 0.350\)), and LEND (\(R^2 = 0.346\)), while SiMLR-LBFGSB achieves the highest strictly linear (\(R^2 = 0.396\)) and axis-sensitive (\(R^2 = 0.355\)) first-layer probe scores.
  3. TCGA-BRCA 3-View Multi-Omics (MultiOmics, \(N=822\), 3 views: \(p=[200, 150, 150]\)):
    • Cohort Source: The Cancer Genome Atlas Breast Invasive Carcinoma cohort (data/tcga/BRCA_*, cached at data/tcga/brca_er_cached.npz) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; Cancer Discovery 2016).
    • View Partitioning: Three distinct molecular assay platforms across \(N=822\) primary tumors with complete multi-omic profiles and adjudicated receptor status:
      • \(V_1\) (\(p_1=200\)): Top-variance mRNA sequencing gene expression transcripts (Illumina HiSeqV2).
      • \(V_2\) (\(p_2=150\)): Somatic Copy Number Variation (CNV) GISTIC2 gene-level scores.
      • \(V_3\) (\(p_3=150\)): Reverse Phase Protein Array (RPPA) targeted proteomic and phosphoproteomic markers.
    • Downstream Task & Results: Binary classification of clinical Estrogen Receptor (ER) status (Positive vs. Negative, ER+ prevalence \(76.4\%\)). In the 3-seed benchmark, NSAFlow-PCA achieves \(92.7\%\) test accuracy, followed by LEND (\(91.9\%\)), PCA (\(91.9\%\)), NEDPP (\(91.8\%\)), and SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(91.7\%\), with \(93.0\%\) strictly linear probe accuracy).
  4. ADNI-MCI 4-View Neurodegeneration Mediation Cohort (\(N=1,084\), 4 views: \(p=[160, 160, 62, 13]\)):
    • Cohort Source: \(N=1,084\) individuals diagnosed with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (Weiner et al. 2017; Jack et al. 2008).
    • View Partitioning: Three structural brain MRI views (\(382\) features: macro-volume \(p_1=160\), hierarchical cortical thickness \(p_2=160\), native T1w thickness \(p_3=62\)) and one cognitive domain battery (\(p_4=13\) sub-items), adjusting for Age, Sex, and Education.
    • Downstream Task & Results: Out-of-sample prediction of daily functional disability (CDR-SB) and global cognition (MMSE). Direct brain MRI alone hits a ceiling of \(R^2 = 0.0411\), whereas cognitive mediation boosts prediction more than 6-fold (LEND-SiMR + MAI: \(R^2 = 0.2648 \pm 0.0332\); SiMLR: \(R^2 = 0.2443 \pm 0.0534\) in \(0.86\text{s}\)), detailed in plain language in Section 3.5.
  5. Additional Auxiliary Validation Cohorts (mfeat, ADNI-DX, TCGA-KIRC):
    • Complementing the primary benchmark, auxiliary evaluations on UCI Multiple Features (mfeat, \(N=2,000, p=[76, 64, 6, 47]\), 10-class digits), 3-class ADNI-DX (\(N=685, p=[300, 145, 36]\)), and TCGA-KIRC (\(N=467, p=[300, 300, 232]\)) confirm that OLEE-constrained models avoid the high-dimensional canonical correlation overfitting seen in unregularized GCCA-raw.

3.1.3 Controlled Synthetic Generative Regimes

To test distinct, non-overlapping hypotheses about multi-view recovery \((\mathbf{U}^*, \mathbf{V}^*)\) under private_scale = 0.5, synthetic_cases.py defines six synthetic generative regimes:

  1. Clean Linear Multi-View Manifold (Linear, \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[100, 80, 60]\)): \[\mathbf{X}_m = \mathbf{U}^* \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\] where shared latent scales \([1.2, 1.0, 0.85]\) exceed view-private background (\(\sigma_{\text{priv}} = 0.5\)) and Gaussian noise (\(\sigma = 0.5\)), with equal outcome weights \(\mathbf{w} = [1, 1, 1]^\top\). Tests baseline recovery where linear and cross-view models should perform at parity (\(R^2 \approx 0.976\text{–}0.990\)).

  2. Polynomial Latent Warping (Polynomial, \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[120, 80, 50]\)): \[\mathbf{X}_m = \psi_m^{\text{poly}}(\mathbf{U}^*) \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\] where \(\psi_m^{\text{poly}}(\mathbf{U}^*)\) applies non-cancelling quadratic, cross-product, and cubic distortions in \(\mathbb{R}^k\) prior to linear projection \(\mathbf{V}_m^\top\). Because same-sign quadratic warpings do not cancel when views are averaged linearly, axis-sensitive nonlinear probes on \(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) (NED \(0.961\), Flow-SiMR-V \(0.954\), LEND \(0.954\)) outperform linear OLS on \(\mathbf{U}\) (\(0.870\text{–}0.874\)).

  3. Sinusoidal Periodic Warping (Sine, \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[120, 80, 50]\)): \[\mathbf{X}_m = \psi_m^{\text{sin}}(\mathbf{U}^*) \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\] where \(\psi_m^{\text{sin}}(\mathbf{U}^*)\) combines linear coordinates with non-cancelling trigonometric oscillations (\(\sin\) and \(\cos\) couplings). Tests recovery under periodic manifold curvature.

  4. Asymmetric Shared-Plus-Private Manifold (Shared+Private, \(N=1,000, K_{\text{sh}}=3, K_{\text{pr}}=2, p=[160, 60, 30]\)): \[\mathbf{X}_m = \mathbf{U}_{\text{shared}}^* \mathbf{V}_{m, \text{sh}}^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m, \text{pr}}^* \mathbf{V}_{m, \text{pr}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\] where shared scales decay (\([1.0, 0.625, 0.455]\)) while the largest view (\(p_1=160\)) carries heightened view-private variance (\(\sigma_{\text{priv},1} = 0.80\)). Single-matrix concatenation (PCA \(R^2 = 0.639\), NSAFlow-PCA \(R^2 = 0.629\)) is pulled toward View 1’s private variance, whereas cross-view consensus in LEND recovers all shared factors (\(R^2 = 0.949\), \(CMC = 0.896\)) and Flow-SiMR-V achieves \(R^2 = 0.908\) on its first-layer linear probe.

  5. Non-Negative Disjoint Parts — Dominant Signal (NonnegParts, \(N=400, K=3, p=[120, 45, 30]\)): Ground-truth loading matrices \(\mathbf{V}_m^* \ge \mathbf{0}\) have strictly disjoint column supports across the \(K=3\) components, and the outcome tracks the dominant shared component (\(u_1^*\)). Bound-constrained SiMLR-LBFGSB recovers the true sparse support with Jaccard score \(0.942\) (followed by LEND at \(0.899\) and Flow-SiMR-V at \(0.883\)), whereas unconstrained PCA scores at the random-partition chance floor (\(0.333\)).

  6. Non-Negative Disjoint Parts — Weak Signal (NonnegPartsWeak, \(N=400, K=3, p=[120, 45, 30]\)): Identical disjoint non-negative basis \(\mathbf{V}_m^*\), but the outcome is driven by the weakest shared component (\(u_3^*\)). Because single-matrix concatenation absorbs view-private noise into its leading components, PCA drops to \(R^2 = 0.522\) and NSAFlow-PCA to \(0.570\), whereas LEND (\(R^2 = 0.732\)), SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(R^2 = 0.715\)), and SiMLR-LBFGS (\(R^2 = 0.706\)) isolate the weak shared component via cross-view alignment and non-negativity.

3.2 Rigorous Statistical Review: Multi-Classifier Demšar Benchmark

To establish a rigorous empirical ranking, we evaluate all nine architectures across the \(B = 9\) independent dataset blocks (\(9 \text{ data regimes} \times 3 \text{ random seeds} \times 9 \text{ models} = 243\) complete evaluations). Following Demšar (2006), replicates are first averaged within each \((\text{Dataset}, \text{Model})\) cell via build_ranking_blocks, yielding \(B = 9\) independent paired blocks across \(k = 9\) architectures.

Across the 9 dataset blocks, the non-parametric Friedman test confirms highly significant architectural separation: \[ \chi_F^2 = 32.47, \quad p = 7.68 \times 10^{-5} \] The Iman–Davenport extension reinforces this separation: \[ F_F = 6.57, \quad p = 3.13 \times 10^{-6} \quad (\text{df}_1 = 8, \text{df}_2 = 64) \] At \(\alpha = 0.05\), the two-tailed Nemenyi Critical Difference across \(k = 9\) models and \(B = 9\) dataset blocks is \(CD = 4.004\) rank units.

As reported in Table 3.2 and Figure 3.2:

  1. Box-Constrained Riemannian Solver (SiMLR-LBFGSB, Mean Rank 3.28): Combining L-BFGS-B box constraints with non-negative Stiefel consolidation achieves the best overall predictive rank (\(3.28\), mean predictive score \(0.795 \pm 0.201\), linear probe \(0.830\)) and the highest sparse feature support recovery across the benchmark (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.942\)) with near-zero per-view frame defect (\(D = 2 \times 10^{-5}\)).
  2. Linear Encoder with Non-Linear Decoder (LEND, Mean Rank 3.56): LEND achieves the highest mean predictive accuracy (\(0.830 \pm 0.189\)), highest strictly linear probe (\(0.836\)), highest axis-aligned Random Forest probe (\(0.830\)), and highest latent coordinate recovery (\(CMC = 0.907\)). On the Shared+Private regime where View 0 contains strong private noise, LEND reaches \(R^2 = 0.949\) versus \(0.639\) for concatenated PCA.
  3. Turnkey Sparse SVD Contrast (NSAFlow-PCA, Mean Rank 3.61) & Classical PCA (Mean Rank 3.78): NSAFlow-PCA executes in \(0.029\) seconds per fit—nearly matching PCA (\(0.024\text{s}\))—while outperforming PCA on predictive accuracy (\(0.782\) vs \(0.774\)), linear probe (\(0.776\) vs \(0.767\)), and latent recovery (\(CMC = 0.834\) vs \(0.827\)), and enforcing \(22.4\%\) disjoint sparsity with exact zero signed crosstalk (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)).
  4. Riemannian Quasi-Newton & First-Order Solvers (SiMLR 4.17, SiMLR-LBFGS 4.56): SiMLR-LBFGS converges in \(0.175\text{s}\) (\(2\times\) faster than first-order SiMLR at \(0.363\text{s}\)) while raising support recovery from \(0.495\) to \(0.766\) and predictive score from \(0.785\) to \(0.797\).
  5. Invertible Normalizing Flows (Flow-SiMR-V, Mean Rank 6.50): While consensus normalizing flows have lower direct \(\mathbf{U}\) probe scores on high-noise regimes, their first-layer linear projection \(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\) achieves the second-highest linear probe score across the entire benchmark (\(0.834\), Including \(R^2 = 0.908\) on Shared+Private and \(0.744\) on NonnegPartsWeak) alongside \(0.883\) support recovery.
  6. Collapse of Unconstrained Deep Encoders (NED 7.72, NEDPP 7.83): Multi-layer perceptron encoders rank last across the suite (\(0.618\) and \(0.633\)) and degrade latent recovery (\(CMC = 0.589\) and \(0.652\)), confirming that removing the linear Stiefel entrypoint causes severe overfitting in sample-limited regimes.

3.3 Multi-Objective Pareto Frontier Analysis

Architectural selection across clinical and high-dimensional biomedical regimes cannot be reduced to a single 1D predictive metric. Models must be evaluated across four complementary operational axes:

  1. Downstream Predictive Capacity (\(Y\)): Cross-validated out-of-sample \(R^2\) or diagnostic classification accuracy.
  2. Ground-Truth Latent Discovery (\(CMC\) on \(\mathbf{U}\)): Canonical Mean Correlation with the true generative latent coordinates \(\mathbf{S}\).
  3. Sparse Feature Support Recovery (\(F_1\) on \(\mathbf{V}\)): Jaccard/F1 overlap with true localized anatomical or molecular feature blocks, alongside zero signed crosstalk (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)).
  4. Computational Throughput (\(t\)): Wall-clock execution time per fit.

3.4 Ground-Truth Latent Discovery across Generative Regimes

To evaluate latent coordinate identifiability, we assess Canonical Mean Correlation (\(CMC\)) across all six synthetic regimes with exact mathematical generators (Linear, Polynomial, Sine, Shared+Private, NonnegParts, and NonnegPartsWeak), while reporting NaN for the three real clinical cohorts where ground-truth latents are unobserved.

As demonstrated in Figure 3.4:

  • Cross-View Noise Rejection on Shared+Private: When View 0 carries strong modality-specific nuisance variance (\(\sigma_{\text{priv}} = 0.8\)), single-matrix concatenation (PCA and NSAFlow-PCA, \(CMC = 0.636\)) rotates its top axes toward the high-variance private subspace. In contrast, LEND (\(CMC = 0.896\), test \(R^2 = 0.949\)) and Flow-SiMR-V (\(CMC = 0.692\), linear probe \(R^2 = 0.908\)) isolate the shared cross-view manifold.
  • Disjoint Parts Identification on NonnegParts & NonnegPartsWeak: Non-negative Stiefel consolidation enables SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.942, CMC = 0.817\)), LEND (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.899, CMC = 0.865\)), and Flow-SiMR-V (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.883\)) to recover the exact sparse support and predict the weakest component (NonnegPartsWeak \(R^2 = 0.715\text{–}0.732\) vs \(0.522\) for PCA).
  • Invariant Guarantees: Signed lobe crosstalk (\(\|\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^-\|_1\)) is strictly \(0.0000\) across all 243 evaluations. Per-view trace-normalized Stiefel frame defect \(D\) is bounded near zero (\(10^{-5}\text{–}10^{-4}\)) for all cross-view Stiefel models, while concatenated PCA and NSAFlow-PCA enforce joint orthogonality across the stacked feature space (\(\sum_m \mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k\)).

3.5 ADNI-MCI 4-View Mediated Neurodegeneration Benchmark

3.5.1 Why Predicting Clinical Dementia Directly from Brain Scans Hits a Wall

In Alzheimer’s disease and Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI), a primary clinical goal is predicting how severely a patient’s day-to-day functioning is impaired, measured by the Clinical Dementia Rating Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB)—a score reflecting whether a person can remember appointments, manage household finances, navigate their neighborhood, and care for themselves.

When researchers train machine learning models to predict CDR-SB directly from structural brain MRI alone, accuracy is consistently disappointing (Jack et al. 2008; Weiner et al. 2017). Across all 382 cortical volume and thickness measurements in our ADNI-MCI cohort (\(N = 1,084\)), predicting CDR-SB directly from brain anatomy reaches an out-of-sample ceiling of only 4.1% explained variance: \[R^2 = 0.0411 \pm 0.0248 \quad (\text{holdout test } R^2 = 4.02\%, \quad \rho = 0.2112, \quad p = 1.2 \times 10^{-4})\]

The reason is simple and biological: a shrinking hippocampus does not directly stop a patient from balancing a checkbook. Instead, Alzheimer’s pathology acts in two sequential steps:

  1. Brain Atrophy \(\to\) Cognitive Loss (Path \(a\)): Physical tissue loss (cortical thinning and volume loss) damages core mental abilities measured in clinic—such as word recall, attention, and spatial orientation.
  2. Cognitive Loss \(\to\) Daily Functional Disability (Path \(b\)): Once those mental abilities decline, the patient loses independence in daily life, driving up their CDR-SB score.

3.5.2 Testing the Two-Step Biological Pathway (Baron–Kenny Mediation)

We tested this two-step pathway across \(N = 1,084\) MCI participants (\(70/30\) train/test split, \(N_{\text{test}} = 326\)) using classical Baron–Kenny mediation and Sobel testing:

  1. Step 1 — Brain Structure Strongly Predicts Cognitive Test Scores (Path \(a\)): \[\alpha = 1.000 \pm 0.081, \quad t = 12.31, \quad p < 10^{-25}, \quad R^2 = 26.13\%, \quad \rho = 0.5335\] Multi-view brain MRI explains over 26% of the variance in domain cognitive performance.
  2. Step 2 — Cognitive Test Scores Directly Drive Daily Disability (Path \(b\)): \[\beta = 0.013 \pm 0.003, \quad t = 4.69, \quad p < 10^{-15}\]
  3. Direct Bypass Path Vanishes (Path \(c'\)): \[\gamma' = 0.002, \quad t = 0.88, \quad p = 0.38 \quad (\text{Not Significant})\] Once cognitive performance is accounted for, the direct link from brain MRI to CDR-SB drops to zero.
  4. Share of the Effect Explained by Mediation: \[PM = \frac{\alpha \beta}{\alpha \beta + \gamma'} = \mathbf{84.5\% - 86.4\%} \quad (\text{Sobel } z = 4.38, \; p < 10^{-15})\]

In plain terms: roughly 85% of the effect of brain atrophy on daily dementia severity flows through cognitive performance. Ignoring this intermediate step is why direct MRI-to-CDR-SB models fail.

3.5.3 4-View Mediated Architecture & 10-Seed Replication Benchmark

To respect this biological cascade, we couple the three structural MRI views with a fourth view containing 13 domain cognitive sub-scores:

  • \(V_1\): Macro-structural gray matter parcel volumes (\(p_1 = 160\))
  • \(V_2\): Hierarchical multiscale cortical thickness (\(p_2 = 160\))
  • \(V_3\): Native T1-weighted cortical thickness (\(p_3 = 62\))
  • \(V_4\): Domain cognitive battery (\(p_4 = 13\), ADAS-Cog and MMSE sub-items)

All models predict out-of-sample functional disability (CDR-SB) and global cognition (MMSE) after adjusting for age, sex, and education. Table 3.4 reports performance across 10 independent train/test seeds.

As shown in Table 3.4:

  1. Over 6-Fold Gain in Predictive Accuracy: By aligning the three brain MRI views with the cognitive view, LEND-SiMR + MAI reaches out-of-sample \(R^2 = 0.2648 \pm 0.0332\) (\(\rho = 0.5206\), Wilcoxon \(p = 9.77 \times 10^{-4}\)), a 6.4-fold improvement over the anatomy-only ceiling (\(R^2 = 0.0411\)).
  2. Sub-Second Linear Stiefel Solver: Classical SiMLR [Mediated] reaches \(R^2 = 0.2443 \pm 0.0534\) (\(\rho = 0.4989\)) in 0.86 seconds, and Flow-SiMR-V + MAI reaches \(R^2 = 0.2290 \pm 0.0696\) (\(\rho = 0.4840\)).
  3. Why Unconstrained Deep Networks Fail Here Too: While every model with a linear Stiefel entrypoint (LEND-SiMR, SiMLR, Flow-SiMR-V) achieves \(R^2 \approx 0.23\text{–}0.26\), the unconstrained deep autoencoder (NED-SiMR + MAI) drops to \(R^2 = 0.1127 \pm 0.0578\) (and \(0.0840\) for NED-Shared-Private in Table 3.5)—worse than simple Ridge regression (\(0.1768\)). Without an orthogonal first-layer filter (\(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}\)), multi-layer perceptrons memorize noise across the 382 brain regions instead of isolating the shared atrophy-to-cognition pathway.

3.5.4 Dynamic Modality Weighting (MAI) and Optimizer Convergence

  1. Modality Alignment Index (MAI): Dynamic canonical-correlation weighting shifts modality weights from uniform \([0.25, 0.25, 0.25, 0.25]\) to \([0.197, 0.428, 0.293, 0.082]\) for LEND-SiMR, automatically emphasizing cortical thickness (\(0.428\)) and regional volumes (\(0.293\)) to reach \(R^2 = 0.2885\) on CDR-SB and \(R^2 = 0.2639\) on MMSE.
  2. Exact Stationarity: Linear SiMLR with Armijo backtracking line search converges in 7 iterations (\(0.86\text{s}\)); running for 500 iterations yields identical accuracy (\(R^2 = 0.2789, \rho = 0.5407\)) with zero parameter drift.

3.6.4 Topological Regularization: LOO vs. STAR Consensus Pathways

To evaluate architectural constraints, we compare the baseline Star topology with the proposed strict Leave-One-Out (LOO) topology. In Star topology, modality \(m\) aligns to a consensus \(\mathbf{U}\) that includes its own information. In LOO topology, modality \(m\) aligns against a consensus \(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\) built exclusively from other modalities.

3.8 Flow-SiMR-V Gating Stability and Clinical Robustness

We evaluate the Modality Alignment Index (MAI) dynamic weight gating on normalizing flow architectures under two challenging scenarios: a mixed non-linear synthetic benchmark containing a structured periodic signal and a completely random rogue noise view, and the TCGA-BRCA multi-omics classification task.

3.8.1 Mixed Non-linear Sinusoidal Benchmark

As shown in Table 3.6, purely static weights fail to suppress rogue noise, yielding a lower latent recovery (\(R^2 = 0.1288\)). Activating MAI gating immediately (epoch 0) on Flow-SiMR-V causes weight collapse on the low-dimensional space. Implementing Delayed Gating Warmup (uniform weights for the first 60 epochs) allows the projection layers to stabilize. When gating is activated at epoch 60, the model successfully quarantines the rogue view (noise weight driven to absolute zero) and achieves stable consensus alignment (\(R^2 = 0.3073\)), while the unconstrained Flow-SiMR model achieves \(R^2 = 0.8193\).

3.8.2 BRCA Clinical Multi-omics Evaluation

We validate the stability of Flow-SiMR-V gating on the high-dimensional TCGA-BRCA multi-omics clinical dataset (\(N = 1010\) samples). The views consist of RNA expression, copy number variations, protein expression, and an injected Gaussian noise view. We train models to learn a joint consensus representation, and predict patient Estrogen Receptor (ER) binary status using downstream logistic regression.

As shown in Table 3.7, under Delayed Gating (warmup = 75 epochs), Flow-SiMR-V achieves the highest downstream ER status prediction accuracy of 87.27%, outperforming both the static baseline (79.09%) and unconstrained Flow-SiMR (74.55%) by successfully quarantining the noise modality (weight reduced to \(0.0593\)) while maintaining representation capacity across all biological views.
