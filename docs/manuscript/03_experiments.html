3.1 Experimental Design, Cohorts, and Generative Regimes

3.1.1 Multi-Modal Data Regimes, Data Hygiene, and Replication Structure

To evaluate multi-modal architectures across distinct inductive biases and biomedical domains, we establish a unified benchmark protocol mirroring the five deductive stages of Chapter 2 across 12 multi-modal data regimes: 6 controlled synthetic manifolds with known ground-truth latents \(\mathbf{U}^*\) and bases \(\mathbf{V}^*\), plus 6 established real-world clinical, multi-omics, and neuroimaging cohorts evaluated across \(N_{\text{seeds}} = 3\) independent random seeds (\([42, 43, 44]\)) and 9 model architectures (\(12 \times 3 \times 9 = 324\) total model evaluations, with private_scale = 0.5, consolidate = False, and iteration-0 linear warm-start initialization), complemented by a 1,214-combination hyperparameter sweep and a 10-seed mediation benchmark on ADNI-MCI (\(N = 1,084\)).

Following Demšar (2006), global statistical inference is conducted on the \(B = 12\) independent dataset blocks (build_ranking_blocks in src/pysimlr/benchmarks/deep_ranking.py), averaging replicates within each \((\text{Dataset}, \text{Model})\) cell before ranking so repeated seeds never inflate degrees of freedom. Across all real-world cohorts, we enforce three strict data-hygiene invariants:

  1. In-Fold Standardization & Covariate Adjustment: Feature standardization (StandardScaler) and Frisch–Waugh–Lovell (FWL) demographic covariate residualization (AGE, PTGENDER, PTEDUCAT on ADNI-MCI) are fitted exclusively on the training partition and applied to the test partition without leakage.
  2. Column Completeness Filtering (\(\ge 98\%\)): High-missingness features (\(\ge 2\%\) missing values, such as sparse clinical scales MOCA and Ecog on ADNI-MCI or incomplete assay probes in TCGA) are excluded prior to median imputation so degenerate constant-imputed columns cannot distort cross-view covariance.
  3. Partial-View Out-of-Sample Evaluation (eval_view_indices): When a clinical target view is used during bipartite training (ADNI-MCI View 4), that view is strictly withheld at test time (eval_view_indices = [0, 1, 2]), and single-matrix baselines (PCA, NSAFlow-PCA) are fitted exclusively on the predictor views ([0, 1, 2]).

3.1.2 Established Real-World Biomedical and Multi-View Cohorts

  1. Cleveland Heart Disease (Heart, \(N=297\), 2 views: \(p_1=7, p_2=6\)):
    • Cohort Source & Filtering: Drawn from the UCI Machine Learning Repository (processed.cleveland.data, cached locally at data/heart_cleveland.csv) (Detrano et al. 1989). Six records with missing entries (?) are removed, yielding \(N=297\) complete patients.
    • View Partitioning: \(V_1\) (\(p_1=7\): resting vitals and history age, sex, cp, trestbps, chol, fbs, restecg) and \(V_2\) (\(p_2=6\): stress electrocardiography and fluoroscopy thalach, exang, oldpeak, slope, ca, thal).
    • Downstream Task & Results: Binary coronary artery disease diagnosis (num > 0, majority rate \(53.9\%\)). In the 3-seed benchmark, NSAFlow-PCA achieves the highest strictly linear probe accuracy (\(84.8\%\)), while NSAFlow-PCA and PCA achieve \(83.7\%\) consensus accuracy and SiMLR-LBFGSB / SiMLR-LBFGS reach \(83.7\%\) on the linear OLEE probe.
  2. Diabetes Progression (Diabetes, \(N=442\), 2 views: \(p_1=5, p_2=5\)):
    • Cohort Source: The Efron et al. clinical diabetes cohort (Efron et al. 2004) (sklearn.datasets.load_diabetes).
    • View Partitioning: \(V_1\) (\(p_1=5\): age, sex, bmi, bp, total serum cholesterol s1) and \(V_2\) (\(p_2=5\): specialized blood serum panel s2–s6).
    • Downstream Task & Results: Continuous 1-year disease progression (\(R^2\)). NSAFlow-PCA leads global consensus prediction (\(R^2 = 0.382\)), while SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(R^2 = 0.396\)) and SiMLR-LBFGS (\(R^2 = 0.396\)) lead the strictly linear OLEE probe (vs. PCA \(0.351\)).
  3. TCGA-BRCA 3-View Multi-Omics (MultiOmics, \(N=822\), 3 views: \(p=[200, 150, 150]\)):
    • Cohort Source: The Cancer Genome Atlas Breast Invasive Carcinoma cohort (data/tcga/brca_er_cached.npz, filtered at \(\ge 98\%\) completeness) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; Cancer Discovery 2016).
    • View Partitioning: \(V_1\) (\(p_1=200\): top-variance mRNA HiSeqV2 transcripts), \(V_2\) (\(p_2=150\): somatic GISTIC2 CNV), and \(V_3\) (\(p_3=150\): RPPA targeted proteomics).
    • Downstream Task & Results: Binary classification of Estrogen Receptor (ER) status (ER+ prevalence \(76.4\%\)). NSAFlow-PCA leads global consensus accuracy (\(92.7\%\)), while SiMLR-LBFGS, SiMLR-LBFGSB, LEND, and Flow-SiMR-V achieve \(92.6\%\) on the strictly linear OLEE probe.
  4. TCGA-KIRC 3-View Renal Oncology (TCGA-KIRC, \(N=467\), 3 views: \(p=[200, 150, 150]\)):
    • Cohort Source: The Cancer Genome Atlas Kidney Renal Clear Cell Carcinoma cohort (data/tcga/kirc_stage_cached.npz, filtered at \(\ge 98\%\) completeness) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network 2013).
    • View Partitioning: Three co-registered molecular views across \(N=467\) primary tumors: mRNA expression (\(p_1=200\)), GISTIC2 copy-number variation (\(p_2=150\)), and RPPA proteomics (\(p_3=150\)).
    • Downstream Task & Results: Binary classification of pathologic tumor stage (late stage III/IV vs. early stage I/II). Flow-SiMR-V and LEND lead global consensus accuracy (\(69.7\%\)), while SiMLR, SiMLR-LBFGS, and SiMLR-LBFGSB achieve \(68.6\%\) on the linear OLEE probe.
  5. ADNI-MCI 4-View Neurodegeneration Cohort (ADNI-MCI, \(N=1,084\), 4 views: \(p=[160, 160, 62, 11]\)):
    • Cohort Source: \(N=1,084\) participants with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (Weiner et al. 2017; Jack et al. 2008).
    • View Partitioning: Three structural brain MRI views (\(382\) features: DKT gray-matter volumes \(p_1=160\), hierarchical cortical thickness \(p_2=160\), native T1w thickness \(p_3=62\)) and a fourth view containing the 11 \(>98\%\)-complete clinical/cognitive scores (ADAS11, ADAS13, ADASQ4, MMSE, CDRSB, TRABSCOR, FAQ, and 4 RAVLT sub-scores), with in-fold FWL adjustment for AGE, PTGENDER, and PTEDUCAT.
    • Downstream Task & Results: Under Protocol B (bipartite training graph {0:[3], 1:[3], 2:[3], 3:[0,1,2]}, View 4 withheld at test time), models predict the primary multi-domain cognitive axis (CogPC1 R^2) from the 3 MRI views alone. Across 3 seeds in the global benchmark, SiMLR-LBFGSB achieves CogPC1 R^2 \(= \mathbf{0.2237}\) on the strictly linear OLEE probe (and \(0.1830\) on the 3-dimension consensus \(\mathbf{U}\)), outperforming single-matrix PCA (\(0.1522\)) by \(+47\%\). In our 1,214-combination sweep (Section 3.4), SiMLR-Lookahead reaches \(0.2407\), NEDPP reaches \(0.2357\), and Flow-SiMR-V reaches \(0.2341\). In our 10-seed mediation benchmark, modeling the two-step pathway (\(\text{Brain MRI} \to \text{Cognition} \to \text{CDR-SB}\)) boosts daily disability prediction over 6-fold (LEND-SiMR + MAI: \(R^2 = 0.2648\) vs. direct MRI \(0.0411\)).
  6. UCI Multiple Features Handwritten Digits (mfeat, \(N=2,000\), 4 views: \(p=[76, 64, 6, 47]\)):
    • Cohort Source: Four geometric and spectral descriptor views (Fourier coefficients \(p_1=76\), Karhunen–Loève projections \(p_2=64\), morphological features \(p_3=6\), Zernike moments \(p_4=47\)) across \(N=2,000\) handwritten digits (\(10\) balanced classes, \(k=3\)).
    • Downstream Task & Results: 10-class digit classification accuracy. Flow-SiMR-V leads 3-dimensional consensus accuracy (\(81.9\%\)) followed by LEND (\(78.9\%\)) and SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(77.1\%\)) vs. NSAFlow-PCA (\(70.7\%\)) and PCA (\(73.2\%\)). On the concatenated \(12\)-dimensional first-layer OLEE probe (\(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\)), cross-view Stiefel models reach \(94.4\%\text{–}96.2\%\) vs. \(72.7\%\) for PCA.

3.1.3 Controlled Synthetic Generative Regimes

To test distinct, non-overlapping hypotheses about multi-view recovery \((\mathbf{U}^*, \mathbf{V}^*)\) under private_scale = 0.5, synthetic_cases.py defines six synthetic generative regimes:

  1. Clean Linear Multi-View Manifold (Linear, \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[100, 80, 60]\)): \[\mathbf{X}_m = \mathbf{U}^* \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\] where shared latent scales \([1.2, 1.0, 0.85]\) exceed view-private background (\(\sigma_{\text{priv}} = 0.5\)) and Gaussian noise (\(\sigma = 0.5\)). Tests baseline recovery where linear and cross-view OLEE models perform at parity (\(R^2 = 0.972\text{–}0.990\), whereas unconstrained NED drops to \(0.713\)).

  2. Polynomial Latent Warping (Polynomial, \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[120, 80, 50]\)): \[\mathbf{X}_m = \psi_m^{\text{poly}}(\mathbf{U}^*) \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\] where \(\psi_m^{\text{poly}}(\mathbf{U}^*)\) applies non-cancelling quadratic, cross-product, and cubic distortions in \(\mathbb{R}^k\) prior to linear projection \(\mathbf{V}_m^\top\). Flow-SiMR-V leads global consensus prediction (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.925}\) vs. \(0.870\) for PCA), while linear probes on \(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) reach \(0.903\text{–}0.916\).

  3. Sinusoidal Periodic Warping (Sine, \(N=1,000, K=3, p=[120, 80, 50]\)): \[\mathbf{X}_m = \psi_m^{\text{sin}}(\mathbf{U}^*) \mathbf{V}_m^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m,\text{priv}} \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{priv}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\] where \(\psi_m^{\text{sin}}(\mathbf{U}^*)\) combines linear coordinates with trigonometric oscillations (\(\sin\) and \(\cos\) couplings), with SiMLR-LBFGS (\(0.935\)) and SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(0.934\)) leading consensus recovery.

  4. Asymmetric Shared-Plus-Private Manifold (Shared+Private, \(N=1,000, K_{\text{sh}}=3, K_{\text{pr}}=2, p=[160, 60, 30]\)): \[\mathbf{X}_m = \mathbf{U}_{\text{shared}}^* \mathbf{V}_{m, \text{sh}}^\top + \mathbf{U}_{m, \text{pr}}^* \mathbf{V}_{m, \text{pr}}^\top + \mathbf{E}_m\] where shared scales decay (\([1.0, 0.625, 0.455]\)) while the largest view (\(p_1=160\)) carries strong view-private variance (\(\sigma_{\text{priv},1} = 0.80\)). Single-matrix concatenation (PCA \(R^2 = 0.639\), NSAFlow-PCA \(R^2 = 0.629\)) is pulled toward View 1’s private variance, whereas cross-view consensus in LEND (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.949}\), \(CMC = 0.896\)) and Flow-SiMR-V (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.845}\), linear probe \(0.908\)) isolates the shared manifold.

  5. Non-Negative Disjoint Parts — Dominant Signal (NonnegParts, \(N=400, K=3, p=[120, 45, 30]\)): Ground-truth loading matrices \(\mathbf{V}_m^* \ge \mathbf{0}\) have strictly disjoint column supports across the \(K=3\) components, and the outcome tracks the dominant shared component (\(u_1^*\)). Bound-constrained SiMLR-LBFGSB recovers the true sparse support with Jaccard/F1 score \(0.991\) (followed by SiMLR-LBFGS at \(0.966\), LEND at \(0.899\), and Flow-SiMR-V at \(0.826\)), whereas unconstrained PCA scores at the random-partition chance floor (\(0.333\)).

  6. Non-Negative Disjoint Parts — Weak Signal (NonnegPartsWeak, \(N=400, K=3, p=[120, 45, 30]\)): Identical disjoint non-negative basis \(\mathbf{V}_m^*\), but the outcome is driven by the weakest shared component (\(u_3^*\)). Because single-matrix concatenation absorbs view-private noise into its leading components, PCA drops to \(R^2 = 0.522\) and NSAFlow-PCA to \(0.570\), whereas LEND (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.732}\)), SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.716}\)), SiMLR-LBFGS (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.716}\)), and Flow-SiMR-V (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.703}\)) isolate the weak shared component via cross-view alignment and non-negativity.

Figure 3.1 illustrates the synthetic mappings from latent coordinate \(\mathbf{U}\) to observable features \(\mathbf{X}\).

3.2 Global Non-Parametric Demšar Benchmark (\(B = 12\) Datasets, \(324\) Evaluations)

To establish a rigorous empirical ranking across all 12 dataset blocks (\(12 \text{ data regimes} \times 3 \text{ random seeds} \times 9 \text{ models} = 324\) complete evaluations), replicates are first averaged within each \((\text{Dataset}, \text{Model})\) cell via build_ranking_blocks, yielding \(B = 12\) independent paired blocks across \(k = 9\) architectures. At \(\alpha = 0.05\), the two-tailed Nemenyi Critical Difference across \(k = 9\) models and \(B = 12\) dataset blocks is \(CD = 3.468\) rank units.

Across both primary evaluation probes—the Global Consensus Probe (\(\mathbf{U}\)) and the Strictly Linear OLEE Basis Probe (\(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\))—the non-parametric Friedman and Iman–Davenport tests confirm highly significant architectural separation (\(\text{df}_1 = 8, \text{df}_2 = 88\)):

  • Global Consensus Probe (Predictive_Metric on \(\mathbf{U}\)): \[\chi_F^2 = 32.06 \quad (p = 9.10 \times 10^{-5}), \qquad F_F = 5.51 \quad (p = 1.19 \times 10^{-5})\]
  • Strictly Linear OLEE Basis Probe (Strictly_Linear_Metric on \(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\)): \[\chi_F^2 = 30.77 \quad (p = 1.54 \times 10^{-4}), \qquad F_F = 5.19 \quad (p = 2.54 \times 10^{-5})\]

As reported in Table 3.2 and Figure 3.2:

  1. Hybrid Linear-Encoder Deep Decoder (LEND, #1 Consensus Rank \(3.58\)): LEND achieves the best global consensus Friedman rank (\(3.58\)), the highest mean consensus predictive score (\(0.760 \pm 0.249\)), #1 synthetic rank (\(2.50\) on XV), and the highest ground-truth latent recovery (\(CMC = 0.907\)), alongside \(0.899\) sparse support recovery.
  2. Bound-Constrained Quasi-Newton Solver (SiMLR-LBFGSB, #1 OLEE Basis Rank \(3.17\), #2 Consensus Rank \(3.62\)): SiMLR-LBFGSB achieves the #1 global Friedman rank on the strictly linear OLEE basis probe (\(3.17\), and #1 on the 6 real-world cohorts with mean rank \(2.50\) and highest mean score \(0.777\)), the #2 global consensus rank (\(3.62\)), and the highest sparse feature support recovery across the benchmark (\(\text{Support } F_1 = \mathbf{0.991}\)) with exact zero frame defect (\(D = 0.0000\)).
  3. Bijective Normalizing Flow with Detached Prior (Flow-SiMR-V, #3 Consensus Rank \(4.17\), #3 Basis Rank \(4.12\)): With detached-prior subspace decoupling (Stage 5), Flow-SiMR-V rises to #3 across the 12-dataset suite (\(0.752 \pm 0.245\) consensus mean, #1 real-cohort consensus mean \(0.627\), \(0.771\) linear probe, \(0.876\) \(CMC\), \(0.826\) support recovery), leading Polynomial (\(0.925\)), TCGA-KIRC (\(69.7\%\)), and mfeat consensus (\(81.9\%\)).
  4. Riemannian Quasi-Newton & First-Order Solvers (SiMLR-LBFGS #2 Basis Rank \(4.00\) / #4 Consensus Rank \(4.25\); SiMLR #5 Basis Rank \(4.50\)): SiMLR-LBFGS achieves #2 rank on the OLEE basis probe (\(4.00\), real-cohort rank \(3.33\)) and \(0.966\) sparse support recovery.
  5. Single-Matrix Turnkey SVD & Dense Linear Baselines (PCA Consensus Rank \(4.50\), NSAFlow-PCA \(4.88\)): NSAFlow-PCA executes in \(0.040\text{s}\) per fit—matching PCA (\(0.043\text{s}\))—while enforcing \(25.0\%\) disjoint sparsity with zero signed crosstalk and leading Heart (\(84.8\%\) linear probe), Diabetes (\(R^2 = 0.382\)), and TCGA-BRCA (\(92.7\%\)). However, on multi-view regimes with view-private noise (Shared+Private) or high-cardinality view structure (mfeat, ADNI-MCI), single-matrix concatenation lags cross-view OLEE alignment (XV Rank \(7.46\) and \(7.67\)).
  6. Collapse of Unconstrained Deep Encoders (NED Rank \(7.29\), NEDPP Rank \(8.04\)): Multi-layer perceptron encoders rank last across the 12-dataset suite (\(0.593\) and \(0.589\) consensus mean; \(CMC = 0.589\) and \(0.652\)) and are significantly outperformed by every OLEE-constrained model under Holm–Bonferroni corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (\(p_{\text{Holm}} \le 7.5 \times 10^{-4}\), Table 3.3).

3.2.1 Multi-Objective 4-Axis Pareto Frontier Analysis

Models are evaluated across four complementary operational axes (Figure 3.3):

  1. Downstream Predictive Capacity (\(Y\)): Cross-validated out-of-sample \(R^2\) or diagnostic classification accuracy.
  2. Ground-Truth Latent Discovery (\(CMC\) on \(\mathbf{U}\)): Canonical Mean Correlation with the true generative latent coordinates \(\mathbf{S}\).
  3. Sparse Feature Support Recovery (\(F_1\) on \(\mathbf{V}\)): Jaccard/F1 overlap with true localized feature blocks, alongside zero signed crosstalk (\(\|\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^-\|_1 = 0\)).
  4. Computational Throughput (\(t\)): Wall-clock execution time per fit.

3.3 Testing Stage 1: The Input Boundary — OLEE Geometry, Support Recovery & Attribution Audits

Stage 1 of our deductive formulation predicts that restricting the input boundary to a Stiefel projection \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) regularized by nsa_flow achieves three simultaneous properties: (1) near-zero per-view frame defect \(D(\mathbf{V}_m)\) and exact zero signed lobe crosstalk (\(\|\mathbf{V}_m^+ \odot \mathbf{V}_m^-\|_1 = 0\)), (2) accurate recovery of disjoint sparse feature supports (Support_Recovery_V) where dense SVD/PCA is rotationally blind, and (3) exact individual-patient feature attribution without post-hoc surrogate error.

3.3.1 Sparse Disjoint Support Recovery & Subspace Identifiability

As shown in Table 3.2 and Figure 3.4:

  • Exact Disjoint Support Recovery on NonnegParts & NonnegPartsWeak: Because PCA is invariant to orthogonal rotations inside \(\text{span}(\mathbf{V})\), its dense mixed-sign basis scores at the random-partition chance floor (\(\text{Support } F_1 = \mathbf{0.333}\)). In contrast, bound-constrained SiMLR-LBFGSB recovers the true disjoint non-negative support with \(\text{Support } F_1 = \mathbf{0.991}\) (and SiMLR-LBFGS with \(\mathbf{0.966}\), LEND with \(\mathbf{0.899}\), and Flow-SiMR-V with \(\mathbf{0.826}\)). On NonnegPartsWeak (where the target depends on the weakest shared factor \(u_3^*\)), LEND (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.732}\)), SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.716}\)), SiMLR-LBFGS (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.716}\)), and Flow-SiMR-V (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.703}\)) outperform PCA (\(R^2 = 0.522\)) by nearly \(+20\) percentage points.
  • Exact Geometric Invariants: Across all 324 evaluations, signed lobe crosstalk (\(\|\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^-\|_1\)) is identically \(0.0000\), and per-view Stiefel frame defect \(D(\mathbf{V}_m)\) is bounded below \(10^{-4}\) for LEND, SiMLR-LBFGSB, SiMLR-LBFGS, and Flow-SiMR-V.

3.3.2 Clinical Attribution Audits: Direct Linear Derivatives vs. Post-Hoc Surrogates

Because the OLEE input mapping is an isometry (\(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{V}_m\)), individual-patient attributions \(\mathbf{c}_{m,ik} = \mathbf{x}_{m,i} \odot \mathbf{v}_{m,k}\) are exact and require zero perturbation sampling.

3.4 Testing Stage 2: Cross-View Consensus, Bipartite Topologies & ADNI-MCI Mediation

Stage 2 of our deductive formulation establishes that cross-view consensus topology (star, loo, and directed/bipartite graph) and scale-profiled alignment energies govern whether multi-view models isolate shared biological signal from view-private variance and background anatomy.

3.4.1 Cross-View Noise Rejection (Shared+Private) and LOO vs. Star Regularization

  1. Asymmetric View-Private Noise (Shared+Private): When View 0 (\(p_1=160\)) carries heightened private variance (\(\sigma_{\text{priv},1} = 0.80\)), single-matrix concatenation (PCA \(R^2 = 0.639, CMC = 0.636\); NSAFlow-PCA \(R^2 = 0.629, CMC = 0.636\)) rotates its leading components toward View 0’s private subspace. By contrast, cross-view consensus in LEND (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.949}, CMC = \mathbf{0.896}\)) and Flow-SiMR-V (\(R^2 = \mathbf{0.845}, CMC = \mathbf{0.880}\), linear probe \(0.908\)) rejects View 0’s private noise and recovers all three shared factors.
  2. Leave-One-Out (LOO) vs. Star Topology: In Star topology, modality \(m\) aligns to a consensus \(\mathbf{U}\) that includes its own projection \(\mathbf{Z}_m\). In LOO topology, modality \(m\) aligns exclusively against \(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\), narrowing the train-test generalization gap on clinical cohorts (Figure 3.8 and Figure 3.9).

3.4.2 ADNI-MCI 4-View Investigation (\(N = 1,084\)): Bipartite Protocol B Sweep & Cognitive Mediation

We conduct two complementary investigations on the 4-view ADNI-MCI cohort (\(N = 1,084\) participants with Mild Cognitive Impairment; 3 structural MRI views totaling \(382\) features plus View 4 clinical/cognitive battery):

3.4.2.1 1. The 1,214-Combination Bipartite Graph (Protocol B) Hyperparameter Sweep

To test Stage 2’s bipartite graph hypothesis systematically, we evaluated 1,214 combinations of optimizer (lookahead, lars, bidirectional_armijo_gradient, armijo_gradient, riemannian, nsa_lbfgsb, torch_lbfgs, adam, adamw), alignment energy (nc, procrustes, align, acc, recon_r2, cca, logcosh, exp), consensus mixing (ica, svd, newton, pca, avg), and spectral triage (MAI off, procrustes_r2, cca, rvcoef, rank_procrustes_r2) under the bipartite graph topology (path_graph = {0:[3], 1:[3], 2:[3], 3:[0,1,2]}) on the 11 \(>98\%\)-complete cognitive measures (adni_mci_1214_grid_sweep.csv, summarized in Table 3.4). At test time, View 4 is withheld and out-of-sample CogPC1 R^2 is evaluated strictly from the 3 MRI views’ linear projections \([\mathbf{X}_{0,\text{test}}\mathbf{V}_0, \mathbf{X}_{1,\text{test}}\mathbf{V}_1, \mathbf{X}_{2,\text{test}}\mathbf{V}_2]\) after in-fold FWL covariate adjustment for age, sex, and education.

As shown in Table 3.4:

  • Every OLEE Architecture Family Reaches CogPC1 R^2 \(\approx 0.22\text{–}0.24\) from Brain MRI Alone: Linear Stiefel solvers (SiMLR-Lookahead \(0.2407\), SiMLR-LARS \(0.2296\), SiMLR-BiArmijo \(0.2277\), SiMLR-LBFGSB \(0.2212\)), deep OLEE models (NEDPP \(0.2357\), NED \(0.2237\), LEND \(0.2190\)), and detached-prior normalizing flows (Flow-SiMR-V \(0.2341\)) all substantially outperform single-matrix concatenation (PCA \(0.1786\) on Seed 42, \(0.1522\) 3-seed mean) by steering the three MRI bases \(\mathbf{V}_0, \mathbf{V}_1, \mathbf{V}_2\) directly toward the cognitive target view during training.

3.4.2.2 2. Why Predicting Clinical Dementia (CDR-SB) Directly from Brain MRI Needs Cognitive Mediation

When predicting clinical dementia severity (CDR-SB) directly from the 382 structural brain MRI measurements alone (Protocol A), out-of-sample accuracy hits a biological ceiling of \(R^2 = 0.0411 \pm 0.0248\) (\(\rho = 0.2203, p = 1.2 \times 10^{-4}\)). Classical Baron–Kenny mediation testing across \(N = 1,084\) MCI participants (\(N_{\text{test}} = 326\)) reveals why:

  1. Path \(a\) (Brain Atrophy \(\to\) Cognitive Loss): Multi-view brain MRI strongly predicts domain cognitive performance (\(\alpha = 1.000 \pm 0.081, t = 12.31, p < 10^{-25}, R^2 = 26.13\%, \rho = 0.5335\)).
  2. Path \(b\) (Cognitive Loss \(\to\) Daily Functional Disability): Cognitive performance directly drives CDR-SB (\(\beta = 0.013 \pm 0.003, t = 4.69, p < 10^{-15}\)).
  3. Direct Bypass Path \(c'\) Vanishes: Controlling for cognition, the direct MRI-to-CDR-SB path is indistinguishable from zero (\(\gamma' = 0.002, t = 0.88, p = 0.38\)), with \(PM = 84.5\%\text{–}86.4\%\) of the brain-atrophy effect mediated through cognition (Sobel \(z = 4.38, p < 10^{-15}\)).

As shown in Table 3.5 and Table 3.6, modeling the two-step mediated pathway yields a \(6.44\times\) gain for LEND-SiMR + MAI (\(R^2 = 0.2648 \pm 0.0332\)) and a \(5.94\times\) gain in \(0.86\text{s}\) for SiMLR (\(R^2 = 0.2443 \pm 0.0534\)), whereas unconstrained deep encoders (NED-SiMR + MAI \(R^2 = 0.1127\); NED-Shared-Private \(R^2 = 0.0840\)) overfit the 382 MRI features and collapse on held-out patients.

3.5 Testing Stage 3: Linear Multi-View Manifold Solvers & Complexity Scaling

Stage 3 of our deductive progression compares first-order Riemannian gradient descent (SiMLR) against quasi-Newton (SiMLR-LBFGS) and bound-constrained quasi-Newton (SiMLR-LBFGSB) solvers on \(\prod_m \text{St}(k, p_m)\):

  1. Superior Real-Cohort Linear Basis Recovery (SiMLR-LBFGSB #1 Rank \(2.50\), SiMLR-LBFGS #2 Rank \(3.33\)): Across the 6 real-world biomedical cohorts (Heart, Diabetes, MultiOmics, TCGA-KIRC, ADNI-MCI, mfeat), SiMLR-LBFGSB and SiMLR-LBFGS rank #1 and #2 on the strictly linear OLEE probe (\(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\) mean \(0.670\) and \(0.663\) vs. \(0.612\) for PCA), and achieve \(0.991\) and \(0.966\) sparse support recovery on the non-negative synthetic regimes.
  2. Complexity Scaling (\(\mathcal{O}(N)\) in Sample Size, \(\mathcal{O}(K^3)\) in Rank): Figure 3.10 confirms that execution wall-time scales linearly with sample size \(N\) (\(\mathcal{O}(N)\)) and cubically with latent rank \(K\) (\(\mathcal{O}(K^3)\) for the \(k \times k\) Gram and Sylvester/Newton-Schulz operations), remaining highly efficient across typical biomedical ranks (\(K \le 64\)).

3.6 Testing Stage 4: Non-Linear Decoding Downstream of OLEE (LEND vs. NED & NEDPP)

Stage 4 of our deductive formulation predicts that keeping the encoder strictly linear (\(\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\)) while placing non-linear MLP capacity in the decoder (LEND) captures curved generative manifolds without suffering the high-dimensional encoder overfitting that afflicts unconstrained deep encoders (NED, NEDPP):

  • Why LEND Ranks #1 on Global Consensus (\(3.58\)) and Synthetic OLEE Basis (\(2.50\)): Across the 12-dataset benchmark, LEND achieves the highest mean consensus score (\(0.760 \pm 0.249\)), the highest ground-truth latent recovery (\(CMC = \mathbf{0.907}\)), and the #1 synthetic rank (\(2.50\) on XV), outperforming NED by \(+0.1669\) (\(p_{\text{Holm}} = 2.7 \times 10^{-5}\)) and NEDPP by \(+0.1710\) (\(p_{\text{Holm}} = 2.7 \times 10^{-5}\)).
  • Why NED and NEDPP Collapse on Shared+Private and NonnegPartsWeak: When encoder MLPs \(f_{\text{enc},m}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m)\) are inserted before consensus formation, they warp view-private noise to artificially match the consensus target during training, causing test-set consensus \(R^2\) to collapse to \(0.429\) (NED) and \(0.523\) (NEDPP) on Shared+Private (vs. \(0.949\) for LEND), and to \(0.292\) (NED) and \(0.222\) (NEDPP) on NonnegPartsWeak (vs. \(0.732\) for LEND).

3.7 Testing Stage 5: Bijective Normalizing Flows Downstream of OLEE (Flow-SiMR-V)

Stage 5 of our deductive progression pairs the OLEE bottleneck \(\mathbf{V}_m\) with volume-preserving RealNVP normalizing flows \(f_{\text{flow},m}: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k\).

3.7.1 Empirical Verification of Detached-Prior Subspace Decoupling

As proved in Chapter 2 (Stage 5) and Appendix C, backpropagating Gaussian prior log-likelihood gradients (\(\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}\)) through a rectangular Stiefel projection (\(k \ll p_m\)) lacks a subspace log-determinant term and drives \(\mathbf{V}_m\) onto the trailing (smallest-eigenvalue) noise eigenvectors of \(\mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m\). Enforcing detached-prior subspace decoupling (\(\text{sg}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}})\) at the prior boundary, coupled with iteration-0 linear Stiefel warm start and zero-initialized identity coupling layers):

  • Raises Flow-SiMR-V out-of-sample CogPC1 R^2 on ADNI-MCI (Protocol B) from \(0.0369\) (collapsed trailing-eigenvector subspace) to \(0.2341\) at \(k=3\) (\(0.2966\) at \(k=6\), a \(+704\%\) improvement).
  • Propels Flow-SiMR-V to #3 overall Friedman rank across all 12 benchmark datasets (Consensus Rank \(4.17\), OLEE Basis Rank \(4.12\), #1 real-cohort consensus mean \(0.627\)), achieving the #1 consensus accuracy on mfeat (\(81.9\%\)), #1 on TCGA-KIRC (\(69.7\%\)), and #1 on Polynomial (\(R^2 = 0.925\)).
