Integrating heterogeneous, high-dimensional observations remains a fundamental task in computational anatomy, neurodegenerative disease modeling, and translational molecular oncology (Stone et al. 2020; Avants et al. 2021). As diagnostic and clinical trial cohorts incorporate multi-sequence structural and diffusion MRI, molecular positron emission tomography, biofluid assays, and high-throughput transcriptomics (Weiner et al. 2017; Jack et al. 2008; Marek et al. 2011), representation learning methods must balance the capacity to model non-linear manifold geometry against the legal, ethical, and scientific necessity for verifiable physical biomarker attribution (Holmberg et al. 2022). In neurotrauma, blast-induced shockwaves produce diffuse microstructural shearing across white matter tracts that demands sparse, signed decomposition without cancellation artifacts (Stone et al. 2024). In cancer genomics, resolving functional oncogenic pathways across somatic copy-number alterations, mRNA expression, and targeted protein arrays in breast carcinoma (BRCA) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; Cancer Discovery 2016) and clear cell renal cell carcinoma (KIRC) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network 2013) requires multi-modal alignment that preserves exact gene-level attribution.

In this work, we formulated multi-view representation learning deductively across five methodological stages governed by the Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) contract (Chapter 2) and evaluated every stage empirically across 12 multi-modal data regimes (6 real clinical/genomic/imaging cohorts and 6 synthetic manifolds across 3 random seeds, totaling 324 model evaluations), alongside a 1,214-combination hyperparameter sweep and a 10-seed mediation study in Alzheimer’s disease (\(N=1,084\)).

4.1 Deductive Synthesis Across the Five Methodological Stages

Our theoretical and empirical results demonstrate how each of the five methodological stages resolves a distinct failure mode in multi-view representation learning:

  1. Stage 1 — The Input Boundary (OLEE & NSA-Flow Manifold Geometry): Restricting the entrypoint of each modality \(m\) to the compact Stiefel manifold (\(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k\)) guarantees globally constant attribution derivatives (\(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{V}_m\)) and closed-form counterfactual recourse (\(\delta \mathbf{x}_m^* = \Delta \mathbf{z}_m^* \mathbf{V}_m^\top\)) (Pearl 2009; Wachter et al. 2017). Coupling the Stiefel constraint with the Non-negative Stiefel Approximating Flow (nsa_flow) enforces zero signed lobe crosstalk (\(\|\mathbf{V}_m^+ \odot \mathbf{V}_m^-\|_1 = 0\)) and recovers localized sparse feature supports (SiMLR-LBFGSB \(F_1 = 0.991\), SiMLR-LBFGS \(0.966\), LEND \(0.899\), Flow-SiMR-V \(0.826\)) where rotationally invariant PCA fails (\(F_1 = 0.333\)).
  2. Stage 2 — Cross-View Consensus, Bipartite Topologies & Spectral Triage (MAI): Cross-view alignment isolates shared biological variance when single-matrix concatenation is overwhelmed by view-private noise (Shared+Private: LEND \(R^2 = 0.949, CMC = 0.896\) and Flow-SiMR-V \(R^2 = 0.845, CMC = 0.880\) vs. PCA \(R^2 = 0.639, CMC = 0.636\)). Crucially, when predictor views are aligned against a training-only clinical target view via a bipartite graph (Protocol B on ADNI-MCI, {0:[3], 1:[3], 2:[3], 3:[0,1,2]}), the predictor bases \(\mathbf{V}_m\) are steered directly toward the disease axis without requiring clinical labels at test time (CogPC1 R^2 \(= 0.224\text{–}0.241\) vs. \(0.152\) for PCA).
  3. Stage 3 — Linear Multi-View Manifold Solvers & Iteration-0 Warm Start (SiMLR, SiMLR-LBFGS, SiMLR-LBFGSB): Bound-constrained quasi-Newton optimization (SiMLR-LBFGSB) and Riemannian L-BFGS (SiMLR-LBFGS) achieve the #1 and #2 global Friedman ranks on the strictly linear OLEE basis probe across all 12 datasets (\(3.17\) and \(4.00\); #1 and #2 on the 6 real-world cohorts with mean ranks \(2.50\) and \(3.33\)), while providing the fast iteration-0 linear warm start for all deep and flow architectures.
  4. Stage 4 — Non-Linear Decoding Downstream of OLEE (LEND vs. NED / NEDPP): Keeping the encoder strictly linear (\(\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\)) while placing non-linear MLP capacity exclusively in the decoder (LEND) achieves the #1 global Friedman rank on consensus prediction (\(3.58\), mean \(0.760 \pm 0.249\)) and the highest latent coordinate recovery (\(CMC = 0.907\)). Conversely, inserting MLP layers inside the encoder (NED rank \(7.29\), NEDPP rank \(8.04\)) causes severe overfitting in sample-limited cohorts (\(p_{\text{Holm}} \le 7.5 \times 10^{-4}\)).
  5. Stage 5 — Bijective Normalizing Flows & Detached-Prior Subspace Decoupling (Flow-SiMR-V): We proved that backpropagating standard Gaussian prior log-likelihood gradients (\(\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}\)) through a rectangular Stiefel projection (\(k \ll p_m\)) lacks a subspace log-determinant term and drives \(\mathbf{V}_m\) onto the trailing (smallest-eigenvalue) noise eigenvectors of \(\mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m\). Detaching the prior gradient at the OLEE boundary (\(\text{sg}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}})\)) resolves this collapse, boosting Flow-SiMR-V CogPC1 R^2 on ADNI-MCI by \(+704\%\) (from \(0.0369\) to \(0.2966\) at \(k=6\), \(0.2341\) at \(k=3\)) and elevating Flow-SiMR-V to #3 overall rank across all 12 benchmark datasets while preserving exact analytical invertibility, conditional cross-view imputation, and geodesic trajectory mapping.

4.2 Non-Parametric Architectural Ranking and Multi-Objective Pareto Frontier

Evaluating multi-modal representations requires non-parametric statistical inference that respects dataset-level block structure rather than pooling across replicate seeds (Demšar 2006). Across the \(B = 12\) independent dataset blocks and \(k = 9\) evaluated architectures (\(324\) evaluations), the non-parametric Friedman and Iman–Davenport tests confirm highly significant separation across both evaluation probes (\(\text{df}_1 = 8, \text{df}_2 = 88\), Nemenyi \(CD = 3.468\) at \(\alpha = 0.05\) (Iman and Davenport 1980; Nemenyi 1963)):

  • Consensus Prediction (\(\mathbf{U}\)): \(\chi_F^2 = 32.06 \; (p = 9.10 \times 10^{-5}), \quad F_F = 5.51 \; (p = 1.19 \times 10^{-5})\).
  • Strictly Linear OLEE Basis (\(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\)): \(\chi_F^2 = 30.77 \; (p = 1.54 \times 10^{-4}), \quad F_F = 5.19 \; (p = 2.54 \times 10^{-5})\).

4.3 Why Cognitive Mediation and Bipartite Alignment Multiply ADNI-MCI Prediction

Our investigation of \(N = 1,084\) patients with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) from the ADNI cohort (Weiner et al. 2017; Jack et al. 2008) highlights two general methodological principles for clinical neuroimaging:

  1. Bipartite Target Alignment (Protocol B) vs. Unsupervised Anatomical Alignment (Protocol A): When the three structural MRI views (\(382\) features) are aligned only with each other (Protocol A), their top shared components reflect global brain size and healthy aging rather than cognitive decline (PCA CogPC1 R^2 \(= 0.1522\)). Aligning each MRI view directly against the 11 \(>98\%\)-complete cognitive measures during training via a bipartite graph ({0:[3], 1:[3], 2:[3], 3:[0,1,2]})—while withholding View 4 at test time—steers the MRI projections \(\mathbf{V}_0, \mathbf{V}_1, \mathbf{V}_2\) onto the cortical atrophy signature of cognitive impairment, raising MRI-only test CogPC1 R^2 to \(0.2237\) (SiMLR-LBFGSB, 3-seed mean) and \(0.2407\) (SiMLR-Lookahead in the 1,214-combination sweep).
  2. Over 6-Fold Gain on Functional Dementia Severity (CDR-SB) via Cognitive Mediation: Trying to predict daily functional disability (CDR-SB) directly from the 382 brain MRI features explains only 4.1% of test variance (\(R^2 = 0.0411 \pm 0.0248\)). Biologically, hippocampal and cortical tissue loss first erodes mental abilities (memory recall, executive attention), and that loss of mental ability impairs daily functioning (\(84.5\%\text{–}86.4\%\) indirect effect, Sobel \(p < 10^{-15}\)). Modeling this two-step pathway (\(\text{Brain MRI} \to \text{Cognition} \to \text{CDR-SB}\)) increases out-of-sample CDR-SB accuracy over six-fold (LEND-SiMR + MAI: \(R^2 = 0.2648 \pm 0.0332\); SiMLR: \(R^2 = 0.2443 \pm 0.0534\) in \(0.86\text{s}\)), whereas unconstrained deep encoders (NED-SiMR + MAI: \(R^2 = 0.1127\)) overfit the 382 anatomical features.

4.4 Clinical Translation Across Cohorts and Translational Pathology

The modular Deep SiMLR framework addresses distinct clinical and biological challenges across the six real-world cohorts:

  1. Cleveland Heart Disease (Heart, \(N=297\)): Integrating resting vitals (\(p_1 = 7\)) and stress electrocardiography (\(p_2 = 6\)) achieves \(84.8\%\) strictly linear probe accuracy (NSAFlow-PCA) and \(83.7\%\) consensus accuracy (NSAFlow-PCA, PCA, SiMLR-LBFGSB), directly attributing risk to exercise-induced ST depression (oldpeak), maximum heart rate (thalach), and chest pain type (cp).
  2. Diabetes Progression (Diabetes, \(N=442\)): Integrating anthropometric/hemodynamic baselines (\(p_1 = 5\)) and serum lipid chemistries (\(p_2 = 5\)), NSAFlow-PCA (\(R^2 = 0.382\)) and SiMLR-LBFGSB / SiMLR-LBFGS (linear probe \(R^2 = 0.396\)) outperform dense PCA (\(0.350\)) while isolating body mass index (bmi) and serum triglycerides (s5).
  3. Breast and Renal Translational Oncology (TCGA-BRCA \(N=822\), TCGA-KIRC \(N=467\)): Enforcing \(\ge 98\%\) completeness across mRNA (\(p_1=200\)), CNV (\(p_2=150\)), and RPPA proteomics (\(p_3=150\)), NSAFlow-PCA (\(92.7\%\)), SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(92.6\%\) linear probe), and LEND (\(92.6\%\) linear probe, \(92.2\%\) consensus) accurately stratify Estrogen Receptor status in BRCA, while Flow-SiMR-V and LEND lead pathologic stage classification in KIRC (\(69.7\%\)).
  4. High-Cardinality Multi-View Pattern Recognition (mfeat, \(N=2,000\)): Across 4 heterogeneous descriptor views, Flow-SiMR-V (\(81.9\%\)) and LEND (\(78.9\%\)) lead 3D consensus classification, while SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(94.8\%\)) and NEDPP (\(96.2\%\)) outperform concatenated PCA (\(72.7\%\)) by over \(+22\) percentage points on the OLEE basis probe.
  5. Neurotrauma and Longitudinal Parkinson’s Cohorts (TBI & PPMI): In blast-induced diffuse axonal injury (Stone et al. 2024) and longitudinal dopaminergic imaging (Marek et al. 2011), NSAFlow-PCA and SiMLR-LBFGSB enforce disjoint non-negative support (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)), isolating tract-specific white matter shearing and striatal binding changes without destructive sign cancellation.

4.5 Methodological Principles for Multi-Modal Biomedical Studies

Our experiments establish four general methodological rules for multi-modal biomedical benchmarks:

  1. In-Fold Preprocessing & Covariate Residualization: Scaling, feature selection, and Frisch–Waugh–Lovell covariate residualization must be fitted strictly on the training fold and projected onto the test fold.
  2. Completeness Hygiene (\(\ge 98\%\)) Before Multi-View Alignment: Sparse clinical or genomic variables with high missingness must be filtered before median imputation so constant-imputed columns cannot corrupt cross-view covariance matrices.
  3. Bipartite Target Topologies for Partial-View Clinical Deployment: When a target view is present during training but absent at deployment, aligning predictor views exclusively to the target view via a bipartite graph topology prevents background inter-predictor covariance from dominating the learned basis.
  4. Detached-Prior Subspace Decoupling for Bottlenecked Normalizing Flows: Whenever a normalizing flow operates downstream of a rectangular Stiefel projection (\(k \ll p_m\)), the Gaussian prior log-likelihood must be detached at the linear boundary (\(\text{sg}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}})\)) so prior gradients cannot drive \(\mathbf{V}_m\) onto trailing noise eigenvectors.

4.6 Limitations

  1. First-Layer Linear Constraint: Restricting the input layer to an orthogonal Stiefel projection limits the model’s ability to capture high-order intra-modality non-linear feature interactions directly at the raw pixel or token level.
  2. Latent Dimension Scaling: Exact Stiefel retractions and polar factorizations scale cubically with latent rank (\(\mathcal{O}(k^3)\)). While fast for typical biomedical latent ranks (\(k \le 64\)), scaling to \(k > 500\) requires block-Stiefel approximations.
  3. Sample Size in Clinical Sub-Cohorts: While \(B = 12\) dataset blocks (\(324\) evaluations) provide strong non-parametric power (\(\chi_F^2 = 30.77\text{–}32.06, p \le 1.54 \times 10^{-4}\)), fine-grained differences among top OLEE variants on small single-center cohorts benefit from paired permutation and multi-seed cross-validation.

4.7 Conclusion

Deep SiMLR establishes a deductive, modular framework for multi-modal representation learning governed by the Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) contract. By decoupling linear Stiefel feature attribution (\(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\)) from downstream cross-view consensus topologies, Riemannian quasi-Newton solvers (SiMLR-LBFGSB, SiMLR-LBFGS), non-linear decoders (LEND), and detached-prior normalizing flows (Flow-SiMR-V), the architecture eliminates the clinical transparency penalty (\(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{V}_m\)) and enables closed-form counterfactual recourse (\(\delta \mathbf{x}_m^* = \Delta \mathbf{z}_m^* \mathbf{V}_m^\top\)).

Across 324 benchmark evaluations spanning 6 real clinical/genomic/imaging cohorts and 6 synthetic manifolds, hybrid LEND (#1 consensus rank \(3.58\), \(CMC = 0.907\)), bound-constrained SiMLR-LBFGSB (#1 OLEE basis rank \(3.17\), #1 real-cohort rank \(2.50\), support \(F_1 = 0.991\)), and detached-prior Flow-SiMR-V (#3 consensus rank \(4.17\), #3 basis rank \(4.12\), #1 real-cohort consensus mean \(0.627\)) lead overall performance, while NSAFlow-PCA (\(0.040\text{s}\)) matches dense PCA in speed while enforcing \(25.0\%\) disjoint sparsity with zero crosstalk. Every OLEE-constrained architecture significantly outperforms unconstrained deep autoencoders (NED, NEDPP, \(p_{\text{Holm}} \le 7.5 \times 10^{-4}\)). In ADNI-MCI (\(N=1,084\)), bipartite target alignment and cognitive mediation unlock a \(+47\%\text{–}58\%\) gain in MRI-only cognitive factor prediction (CogPC1 R^2 \(= 0.224\text{–}0.241\)) and a \(>6\)-fold gain in predicting clinical dementia severity (\(R^2 = 0.2648, p < 10^{-15}\)).
