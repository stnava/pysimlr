Integrating heterogeneous, high-dimensional observations remains a fundamental task in computational anatomy, neurodegenerative disease modeling, and translational molecular oncology (Stone et al. 2020; Avants et al. 2021). As diagnostic and clinical trial cohorts incorporate multi-sequence structural and diffusion MRI, molecular positron emission tomography, biofluid assays, and high-throughput transcriptomics (Weiner et al. 2017; Jack et al. 2008; Marek et al. 2011), representation learning methods must balance the capacity to model non-linear manifold geometry against the legal, ethical, and scientific necessity for verifiable physical biomarker attribution (Holmberg et al. 2022). In neurotrauma, blast-induced shockwaves produce diffuse microstructural shearing across white matter tracts that demands sparse, signed decomposition without cancellation artifacts (Stone et al. 2024). In cancer genomics, resolving functional oncogenic pathways across somatic copy-number alterations, mRNA expression, and targeted protein arrays in breast carcinoma (BRCA) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; Cancer Discovery 2016) and clear cell renal cell carcinoma (KIRC) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network 2013) requires multi-modal alignment that preserves exact gene-level attribution.

In this work, we formulated multi-view representation learning as a constrained variational problem governed by the Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) contract, coupling an initial linear Stiefel projection with downstream Riemannian optimizers, non-linear decoders, and volume-preserving normalizing flows. Through evaluation across 9 distinct data regimes (3 real clinical/genomic cohorts and 6 synthetic manifolds across 3 random seeds, totaling 243 model evaluations), alongside dedicated investigations of 4-view cognitive mediation in Alzheimer’s disease (\(N=1,084\)), we characterize the operational boundaries of multi-modal integration and establish empirical guidelines for biomedical deployment.

4.1 Non-Parametric Architectural Ranking and Empirical Synthesis

Evaluating multi-modal representations requires non-parametric statistical inference that respects dataset-level block structure rather than pooling across replicate seeds (Demšar 2006). Across the \(B = 9\) independent dataset blocks and \(k = 9\) evaluated architectures, the non-parametric Friedman test confirms highly significant performance differences (\(\chi_F^2 = 32.47, p = 7.68 \times 10^{-5}\)), corroborated by the Iman–Davenport extension (\(F_F = 6.57, p = 3.13 \times 10^{-6}\), \(\text{df}_1 = 8, \text{df}_2 = 64\)) (Iman and Davenport 1980). With nine models and nine dataset blocks, the two-tailed Nemenyi Critical Difference at \(\alpha = 0.05\) is \(CD = 4.004\) (Nemenyi 1963), and the median between-model spread-to-noise ratio is \(6.15\times\).

The empirical rankings establish four clear architectural tiers:

  1. Box-Constrained Riemannian & Deep Hybrid Frontier (SiMLR-LBFGSB, LEND): SiMLR-LBFGSB achieves the best overall predictive rank (\(3.28\), mean predictive score \(0.795 \pm 0.201\), linear probe \(0.830\)) and the highest sparse support recovery across the suite (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.942\)) with near-zero per-view Stiefel frame defect (\(D = 2 \times 10^{-5}\)). LEND achieves rank \(3.56\) and leads the benchmark in mean predictive accuracy (\(0.830 \pm 0.189\)), strictly linear probe (\(0.836\)), axis-aligned Random Forest probe (\(0.830\)), and latent coordinate recovery (\(CMC = 0.907\)), excelling on Shared+Private (\(R^2 = 0.949\) vs \(0.639\) for PCA) and NonnegPartsWeak (\(R^2 = 0.732\) vs \(0.522\) for PCA).
  2. Ultra-Fast Turnkey SVD & Dense Linear Tier (NSAFlow-PCA, PCA): NSAFlow-PCA achieves rank \(3.61\) (\(0.782 \pm 0.200\), \(CMC = 0.834\)) in \(0.029\) seconds per fit—matching PCA (rank \(3.78\), \(0.774 \pm 0.213\), \(0.024\text{s}\)) in speed while outperforming PCA on Diabetes (\(R^2 = 0.382\) vs \(0.350\)) and TCGA-BRCA Multi-Omics (\(92.7\%\) vs \(91.9\%\)) and enforcing \(22.4\%\) disjoint sparsity with zero signed crosstalk (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)).
  3. First-Order, Quasi-Newton & Invertible Flow Tier (SiMLR, SiMLR-LBFGS, Flow-SiMR-V): Classical SiMLR (rank \(4.17\), \(0.785 \pm 0.205\), \(0.363\text{s}\)) and quasi-Newton SiMLR-LBFGS (rank \(4.56\), \(0.797 \pm 0.193\), \(0.175\text{s}\), \(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.766\)) provide fast cross-view Stiefel convergence. Flow-SiMR-V (rank \(6.50\) on \(\mathbf{U}\)) achieves the second-highest strictly linear \(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\) probe across the entire benchmark (\(0.834\), including \(0.908\) on Shared+Private and \(0.744\) on NonnegPartsWeak) alongside \(0.883\) sparse support recovery and exact analytical invertibility.
  4. Dominated Unconstrained Deep Tier (NED, NEDPP): Fully non-linear autoencoders (NED, rank \(7.72\), \(0.618 \pm 0.227\); NEDPP, rank \(7.83\), \(0.633 \pm 0.249\)) rank last across the benchmark and are significantly outperformed by every OLEE-constrained model under Holm-Bonferroni corrected Wilcoxon signed-rank tests (\(p_{\text{Holm}} \le 0.018\)).

4.2 Multi-Objective Pareto Frontier Analysis

Architecture selection in biomedical discovery cannot be guided by predictive accuracy alone. We evaluate models across four complementary operational criteria:

  1. Downstream Predictive Capacity (\(Y\)) [Maximize]: Evaluated via cross-validated \(R^2\) or classification accuracy across all 9 benchmark tasks.
  2. Ground-Truth Latent Discovery (\(CMC\) on \(\mathbf{U}\)) [Maximize]: Evaluated via Canonical Mean Correlation between estimated consensus coordinates \(\mathbf{U}\) and true generative latents \(\mathbf{S}\) across the 6 synthetic regimes.
  3. Sparse Support Localization & Invariants (\(\text{Support } F_1, D, \text{Crosstalk}\)): Evaluated via Jaccard/F1 support overlap on sparse non-negative regimes, trace-normalized Stiefel Frame Defect \(D\), and signed lobe crosstalk \(\|\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^-\|_1 = 0.0000\).
  4. Computational Efficiency (\(t\)) [Minimize]: Evaluated via wall-clock fit time in seconds.

4.2.1 Structure of the Pareto Non-Dominated Set

The empirical results define five distinct operational regimes on the Pareto frontier:

  • The Predictive and Consensus Discovery Frontier (LEND): Achieving the highest predictive accuracy (\(0.830\)), highest linear probe (\(0.836\)), and highest latent coordinate recovery (\(CMC = 0.907\)), LEND combines a linear Stiefel encoder with a non-linear MLP decoder to isolate shared multi-view structure even when individual views carry heavy private noise (Shared+Private \(R^2 = 0.949\)).
  • The Sparse Support Localization Frontier (SiMLR-LBFGSB): Achieving the best overall Friedman rank (\(3.28\)) and highest sparse support recovery (\(F_1 = 0.942\)), SiMLR-LBFGSB enforces strict non-negative box constraints alongside Stiefel consolidation (NonnegPartsWeak \(R^2 = 0.715\) vs \(0.522\) for PCA).
  • The Ultra-Fast Turnkey Sparse Frontier (NSAFlow-PCA): Executing in \(0.029\text{s}\) with \(22.4\%\) disjoint sparsity, NSAFlow-PCA matches PCA (\(0.024\text{s}\)) in speed while outperforming it in predictive accuracy (\(0.782\) vs \(0.774\)) and providing signed, non-cancelling feature lobes.
  • The Fast Quasi-Newton Frontier (SiMLR-LBFGS): Executing in \(0.175\text{s}\) (\(2\times\) faster than first-order SiMLR), SiMLR-LBFGS delivers \(0.797\) predictive accuracy and \(0.766\) support recovery with Per-view frame defect \(D = 4.7 \times 10^{-4}\).
  • The Invertible Diffeomorphic Frontier (Flow-SiMR-V): Combining a linear Stiefel entrypoint with bijective RealNVP coupling layers, Flow-SiMR-V achieves \(0.834\) on the strictly linear \(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\) probe and \(0.883\) support recovery while supporting exact analytical inversion for geodesic trajectory mapping.

4.3 The Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) and Eliminating the Transparency Penalty

A central design requirement in medical imaging, oncology biomarker discovery, and clinical trials is that representation projections remain verifiable. In standard deep multi-view networks, deeply nested activation functions (\(\mathbf{Z} = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_2 \sigma(\mathbf{W}_1 \mathbf{X} + \mathbf{b}_1) + \mathbf{b}_2)\)) entangle features, precluding direct inspection of how physical measurements relate to latent scores (Rudin 2019). In clinical practice, this incurs an unacceptable transparency penalty: if a latent score stratifies a patient into an aggressive treatment arm, biostatisticians and clinicians must verify whether that score is driven by true anatomical atrophy or batch artifacts (Holmberg et al. 2022).

Post-hoc explainers (such as SHAP and LIME) attempt to circumvent this by querying local surrogate models on randomly perturbed inputs (Ribeiro et al. 2016; Lundberg and Lee 2017). In anatomical and physiological cohorts, post-hoc methods suffer from an out-of-distribution sampling defect: perturbing features independently creates synthetic points that violate physiological covariance (e.g., pairing impossible cortical thickness values or mutually exclusive transcript profiles). This instability can introduce false-positive biomarker attributions (Holmberg et al. 2022; Radulovic 2023).

Deep SiMLR enforces verifiable attribution by restricting the entrypoint of each modality to the Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) contract: \[ \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \text{subject to } \mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m) = \{\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}_k\} \] This architectural contract yields three mathematical and clinical guarantees:

  1. Globally Constant Attribution Gradients: The marginal contribution of feature \(j\) to latent component \(k\) is identically constant across the input domain: \[ \nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \frac{\partial Z_{m, ik}}{\partial X_{m, ij}} = V_{m, jk} \] Feature loadings are exact, closed-form, and independent of heuristic perturbation parameters.
  2. Closed-Form Counterfactual Recourse: Determining the minimal physical perturbation \(\delta \mathbf{x}_m\) required to produce a targeted therapeutic shift in latent coordinates \(\Delta \mathbf{z}_m\) reduces to an exact orthogonal projection: \[ \delta \mathbf{x}_m^* = \Delta \mathbf{z}_m \mathbf{V}_m^\top \] This eliminates iterative gradient searches, non-convex local minima, and off-manifold sampling artifacts, connecting dimensionality reduction directly to actionable causal recourse frameworks (Pearl 2009; Wachter et al. 2017).
  3. Signed Support Consolidation: Decomposing loading matrices into positive and negative lobes (\(\mathbf{V} = \mathbf{V}^+ - \mathbf{V}^-\)) and enforcing disjoint support (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)) prevents positive and negative weights from cancelling destructively, ensuring that positive and negative coefficients identify distinct anatomical or biological processes.

4.4 Why Cognitive Mediation Multiplies ADNI-MCI Prediction by Over 6-Fold

The practical value of the OLEE contract is clearest in our study of \(N = 1,084\) patients with Mild Cognitive Impairment (MCI) from the ADNI cohort (Weiner et al. 2017; Jack et al. 2008). Clinicians evaluate functional dementia severity using the Clinical Dementia Rating – Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB), which measures how well a patient manages daily life (remembering events, handling household tasks, staying oriented).

Integrating three structural brain MRI views (\(382\) regional volume and cortical thickness measurements) with a fourth view of clinical cognitive tests (\(p_4 = 13\)) reveals three clear findings:

  1. Brain Scans Alone Cannot Predict Daily Disability (\(R^2 \approx 0.041\)): Trying to predict CDR-SB directly from the 382 brain MRI features hits a hard ceiling of only 4.1% explained variance (Direct-Anatomy [SiMLR]: \(R^2 = 0.0411 \pm 0.0248, \rho = 0.2203\)), while raw Ridge regression fails completely (\(R^2 = -0.1810\)). Biologically, tissue loss in the cortex and hippocampus does not directly cause a patient to struggle with daily errands; instead, brain atrophy first erodes specific mental abilities (memory recall, executive attention), and it is that loss of mental ability that impairs daily functioning.
  2. Modeling the Two-Step Pathway Multiplies Accuracy Over 6-Fold (\(R^2 \approx 0.265\)): When our 4-view models align structural brain MRI with the cognitive test battery along the natural pathway (\(\text{Brain MRI} \to \text{Cognition} \to \text{CDR-SB}\)), out-of-sample accuracy jumps more than six-fold:
    • LEND-SiMR + MAI [Mediated]: \(R^2 = 0.2648 \pm 0.0332\) (\(6.44\times\) higher than direct MRI, \(p = 9.77 \times 10^{-4}\))
    • SiMLR [Mediated]: \(R^2 = 0.2443 \pm 0.0534\) (\(5.94\times\) higher, solved in \(0.86\text{s}\))
    • Flow-SiMR-V + MAI [Mediated]: \(R^2 = 0.2290 \pm 0.0696\) (\(5.57\times\) higher) Formal Baron–Kenny mediation confirms that 84.5% to 86.4% of the total effect of brain atrophy on CDR-SB is indirect, traveling through cognitive performance (\(p < 10^{-15}\)).
  3. Unconstrained Deep Networks Collapse on High-Dimensional Brain Data: Even with all four views available, unconstrained deep autoencoders fail (NED-SiMR + MAI: \(R^2 = 0.1127 \pm 0.0578\); NED-Shared-Private: \(R^2 = 0.0840\)). Without the orthogonal first-layer Stiefel constraint (\(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}\)), multi-layer perceptrons overfit noise across the 382 anatomical features and miss the shared brain-to-cognition axis.

4.5 Normalizing Flows: Invertibility and Geodesic Trajectories

The performance of Flow-SiMR-V arises from combining linear Stiefel filtering with bijective affine coupling layers:

  1. Information Preservation: Unlike standard autoencoders that compress data through lossy bottlenecks with positive reconstruction error (\(\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} > 0\)), normalizing flows are strictly bijective with unit or triangular Jacobian determinants (Dinh et al. 2016; Rezende and Mohamed 2015). Forward and inverse mappings are analytically exact.
  2. Prior Regularization: Normalizing flows maximize exact log-likelihood under a standard multivariate Gaussian prior \(\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I})\), acting as a continuous density regularizer that prevents coordinate collapse.
  3. Stiefel Dimensionality Reduction: Prepending the linear projection \(\mathbf{V}_m\) compresses ambient observations to the intrinsic \(k\)-dimensional latent subspace before coupling layers operate. This reduces parameter counts and prevents flows from fitting high-frequency isotropic noise.

4.5.1 Geodesic Latent Trajectories

Because normalizing flows define a smooth diffeomorphism between the input subspace and the Gaussian latent space, Flow-SiMR-V enables continuous geodesic path interpolation. For a patient presenting with high disease risk at coordinate \(\mathbf{z}_{\text{start}}\), the linear trajectory toward a healthy reference \(\mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}\): \[ \mathbf{z}(t) = (1 - t) \mathbf{z}_{\text{start}} + t \mathbf{z}_{\text{target}}, \quad t \in [0, 1] \] can be inverted through the flow and projected into observable feature space: \[ \mathbf{x}_m(t) = \text{Flow}_{\psi_m}^{-1}(\mathbf{z}(t)) \mathbf{V}_m^\top \] This traces a continuous, physically grounded trajectory of biomarker changes across disease stages.

4.6 Clinical Translation Across Cohorts and Translational Pathology

The modular Deep SiMLR framework addresses distinct clinical and biological challenges across the real-world clinical cohorts, neurodegenerative registries, neurotrauma models, and translational oncology:

  1. Cleveland Heart Disease (Cardiovascular Diagnostic Profiling): Integrating resting clinical vitals (\(p_1 = 7\)) and stress electrocardiography/laboratory measures (\(p_2 = 6\)) across \(N = 297\) patients for binary coronary artery disease diagnosis (\(83.7\%\) test accuracy for NSAFlow-PCA and PCA, \(84.8\%\) linear probe). The OLEE matrix \(\mathbf{V}\) directly identifies exercise-induced ST depression (oldpeak), maximum heart rate (thalach), and chest pain type (cp) as the dominant orthogonal axes.
  2. Diabetes Progression (Endocrine Risk Trajectories): Modeling quantitative 1-year disease progression from anthropometric baselines (\(p_1 = 5\)) and serum lipid biomarkers (\(p_2 = 5\)) across \(N = 442\) patients. The linear entrypoint attributes progression directly to body mass index (BMI) and serum triglycerides (S5), with NSAFlow-PCA (\(R^2 = 0.382\)) and SiMLR-LBFGSB (linear probe \(R^2 = 0.396\)) outperforming dense PCA (\(0.350\)).
  3. Multiple Features (mfeat, High-Cardinality Multi-View Benchmarking): Integrating 4 core geometric/transform views (\(p = [76, 64, 6, 47]\)) in the initialization benchmark and all 6 views (\(p = [76, 216, 64, 240, 47, 6]\)) in the full mfeat6 benchmark across \(N = 2000\) samples, establishing that OLEE-constrained architectures effectively discover common consensus without view-dominance collapse.
  4. ADNI Cognitive Mediation (Neurodegenerative Progression): As demonstrated in the 4-view ADNI-MCI cohort (\(N = 1,084\)), the OLEE contract enables the two-step brain-to-cognition-to-function pathway that unlocks a >6-fold accuracy gain (\(R^2 \approx 0.265\)) while insulating the model from the representation collapse observed in unconstrained deep networks.
  5. Parkinson’s Progression Markers Initiative (PPMI): In longitudinal Parkinson’s disease cohorts (Marek et al. 2011), clinical trials require monitoring early dopaminergic denervation using \(^{123}\text{I}\)-ioflupane SPECT (DaTscan) striatal binding ratios, CSF \(\alpha\)-synuclein seed amplification assays, and motor UPDRS-III examinations. The OLEE contract ensures that striatal binding ratios maintain direct linear attribution, enabling auditable tracking of disease modification without black-box confounding.
  6. Blast Neurotrauma and Traumatic Brain Injury (TBI): Low-level blast shockwave exposure induces diffuse axonal injury (DAI) characterized by non-local shear strains across white matter tracts (Stone et al. 2024). In these high-dimensional diffusion tensor imaging regimes, standard correlation objectives capitalize on chance associations. NSAFlow-PCA and SiMLR-LBFGSB enforce disjoint, non-negative support (\(\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^- = \mathbf{0}\)), isolating distinct anatomical tract disruptions without destructive crosstalk cancellation.
  7. Translational Oncology (TCGA-BRCA & TCGA-KIRC): Across \(N = 822\) primary breast invasive carcinoma tumors in the primary benchmark (rna \(p_1=200\), cnv \(p_2=150\), rppa \(p_3=150\)) and \(N = 1,010\) tumors in the full-cohort audit (The Cancer Genome Atlas Network 2012; Cancer Discovery 2016), NSAFlow-PCA (\(92.7\%\)) and SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(93.0\%\) linear probe) isolate coordinated copy-number alterations, transcriptomic drivers (ESR1, GATA3, PGR), and targeted proteomics while preserving exact feature weights. In clear cell renal cell carcinoma (KIRC) (The Cancer Genome Atlas Research Network 2013), multi-omics integration aligns chromosomal 3p loss with downstream VHL-HIF1A angiogenic pathway activation.

4.7 Methodological Considerations in Biomedical Cohorts

Our findings highlight three methodological principles for multi-modal analysis in computational anatomy and clinical cohorts:

  1. Preprocessing Sensitivity (Centering vs. Standardization): In clinical neuroimaging (e.g., ADNI cortical thickness in millimeters), features share a common physical unit. Standardizing columns to unit variance (\(z\)-scoring) scales low-variance noise regions equally with high-variance atrophy hubs, which can alter or reverse downstream findings. When physical units are commensurate, mean-centering alone preserves genuine biological variance gradients.
  2. Subspace Recovery vs. Axis Localization: An orthogonal rotation \(\mathbf{M} \in O(k)\) alters the orientation of basis axes without changing the linear subspace (\(\text{span}(\mathbf{X} \mathbf{V} \mathbf{M}) = \text{span}(\mathbf{X} \mathbf{V})\)). While linear prediction depends only on the subspace, localized biomarker discovery requires aligned, sparse, and non-negative axes—as demonstrated by the gap between PCA (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.333\)) and SiMLR-LBFGSB (\(\text{Support } F_1 = 0.942\)) on NonnegPartsWeak.
  3. Dataset-Level Block Inference: Ranking multi-modal architectures must aggregate across replicate seeds within each dataset block before computing Friedman and Nemenyi statistics (Demšar 2006), avoiding artificial inflation of sample size from repeated random initializations.

4.8 Limitations

  1. First-Layer Linear Constraint: Restricting the input layer to an orthogonal Stiefel projection limits the model’s ability to capture high-order intra-modality non-linear feature interactions directly from raw inputs. When predictive signal is driven entirely by non-linear combinations within a single view, unconstrained deep encoders may achieve higher intra-modality expressivity.
  2. Latent Dimension Scaling: Exact Stiefel retractions and polar factorizations scale cubically with latent rank (\(\mathcal{O}(k^3)\)). While efficient for typical biomedical latent spaces (\(k \le 64\)), scaling to massive latent spaces (\(k > 500\)) requires block-Stiefel or stochastic retraction methods.
  3. Number of Benchmark Regimes: Block-level Nemenyi inference across \(B = 9\) datasets and \(k = 9\) models yields a conservative critical difference (\(CD = 4.004\)), requiring paired Wilcoxon and exact sign-flip permutation tests across the 27 paired runs to resolve finer pairwise differences.

4.9 Conclusion

Deep SiMLR establishes a modular framework for multi-modal representation learning governed by the Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) contract. By decoupling linear feature attribution from downstream non-linear coordinate modeling, the architecture eliminates the clinical transparency penalty (\(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{V}_m\)) and enables closed-form counterfactual recourse (\(\delta \mathbf{x}_m^* = \Delta \mathbf{z}_m^* \mathbf{V}_m^\top\)) while capturing complex manifold structure.

Across 243 benchmark evaluations spanning 3 real clinical/genomic cohorts and 6 synthetic manifolds, box-constrained Riemannian optimization (SiMLR-LBFGSB, rank \(3.28\), support \(F_1 = 0.942\)) and linear-encoder/non-linear-decoder hybrids (LEND, rank \(3.56\), predictive \(0.830\), \(CMC = 0.907\)) lead overall performance, while NSAFlow-PCA (rank \(3.61\), \(0.029\text{s}\)) matches dense PCA in speed while enforcing \(22.4\%\) disjoint sparsity with zero crosstalk. Every OLEE-constrained architecture significantly outperforms unconstrained deep autoencoders (NED, NEDPP, \(p_{\text{Holm}} \le 0.018\)). In longitudinal neurodegeneration (ADNI-MCI, \(N=1,084\)), the OLEE contract unlocks a >6-fold cognitive mediation gain on clinical dementia severity (\(R^2 \approx 0.2648, p < 10^{-15}\)) while preventing the overfitting collapse that afflicts unconstrained deep networks.
