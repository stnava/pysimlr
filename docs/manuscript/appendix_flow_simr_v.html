C.1 Model Formulation

Flow-SiMR-V prepends a linear entrypoint projection matrix \(\mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\) to each view’s volume-preserving Normalizing Flow (Dinh et al. 2017; Rezende and Mohamed 2015; Kingma and Dhariwal 2018; Papamakarios et al. 2021; Kobyzev et al. 2020). This architecture provides a linear bottleneck at the input boundary while retaining bijective density mapping, conditional cross-view inference, and latent trajectory interpolation on the \(k\)-dimensional subspace. The normalizing flows are implemented in PyTorch (Paszke et al. 2019), adapting coupling structures from the Latent-Aligned Multiview Normalizing Flows (LAMNrFlows) framework (ntustison/LAMNrFlows).

C.1.1 Mathematical Formulation

For each input modality \(\mathbf{X}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p_m}\) (\(m = 1, \dots, M\)):

  1. Linear entrypoint bottleneck: \[ \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \qquad \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}, \] where \(\mathbf{V}_m\) is regularized via nsa_flow (\(w \in (0, 1)\), default \(w = 0.5\)) or polar retraction (Higham 2008; Absil et al. 2008).
  2. Low-dimensional bijective normalizing flow: \[ \mathbf{Z}_m = f_{\text{flow}, m}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}), \] where \(f_{\text{flow}, m}: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k\) is a composition of RealNVP affine coupling layers (Dinh et al. 2017) with exact inverse \(f_{\text{flow}, m}^{-1}\) and tractable log-determinant \(\log |\det J_{f_{\text{flow}, m}}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}})|\).
  3. Consensus mixing and inverse reconstruction: \[ \mathbf{U}_m = \operatorname{Consensus}\big(\{\mathbf{Z}_j\}_{j \in \mathcal{N}(m)}\big), \qquad \hat{\mathbf{X}}_m = f_{\text{flow}, m}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_m) \mathbf{V}_m^\top. \]

C.2 Rectangular OLEE Prior Collapse and Detached-Prior Optimization

A structural distinction separates square ambient-space normalizing flows (Flow-SiMR, where \(f_{\text{flow},m}: \mathbb{R}^{p_m} \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\)) from rectangular OLEE-bottlenecked flows (Flow-SiMR-V, where \(\mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k}\) with \(k \ll p_m\) precedes \(f_{\text{flow},m}: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k\)).

C.2.1 Why End-to-End Gaussian Prior Gradients Collapse \(\mathbf{V}_m\)

In standard normalizing flows, maximum likelihood training minimizes the negative log-likelihood under a standard isotropic Gaussian prior \(p(\mathbf{z}) = \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_k)\): \[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}; f_{\text{flow},m}) = \frac{1}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^n \|f_{\text{flow},m}(\mathbf{z}_{m,\text{lin},i})\|_2^2 - \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n \log \left|\det \frac{\partial f_{\text{flow},m}}{\partial \mathbf{z}_{m,\text{lin}}}(\mathbf{z}_{m,\text{lin},i})\right|. \] When the rectangular projection \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) (\(k \ll p_m\)) precedes the flow, the change-of-variables determinant \(\log |\det J_{f_{\text{flow},m}}|\) accounts only for volume changes inside the \(k\)-dimensional subspace and contains no log-determinant term for the rectangular projection \(\mathbf{V}_m\). If \(\nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}\) is backpropagated into \(\mathbf{V}_m\), the quadratic penalty \(\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{Z}_m\|_2^2 = \frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m) + \mathcal{O}(\|f_{\text{flow},m} - \mathrm{id}\|)\) is minimized by rotating \(\mathbf{V}_m\) onto the trailing eigenvectors of \(\mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m\) with the smallest eigenvalues \(\lambda_{p_m-k+1}, \dots, \lambda_{p_m}\).

C.2.2 Detached-Prior Subspace-Decoupled Optimization

To prevent rectangular prior collapse, Flow-SiMR-V detaches \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\) at the input to the Gaussian prior penalty:

  1. OLEE basis update (\(\mathbf{V}_m\)): Updated exclusively by the cross-view alignment energy \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\) and within-view relative reconstruction loss \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}\), while detaching \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\) in the prior term (\(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}) = \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}.\text{detach}()\)).
  2. Bijective flow update (\(f_{\text{flow},m}\)): Trained via \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(f_{\text{flow},m}(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}))) + \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}\), initialized with zero scale and translation weights (\(s_\ell = 0, t_\ell = 0\)) at epoch \(0\) so \(f_{\text{flow},m}^{(0)}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}) = \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\).

In the ADNI-MCI 1,214-configuration sweep (\(N = 1,084\), \(k = 3\), Protocol B), Flow-SiMR-V with detached-prior optimization reaches an out-of-sample CogPC1 \(R^2\) of \(0.2341\) (Mean11_RidgeCV \(= 0.1054\), and \(0.1930 \pm 0.0048\) across the 3-seed benchmark), ranking third overall across all 12 benchmark datasets.

C.3 Performance Visualizations

C.3.1 Conditional Clinical Imputation (True vs. Imputed Serums)

Because Flow-SiMR-V preserves invertibility on the \(k\)-dimensional bottleneck space, it supports conditional cross-view inference via the Gaussian Schur complement in latent space. In Figure C.1, we train Flow-SiMR-V on the Diabetes dataset under a 70/30 split, withhold the blood serum view on test patients, and impute serum triglycerides (s5) from vitals alone.

C.3.2 Patient-Level Linear Attribution

Prepending the linear encoder matrix \(\mathbf{V}_m\) ensures that the normalizing flow coupling layers do not obscure feature importance at the input boundary. Figure C.2 displays the local feature contributions \(\mathbf{x}_{m,i} \odot \mathbf{v}_{m,l}\) for a patient in the Heart Disease cohort.

Tracing a straight-line interpolation in the shared latent space between a low-risk and high-risk patient and inverting it through \(f_{\text{flow},m}^{-1}\) and \(\mathbf{V}_m^\top\) defines a smooth trajectory in feature space (Figure C.3).
