We develop the Deep SiMLR framework deductively in five progressive stages. Each stage addresses a structural limitation of the preceding one while preserving exact physical auditability at the input boundary:

  1. Stage 1 (The Input Boundary — OLEE & NSA-Flow Geometry): Why every modality must enter through a linear projection \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) on the Stiefel or Non-negative Stiefel Approximating (NSA) manifold, and why evaluating basis recovery requires both linear subspace probes (\(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{U}\)) and direct sparse support localization (\(\text{Support } F_1\)).
  2. Stage 2 (Cross-View Consensus, Graph Topologies & Spectral Triage): How \(M\) views form a shared consensus without antipodal sign cancellation, self-memorization (loo), or nuisance-view dominance (graph bipartite target alignment and Leave-One-Out Modality Alignment Index MAI).
  3. Stage 3 (Linear Multi-View Manifold Solvers — SiMLR, SiMLR-LBFGS, SiMLR-LBFGSB): How iteration-0 warm-start calibration, Sylvester/Newton–Schulz polar retractions, and bound-constrained quasi-Newton steps solve the linear multi-view problem in milliseconds.
  4. Stage 4 (Non-Linear Decoding Downstream of OLEE — LEND vs. NED and NEDPP): How pairing a linear Stiefel encoder with a non-linear multilayer perceptron decoder captures curved observational manifolds without sacrificing constant input attribution or overfitting high-dimensional views.
  5. Stage 5 (Bijective Normalizing Flows Downstream of OLEE — Flow-SiMR-V): How composing the rectangular OLEE projection with invertible affine coupling layers in \(\mathbb{R}^k\) enables exact density estimation, conditional cross-view inference, and geodesic trajectory mapping—provided the rectangular encoder is insulated from prior-induced variance collapse via a detached-prior objective.

2.1 Stage 1: The Input Boundary — OLEE and NSA-Flow Manifold Geometry

2.1.1 The Multi-Modal Inverse Problem

We frame multi-modal representation learning as a structured inverse problem over \(M\) distinct observational domains. Let \(\mathbf{X}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p_m}\) denote the centered observation matrix for modality \(m \in \{1, \dots, M\}\), where \(n\) is the number of co-registered subjects and \(p_m\) is the ambient feature dimensionality. Each modality provides a partial, noisy observation of an underlying shared latent biological state \(\mathbf{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) governed by modality-specific forward generative processes: \[ \mathbf{X}_m = g_m(\mathbf{S}) + \mathbf{E}_m \tag{2.1}\] where \(g_m: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\) is a smooth generative map applied row-wise across observations, and \(\mathbf{E}_m\) represents domain-specific observational noise and idiosyncratic view-private variance. The objective is to estimate an auditable coordinate representation of \(\mathbf{S}\) while isolating cross-modal consensus from view-private noise.

2.1.2 The Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) as a Structural Information Bottleneck

Standard deep autoencoders and multi-layer perceptrons process inputs via unconstrained compositions \(\mathbf{Z} = \text{MLP}(\mathbf{X})\). Following Tishby’s Information Bottleneck principle (Tishby et al. 2000), unconstrained deep encoders entangle features across successive layers, readily memorizing high-dimensional noise and structured nuisance variables when \(p_m \sim n\). In biomedical discovery, this produces representation collapse on held-out subjects and obscures physical feature attribution (Rudin 2019).

We resolve this entanglement at the architectural boundary by enforcing the Only Linear Encoder Entrypoint (OLEE) contract. OLEE mandates that the initial transformation of any modality \(\mathbf{X}_m\) is a linear projection onto the compact Stiefel manifold \(\text{St}(k, p_m) = \{ \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k \}\) (Edelman et al. 1998; Boumal 2023): \[ \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}, \quad \mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m), \quad \mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k \tag{2.2}\] Because the intrinsic dimension is strictly bounded (\(k \ll p_m\)) and \(\mathbf{V}_m\) acts as a partial isometry, OLEE imposes a geometric information bottleneck at the input boundary: \[ \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \quad \mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m), \quad \mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k \tag{2.3}\] All subsequent non-linear operations (decoders, prediction heads, or normalizing flows) consume \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\), never raw features \(\mathbf{X}_m\). This structural contract yields three exact mathematical guarantees:

  1. Exact Feature Attribution & Constant Input Gradient: The latent score \(z_{i, l}\) for subject \(i\) on component \(l\) is strictly the linear inner product \(\sum_{j=1}^{p_m} X_{i, j} V_{j, l}\). The directional derivative of the first-layer representation with respect to input observations is globally constant across the entire domain: \[ \nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m} \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{V}_m \tag{2.4}\] Because this input Jacobian is independent of \(\mathbf{X}_m\), the attribution profile is invariant across subjects, eliminating gradient saturation and post-hoc surrogate sampling artifacts (Lundberg and Lee 2017; Radulovic 2023).
  2. Global Variance Partitioning: Because \(\mathbf{V}_m^\top \mathbf{V}_m = \mathbf{I}_k\), the projection is an isometry on \(\mathbb{R}^k\), and projected variance decomposes additively across orthogonal axes: \(\text{Var}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}) = \sum_{l=1}^k \text{Var}(\mathbf{z}_{m, l})\).
  3. Closed-Form Minimum-Norm Counterfactuals: Targeted counterfactual state transitions can be evaluated analytically in closed form without iterative gradient search, as established by the following theorem.

2.1.2.1 Theorem: Closed-Form Minimum-Norm Counterfactuals

Theorem:
Let a subject have latent representation \(\mathbf{z} = \mathbf{x} \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^k\) under the OLEE basis \(\mathbf{V} \in \text{St}(k, p)\). To transition the subject to a targeted counterfactual latent state \(\mathbf{z}^* = \mathbf{z} + \Delta \mathbf{z}\), the unique perturbation \(\delta \mathbf{x}^* \in \mathbb{R}^p\) that minimizes the Euclidean intervention effort \(\|\delta \mathbf{x}\|_2^2\) while satisfying \((\mathbf{x} + \delta \mathbf{x}) \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{z}^*\) is given analytically by: \[ \delta \mathbf{x}^* = \Delta \mathbf{z} \mathbf{V}^\top \tag{2.5}\]

Proof:
The counterfactual perturbation problem is formulated as the constrained optimization: \[ \min_{\delta \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^p} \frac{1}{2} \|\delta \mathbf{x}\|_2^2 \quad \text{subject to} \quad \delta \mathbf{x} \mathbf{V} = \Delta \mathbf{z} \tag{2.6}\] Introducing Lagrange multipliers \(\boldsymbol{\lambda} \in \mathbb{R}^k\), the Lagrangian is: \[ \mathcal{L}(\delta \mathbf{x}, \boldsymbol{\lambda}) = \frac{1}{2} \delta \mathbf{x} \delta \mathbf{x}^\top - (\delta \mathbf{x} \mathbf{V} - \Delta \mathbf{z}) \boldsymbol{\lambda} \] Setting the gradient with respect to \(\delta \mathbf{x}\) to zero gives \(\nabla_{\delta \mathbf{x}} \mathcal{L} = \delta \mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{V}^\top = \mathbf{0} \implies \delta \mathbf{x} = \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{V}^\top\). Multiplying on the right by \(\mathbf{V}\) and using \(\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}_k\) yields \(\delta \mathbf{x} \mathbf{V} = \boldsymbol{\lambda}^\top \mathbf{I}_k = \Delta \mathbf{z}\), whence \(\delta \mathbf{x}^* = \Delta \mathbf{z} \mathbf{V}^\top\). \(\square\)

2.1.3 Scale-Invariant Frame Defect, Angle Defect, and Signed Support Consolidation

An unconstrained orthonormal basis on \(\text{St}(k, p_m)\) is still dense and mixed-sign: every feature loads on every component, and positive and negative weights cancel within each column. To interpolate smoothly between dense orthogonal projections and sparse, disjoint, non-negative feature clusters, we regularize \(\mathbf{V}_m\) via Non-negative Stiefel Approximating Flow (nsa_flow) (Avants et al. 2025).

2.1.3.1 Trace-Normalized Stiefel Frame Defect (\(D\))

The trace-normalized frame defect (src/pysimlr/utils.py:504-630) measures departure from equal-norm column orthonormality: \[ D(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{1}{1 - 1/k} \left\| \frac{\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V}}{\text{tr}(\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V})} - \frac{1}{k} \mathbf{I}_k \right\|_F^2 \tag{2.7}\] Let \(\mathbf{G} = \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{k \times k}\) and \(t = \text{tr}(\mathbf{G}) = \|\mathbf{V}\|_F^2\). Writing \(\tilde{\mathbf{G}} = \mathbf{G} / t\), the Frobenius norm expands into off-diagonal column decorrelation plus diagonal norm balance: \[ \left\|\tilde{\mathbf{G}} - \frac{1}{k} \mathbf{I}_k\right\|_F^2 = \sum_{i \ne j} \tilde{G}_{ij}^2 + \sum_{i=1}^k \left( \tilde{G}_{ii} - \frac{1}{k} \right)^2 \] Because the participation ratio is \(\text{EffRank}(\mathbf{V}) = t^2 / \|\mathbf{G}\|_F^2\), the defect connects directly to effective rank: \(D(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{1}{1 - 1/k} \left( \frac{1}{\text{EffRank}(\mathbf{V})} - \frac{1}{k} \right)\). Differentiating with respect to \(\mathbf{V}\) yields: \[ \nabla_{\mathbf{V}} D(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{4}{t^2 (1 - 1/k)} \left[ \mathbf{V} \mathbf{G} - \frac{\|\mathbf{G}\|_F^2}{t} \mathbf{V} \right] \tag{2.8}\] By Euler’s theorem for degree-0 homogeneous functions, \(\langle \nabla_{\mathbf{V}} D(\mathbf{V}), \mathbf{V} \rangle \equiv 0\), so \(D(\mathbf{V})\) exerts zero radial force on matrix scale.

2.1.3.2 Squared-Cosine Angle Defect (\(C\))

When biological components have unequal norms, we also evaluate the column-scale-invariant squared-cosine angle defect (angle_defect in src/pysimlr/utils.py:633-724), with \(\mathbf{U} = \mathbf{V} \text{diag}(\|\mathbf{v}_1\|_2^{-1}, \dots, \|\mathbf{v}_k\|_2^{-1})\): \[ C(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{\|\mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{U} - \mathbf{I}_k\|_F^2}{k(k - 1)} \tag{2.9}\] with gradient: \[ \nabla_{\mathbf{V}} C(\mathbf{V}) = \frac{4}{k(k - 1)} \left[ \mathbf{U} \mathbf{H} - \mathbf{U} \, \text{diag}\left(\text{rowsum}(\mathbf{H}^{\odot 2})\right) \right] \text{diag}(\|\mathbf{v}_1\|_2^{-1}, \dots, \|\mathbf{v}_k\|_2^{-1}), \quad \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{U} - \mathbf{I}_k \tag{2.10}\] For non-negative matrices \(\mathbf{V} \ge \mathbf{0}\) with non-zero columns, \(C(\mathbf{V}) = 0\) if and only if the columns have pairwise disjoint supports.

2.1.3.3 Direct SVD Contrast Splitting and Support Consolidation

To represent bidirectional biological responses (e.g., cortical atrophy vs. ventricular expansion) without sign cancellation, we lift each basis \(\mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{p \times k}\) into paired non-negative lobes: \[ \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{V}^+ - \mathbf{V}^-, \quad \mathbf{V}^+ = \max(\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{0}), \quad \mathbf{V}^- = \max(-\mathbf{V}, \mathbf{0}) \tag{2.11}\] forming \(\mathbf{W} = [\mathbf{V}^+ \mid \mathbf{V}^-] \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}^{p \times 2k}\), initialized via Direct SVD Contrast Splitting: \[ \mathbf{E} = \text{SVD}_k(\mathbf{X}), \quad \mathbf{W}_0 = [\max(\mathbf{E}, \mathbf{0}) \mid \max(-\mathbf{E}, \mathbf{0})] \tag{2.12}\] When strict disjoint assignment is requested (consolidate=True), the deterministic support consolidation operator assigns each feature \(j\) to its winning lobe: \[ \text{win}(j) = \arg\max_{l \in \{1, \dots, 2k\}} W_{j, l} \tag{2.13}\] guaranteeing exact zero lobe crosstalk (\(\|\mathbf{V}^+ \odot \mathbf{V}^-\|_1 = 0\)).

2.1.4 Subspace Invariance vs. Sparse Basis Localization

Evaluating whether a method recovered the right basis requires distinguishing the linear subspace \(\text{span}(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V})\) from the individual columns of \(\mathbf{V}\).

2.1.4.1 Theorem: Rotation Invariance of Linear Subspace Probes

Theorem:
Let \(\mathbf{Z}_1 = \mathbf{X} \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) and let \(\mathbf{M} \in \text{GL}(k, \mathbb{R})\) be any invertible \(k \times k\) matrix defining an alternative basis \(\mathbf{Z}_2 = \mathbf{X} \mathbf{V} \mathbf{M} = \mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M}\). For any regression target \(\mathbf{y} \in \mathbb{R}^{n}\):

  1. The Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) projection hat matrices are identical: \(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_2} \equiv \mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_1}\).
  2. The fitted values and regression \(R^2\) are strictly invariant: \(\hat{\mathbf{y}}_2 \equiv \hat{\mathbf{y}}_1\) and \(R^2(\mathbf{Z}_2, \mathbf{y}) \equiv R^2(\mathbf{Z}_1, \mathbf{y})\).

Proof:
The OLS hat matrix projecting \(\mathbf{y}\) onto the column space of \(\mathbf{Z}_2\) is: \[ \mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_2} = \mathbf{Z}_2 \left( \mathbf{Z}_2^\top \mathbf{Z}_2 \right)^{-1} \mathbf{Z}_2^\top \tag{2.14}\] Substituting \(\mathbf{Z}_2 = \mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M}\) and using \((\mathbf{A} \mathbf{B} \mathbf{C})^{-1} = \mathbf{C}^{-1} \mathbf{B}^{-1} \mathbf{A}^{-1}\) for invertible \(\mathbf{M}\) yields \(\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_2} = \mathbf{Z}_1 \mathbf{M} \mathbf{M}^{-1} (\mathbf{Z}_1^\top \mathbf{Z}_1)^{-1} (\mathbf{M}^\top)^{-1} \mathbf{M}^\top \mathbf{Z}_1^\top = \mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{Z}_1}\). \(\square\)

Consequently, we evaluate representations across both linear probes—the Strictly Linear OLEE Basis Probe (\(\mathbf{X}\mathbf{V}\)) and the Global Consensus Probe (\(\mathbf{U}\))—together with Ground-Truth Support Recovery (\(\text{Support } F_1\) on \(\mathbf{V}\)) and Canonical Mean Correlation (\(CMC\) on \(\mathbf{U}\)), which directly measure whether \(\mathbf{V}_m\) localizes true disjoint feature clusters and whether \(\mathbf{U}\) recovers the underlying latent coordinates.

2.2 Stage 2: Cross-View Consensus — Mixing, Graph Topologies, and Spectral Triage

Given per-modality latent representations \(\mathbf{Z}_m = f_m(\mathbf{X}_m) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) and decoders \(g_m: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\), the global joint-variational functional couples within-modality reconstruction fidelity, cross-modal alignment against a modality-specific consensus target \(\mathbf{U}_m\), and manifold regularization: \[ \min_{\{f_m, g_m\}_{m=1}^M} \sum_{m=1}^M \left[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}(\mathbf{X}_m, g_m(\mathbf{Z}_m)) + \lambda_{\text{sim}} w_m \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}(\mathbf{Z}_m, \mathbf{U}_m) + \gamma D(\mathbf{V}_m) \right] + \mathcal{R}(\mathbf{Z}) \tag{2.15}\]

2.2.1 Consensus Topologies: star, loo, and Directed/Bipartite graph

How the target \(\mathbf{U}_m\) is constructed from the set of modality projections \(\{\mathbf{Z}_j\}_{j=1}^M\) governs what shared structure the bases \(\mathbf{V}_m\) learn:

  1. Global Star Topology (topology="star"): Every modality aligns to a single pooled consensus \(\mathbf{U} = \text{Consensus}(\{\mathbf{Z}_j\}_{j=1}^M)\). In high-capacity models, including \(\mathbf{Z}_m\) inside its own target allows modality \(m\) to partially satisfy the alignment objective through self-correlation.
  2. Leave-One-Out Topology (topology="loo"): Modality \(m\) aligns strictly against the consensus of all other modalities: \[ \mathbf{U}_{\neq m} = \text{Consensus}\left( \{\mathbf{Z}_j\}_{j \neq m} \right) \tag{2.16}\] quarantining modality \(m\) from contributing to its own target and preventing self-memorization.
  3. Directed / Bipartite Graph Topology (topology="graph", path_graph) and Partial-View Test Inference:
    In many clinical and biological studies, \(M-1\) predictor views \(\{\mathbf{X}_1, \dots, \mathbf{X}_{M-1}\}\) (such as three structural brain MRI views or three multi-omics assays) are available both during training and at deployment, whereas a \(M\)-th clinical or phenotypic target view \(\mathbf{X}_M = \mathbf{Y}_{\text{clin}}\) (such as a multi-domain cognitive battery) is observed during training but withheld at test time.
    When the predictor views are aligned only with each other (Protocol A), their cross-view covariance is dominated by high-variance background anatomy or housekeeping biology rather than the clinical phenotype. By specifying a bipartite adjacency graph \(\mathcal{G}\) connecting each predictor view \(m \in \{1, \dots, M-1\}\) exclusively to the target view \(M\): \[ \mathbf{U}_m = \text{Consensus}(\{\mathbf{Z}_M\}) \quad (m = 1, \dots, M-1), \qquad \mathbf{U}_M = \text{Consensus}\left(\{\mathbf{Z}_1, \dots, \mathbf{Z}_{M-1}\}\right) \tag{2.17}\] every predictor basis \(\mathbf{V}_m\) is steered directly toward the clinical subspace during training. At test time, View \(M\) is withheld (eval_view_indices \(= \{1, \dots, M-1\}\)) and out-of-sample latent coordinates are computed strictly from the predictor views’ OLEE projections: \[ \hat{\mathbf{U}}_{\text{test}} = \frac{1}{M - 1} \sum_{m=1}^{M-1} \mathbf{X}_{m,\text{test}} \mathbf{V}_m \tag{2.18}\] This bipartite training / partial-view testing protocol requires zero test labels while preventing healthy anatomical covariance among the predictor views from swamping the disease-relevant subspace.

2.2.2 Scale-Profiled Alignment Energies (procrustes, recon_r2, align, acc, logcosh)

The alignment functional \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}(\mathbf{Z}_m, \mathbf{U}_m)\) must be scale-well-posed so it cannot be trivially minimized by shrinking \(\|\mathbf{V}_m\|_F \to 0\):

  • Scale-Profiled Procrustes & Cosine Alignment (procrustes, align, acc): Measures orthogonal Procrustes similarity \(-\sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i(\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_m^\top \tilde{\mathbf{U}}_m)\) or normalized trace correlation \(-\text{tr}(\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_m^\top \tilde{\mathbf{U}}_m)\) on Frobenius-normalized projections.
  • Scale-Profiled Reconstruction \(R^2\) (recon_r2): Profiles out optimal linear scaling before measuring residual variance, eliminating the zero-basis trap of unnormalized regression MSE.
  • Robust Log-Cosh Negentropy (logcosh): To bound the influence of heavy-tailed clinical outliers (calculate_ica_energy in src/pysimlr/simlr.py:844-848), we evaluate: \[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{log-cosh}}(\mathbf{Z}_m, \mathbf{U}_{m}) = - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^k \sum_{j=1}^k \left( |C_{ji}| - \log 2 + \log\left(1 + e^{-2|C_{ji}|}\right) \right), \quad \mathbf{C} = \mathbf{U}_m^\top \mathbf{Z}_m \tag{2.19}\] whose first-order variational gradient with respect to \(\mathbf{V}_m\) has strictly bounded leverage (\(|\tanh(C_{ji})| < 1\)): \[ \nabla_{\mathbf{V}_m} \mathcal{L}_{\text{log-cosh}} = - \frac{1}{n} \mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{U}_{m} \tanh(\mathbf{C}) \tag{2.20}\]

2.2.3 Sign-Aligned Consensus Mixing (pca, svd, ica, newton)

Latent factorizations are invariant under coordinate sign reflections (\(\mathbf{z}_j \leftrightarrow -\mathbf{z}_j\)). When two views recover the same biological factor with opposite signs, naive cross-modal averaging suffers destructive cancellation: \[ \bar{\mathbf{u}}_l = \frac{1}{M-1} \sum_{j \ne m} \mathbf{z}_{j, l} \approx \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{u}_l - \mathbf{u}_l) = \mathbf{0} \tag{2.21}\] Prior to mixing (align_column_signs in src/pysimlr/utils.py:2003-2064), we synchronize column signs greedily against the running coordinate sum \(\mathbf{R}\): \[ s_{j, l} = \text{sign}\left( \sum_{i=1}^n Z_{j, il} R_{il} \right), \quad \tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_j = \mathbf{Z}_j \, \text{diag}(s_{j, 1}, \dots, s_{j, k}), \quad \mathbf{R} \leftarrow \mathbf{R} + \tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_j \tag{2.22}\] From the sign-aligned projections \(\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_j\), pysimlr supports two families of consensus mixing: 1. Sign-Aligned Mean + Gram–Schmidt (mixing_algorithm="pca") and Spectral Concatenation ("svd", "ica"): Particularly effective for linear manifold solvers where exact block coordinate updates are taken at each iteration. 2. Differentiable Newton–Schulz Polar Mixing (mixing_algorithm="newton"): Computes the closest column-orthogonal consensus via 10 cubic Newton–Schulz iterations without GPU-serializing SVD calls or discontinuous singular-vector sign flips across mini-batches: \[ \bar{\mathbf{U}}_{m} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{N}(m)|} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(m)} \tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_j, \qquad \mathbf{U}_{m} = \text{NewtonSchulz}\left( \bar{\mathbf{U}}_{m}, \text{iterations}=10 \right) \tag{2.23}\] with exponential moving-average anchor sign synchronization across mini-batch epochs: \[ \mathbf{A}^{(t)} = 0.9 \, \mathbf{A}^{(t-1)} + 0.1 \left( \mathbf{A}_{\text{new}} \odot \text{signs} \right) \tag{2.24}\]

2.2.4 Spectral Triage and the Modality Alignment Index (MAI)

When integrating \(M \ge 3\) heterogeneous views, an uninformative or corrupted modality can pull the consensus toward isotropic noise. Under the Baik–Ben Arous–Péché (BBP) phase transition (Baik et al. 2005), informative modalities exhibit leading singular values that escape the Marchenko–Pastur bulk, whereas pure noise views remain flat (Figure 2.1).

In both linear (simlr) and deep/flow models (lend_simr, ned_simr, flow_simr, flow_simr_v), we score each modality \(m\) against its Leave-One-Out consensus \(\mathbf{U}_{\neq m}\) via Scale-Profiled Procrustes \(R^2\) (or canonical correlation cca / RV-coefficient rvcoef): \[ R_m^2 = \left( \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i(\tilde{\mathbf{Z}}_m^\top \tilde{\mathbf{U}}_{\neq m}) \right)^2 \in [0, 1] \tag{2.25}\] optionally modulated by spectral sharpness \(\mathcal{S}_m = \sigma_1(\mathbf{Z}_m) / \sum_{j=1}^k \sigma_j(\mathbf{Z}_m)\) (mai_metric="procrustes_r2_sharp"): \[ \text{MAI}_m = R_m^2 \cdot \mathcal{S}_m \tag{2.26}\] (Note: When \(M = 2\), Leave-One-Out compares View 1 to View 2 and View 2 to View 1 with a symmetric spectrum, so \(\text{MAI}_1 \equiv \text{MAI}_2\) and weights remain uniform \([0.5, 0.5]\); MAI triage is therefore specifically a property of \(M \ge 3\) views.)

The raw alignment indices are smoothed via EMA and passed through a mean-centered sigmoid gate with linear warmup: \[ \mathbf{mai}^{(t)} = 0.9 \, \mathbf{mai}^{(t-1)} + 0.1 \, \mathbf{mai}_{\text{raw}}^{(t)} \tag{2.27}\] \[ \omega_m^{\text{gate}} = \sigma\left( \kappa \cdot (\text{mai}_m^{(t)} - \mu_{\text{mai}}) \right) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left(-\kappa (\text{mai}_m^{(t)} - \mu_{\text{mai}})\right)} \tag{2.28}\] \[ \tilde{w}_m = \text{mai}_m^{(t)} \cdot \omega_m^{\text{gate}}, \quad w_m^{\text{target}} = \frac{\tilde{w}_m}{\sum_{j=1}^M \tilde{w}_j + 10^{-8}} \tag{2.29}\] \[ \mathbf{w}^{(t)} = (1 - \rho) \mathbf{w}_{\text{uniform}} + \rho \mathbf{w}^{\text{target}} \tag{2.30}\] While simple averaging across \(M\) views reduces independent noise at rate \(1/M\), correlated cross-modal artifacts leave an irreducible floor bounded by the cross-view noise correlation \(\rho_{\text{noise}}\): \[ \text{Var}(\mathbf{U}) = \frac{\sigma^2}{M} (1 - \rho_{\text{noise}}) + \rho_{\text{noise}} \sigma^2 \tag{2.31}\] which LOO/bipartite topologies and MAI actively suppress.

2.3 Stage 3: Linear Multi-View Manifold Solvers (SiMLR, SiMLR-LBFGS, SiMLR-LBFGSB)

When both the encoder and decoder are linear (\(f_m(\mathbf{X}_m) = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\), \(g_m(\mathbf{Z}_m) = \mathbf{U}_m \mathbf{W}_m\) with \(\mathbf{W}_m = \mathbf{U}_m^\dagger \mathbf{X}_m\)), the joint-variational problem reduces to optimizing \(\{\mathbf{V}_m\}_{m=1}^M\) directly on the Stiefel/NSA-Flow manifold (Figure 2.2).

2.3.1 Iteration-0 Warm-Start Calibration

A critical numerical invariant in alternating manifold optimization is Iteration-0 Warm-Start Calibration (src/pysimlr/simlr.py:1512-1606). When bases \(\{\mathbf{V}_m^{(0)}\}\) are initialized via per-view SVD/PCA or GCCA, the initial projections \(\mathbf{Z}_m^{(0)} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m^{(0)}\) already span a high-variance subspace. Evaluating the initial consensus \(\mathbf{U}^{(0)}\), calibrating the relative modality normalization weights normalizing_weights and orth_weights, and recording init_total_energy at iteration \(t = 0\) prior to the first gradient step ensures that the initial step size is properly scaled and that best_v_mats can never degrade below the warm-start initialization.

2.3.2 Riemannian Tangent Projection, Sylvester Polar Factor, and Newton–Schulz Retraction

For each basis \(\mathbf{V} \in \text{St}(k, p_m)\): \[ \text{St}(k, p_m) = \{ \mathbf{V} \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{I}_k \} \tag{2.32}\] the tangent space and Riemannian gradient projection (project_gradient in src/pysimlr/simlr.py:111-146) are: \[ T_{\mathbf{V}} \text{St}(k, p_m) = \{ \Delta \in \mathbb{R}^{p_m \times k} \mid \mathbf{V}^\top \Delta + \Delta^\top \mathbf{V} = \mathbf{0} \} \tag{2.33}\] \[ \Pi_{T_{\mathbf{V}}}(\mathbf{G}) = \mathbf{G} - \mathbf{V} \, \text{sym}(\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{G}) = \mathbf{G} - \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{V} (\mathbf{V}^\top \mathbf{G} + \mathbf{G}^\top \mathbf{V}) \tag{2.34}\] To map an updated iterate \(\mathbf{Y}\) back to \(\text{St}(k, p_m)\) via the polar factor: \[ R(\mathbf{Y}) = \mathbf{Y} (\mathbf{Y}^\top \mathbf{Y})^{-1/2} \tag{2.35}\] without differentiating through torch.linalg.svd (whose backward pass divides by \(\sigma_i^2 - \sigma_j^2\) and produces NaN at repeated singular values \(\sigma_i = 1\)), pysimlr uses nsa_flow.polar_factor, which differentiates \(\mathbf{H} = (\mathbf{Y}^\top \mathbf{Y})^{1/2}\) via the continuous Lyapunov/Sylvester equation: \[ \mathbf{H} (d\mathbf{H}) + (d\mathbf{H}) \mathbf{H} = (d\mathbf{Y})^\top \mathbf{Y} + \mathbf{Y}^\top (d\mathbf{Y}) \tag{2.36}\] or applies the matrix-multiplication-only cubic Newton–Schulz recurrence: \[ \mathbf{Y}_0 = \frac{\mathbf{Y}}{\|\mathbf{Y}\|_F}, \quad \mathbf{Y}_{\tau+1} = \frac{1}{2} \mathbf{Y}_\tau \left( 3 \mathbf{I}_k - \mathbf{Y}_\tau^\top \mathbf{Y}_\tau \right) \tag{2.37}\] whose singular value errors \(\epsilon_{\tau, i} = 1 - \sigma_{\tau, i}\) contract quadratically for \(\sigma_{0, i} \in (0, \sqrt{3})\): \[ \|\mathbf{Y}_{\tau+1}^\top \mathbf{Y}_{\tau+1} - \mathbf{I}_k\| = \mathcal{O}\left(\|\mathbf{Y}_\tau^\top \mathbf{Y}_\tau - \mathbf{I}_k\|^2\right) \tag{2.38}\] \[ \epsilon_{\tau+1, i} = 1 - \sigma_{\tau+1, i} = \frac{3}{2}\epsilon_{\tau, i}^2 - \frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{\tau, i}^3 = \epsilon_{\tau, i}^2 \left( \frac{3 - \epsilon_{\tau, i}}{2} \right) \tag{2.39}\] as illustrated geometrically in Figure 2.3.

2.3.3 Quasi-Newton Curvature Steps (SiMLR-LBFGS and Bound-Constrained SiMLR-LBFGSB)

First-order optimizers (armijo_gradient, lars, adam, hybrid_adam) canzig-zag across narrow ravines formed by cross-view alignment and orthogonality penalties. We provide two second-order quasi-Newton linear solvers:

  1. SiMLR-LBFGS (optimizer_type="torch_lbfgs"): Uses two-loop inverse-Hessian recursion with Strong Wolfe line search and an analytical gradient closure (src/pysimlr/optimizers.py:841-894): \[ \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t + \alpha_t \mathbf{p}_t) \le \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t) + c_1 \alpha_t \text{tr}(\nabla \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{V}_t)^\top \mathbf{p}_t) \tag{2.40}\]
  2. SiMLR-LBFGSB (optimizer_type="nsa_lbfgsb"): Uses the bound-constrained L-BFGS-B solver from nsa_flow, enforcing non-negativity (\(\mathbf{V}_m \ge \mathbf{0}\)) directly inside the active-set quasi-Newton subspace step rather than by post-hoc clipping.

2.4 Stage 4: Non-Linear Decoding Downstream of OLEE (LEND vs. NED and NEDPP)

When the generative mapping \(\mathbf{X}_m = g_m(\mathbf{S}) + \mathbf{E}_m\) is non-linear, a linear decoder \(g_m(\mathbf{Z}_m) = \mathbf{Z}_m \mathbf{W}_m\) cannot reconstruct curved manifold trajectories. However, replacing the encoder with an unconstrained MLP destroys the constant attribution guarantee \(\nabla_{\mathbf{X}_m}\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{V}_m\) and risks memorizing high-dimensional noise when \(p_m \sim n\).

2.4.1 LEND (Linear Encoder, Non-Linear Decoder)

LEND resolves this asymmetry by keeping the encoder strictly linear on the Stiefel/NSA-Flow manifold while parameterizing the decoder as a multi-layer perceptron (Figure 2.4): \[ \text{Encoder (OLEE): } \mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m, \qquad \text{Decoder: } \hat{\mathbf{X}}_m = \text{MLP}_{\theta_m}(\mathbf{Z}_m) \] Because \(\mathbf{Z}_m = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\), LEND retains exact first-layer attribution and closed-form counterfactuals at test time while allowing \(\text{MLP}_{\theta_m}\) to absorb non-linear observational curvature during training.

2.4.2 NED and NEDPP (Post-Bottleneck Non-Linear Encoders and Shared–Private Disentanglement)

To test when post-bottleneck encoder non-linearity or explicit private subspaces help or hurt, we also formulate:

  • NED (Non-linear Encoder–Decoder, Figure 2.5): Applies an MLP encoder head after the initial \(k\)-dimensional Stiefel bottleneck: \(f_m(\mathbf{X}_m) = \text{MLP}_{\phi_m}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m)\), \(g_m(\mathbf{Z}_m) = \text{MLP}_{\theta_m}(\mathbf{Z}_m)\).
  • NEDPP (Shared–Private Disentangled NED, Figure 2.6): Splits each view into shared and view-private branches \([\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{shared}} \parallel \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{private}}]\) initialized with orthogonal Stiefel bases \([\mathbf{V}_{m,\text{sh}}, \mathbf{V}_{m,\text{pr}}]\) and regularized by a cross-covariance disentanglement penalty \(\|\text{Cov}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{shared}}, \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{private}})\|_F^2 \to 0\).

2.5 Stage 5: Bijective Normalizing Flows Downstream of OLEE (Flow-SiMR-V vs. Flow-SiMR)

Although LEND provides non-linear decoding, an MLP decoder \(g_{\theta_m}: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\) is not analytically invertible and does not endow the latent space with an exact probability density for closed-form conditional inference across missing views or geodesic path integration.

2.5.1 Full-Space Flow-SiMR vs. OLEE-Projected Flow-SiMR-V

We distinguish two normalizing flow architectures in src/pysimlr/flows.py:

  1. Full-Space Normalizing Flow (Flow-SiMR, Figure 2.7): Applies an invertible sequence of RealNVP affine coupling layers directly on the ambient feature space \(\mathbb{R}^{p_m} \to \mathbb{R}^{p_m}\): \[ \mathbf{Z}_m = \text{Flow}_{\psi_m}(\mathbf{X}_m) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times p_m} \tag{2.41}\] partitioning the output into shared coordinates \(\mathbf{Z}_{m, 1:k}\) and private coordinates \(\mathbf{Z}_{m, k+1:p_m}\). While exact on low-dimensional views, Flow-SiMR scales as \(\mathcal{O}(p_m^2)\) per coupling layer and sacrifices input linearity.
  2. OLEE-Projected Latent Normalizing Flow (Flow-SiMR-V, Figure 2.8): Projects each modality onto the \(k\)-dimensional Stiefel/NSA-Flow manifold first, then applies an invertible RealNVP + ActNorm flow strictly within \(\mathbb{R}^k\): \[ \mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}, \quad \mathbf{Z}_m = \text{Flow}_{\psi_m}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}) \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k} \tag{2.42}\]

2.5.2 Proposition: Rectangular OLEE Prior Collapse and the Detached-Prior Solution

Combining a rectangular Stiefel projection \(\mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m)\) (\(p_m > k\)) with a normalizing flow introduces a subtle variational pathology if the Gaussian prior negative log-likelihood (NLL) is backpropagated naively into \(\mathbf{V}_m\).

Proposition (Rectangular OLEE Variance Collapse under Unconstrained Flow Prior Gradients):
Let \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}} = \mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times k}\) with \(\mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m)\) (\(p_m > k\)), and let \(\mathbf{Z}_m = f_{\psi_m}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}})\) be a diffeomorphism on \(\mathbb{R}^k\) regularized by the standard normal prior negative log-likelihood: \[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}(\mathbf{V}_m, \psi_m) = \frac{1}{2n} \left\| f_{\psi_m}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m) \right\|_F^2 - \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \log \left| \det J_{f_{\psi_m}}\left(\mathbf{x}_{m, i} \mathbf{V}_m\right) \right| \] While the square bijection \(f_{\psi_m}: \mathbb{R}^k \to \mathbb{R}^k\) is prevented from trivially contracting its output by its own log-Jacobian barrier \(-\log |\det J_{f_{\psi_m}}|\), the rectangular projection \(\mathbf{V}_m: \mathbb{R}^{p_m} \to \mathbb{R}^k\) has no log-determinant term in \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}\). Consequently, for any fixed flow with bounded Lipschitz constant, minimizing \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}\) with respect to \(\mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m)\) drives \(\mathbf{V}_m\) toward the minimum-variance eigenvectors of the sample covariance \(\frac{1}{n}\mathbf{X}_m^\top \mathbf{X}_m\): \[ \arg\min_{\mathbf{V}_m \in \text{St}(k, p_m)} \frac{1}{2n} \|\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\|_F^2 = \text{span}\{ \mathbf{e}_{p_m - k + 1}, \dots, \mathbf{e}_{p_m} \} \] rotating \(\mathbf{V}_m\) out of the biological signal subspace and into the lowest-variance noise directions (empirically collapsing \(\text{Var}(\mathbf{X}_1 \mathbf{V}_1)\) on ADNI-MCI from \(29.53\) at initialization to \(1.79\)).

Solution (Detached-Prior Variational Objective with ActNorm Scale Preservation):
In FlowSiMRVModel.forward (src/pysimlr/flows.py:920-934), we evaluate the normalizing flow prior NLL on the stop-gradient (detached) first-layer coordinates \(\text{sg}(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}})\) while keeping the full computational graph active for cross-view alignment and feature reconstruction: \[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{Flow-SiMR-V}}(\mathbf{V}_m, \psi_m) = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}\left(\mathbf{X}_m, f_{\psi_m}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_m) \mathbf{V}_m^\top\right) + \lambda_{\text{sim}} w_m \mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\left(f_{\psi_m}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m), \mathbf{U}_m\right) + \beta_{\text{prior}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{NLL}}\left(f_{\psi_m}(\text{sg}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m))\right) + \gamma D(\mathbf{V}_m) \tag{2.43}\] In this factored objective: 1. The first-layer basis \(\mathbf{V}_m\) is updated strictly by cross-view alignment \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{align}}\), ambient reconstruction \(\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}\), and Stiefel regularization \(D(\mathbf{V}_m)\)—never by the variance-shrinking prior norm. 2. The first layer of \(f_{\psi_m}\) is an invertible ActNorm layer initialized to \(s = -\log \text{std}(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m^{(0)})\), which maps \(\mathbf{Z}_{m,\text{lin}}\) to unit scale in the forward pass and automatically restores the true physical scale of \(\mathbf{X}_m \mathbf{V}_m\) during the inverse pass \(f_{\psi_m}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_m) \mathbf{V}_m^\top\).

2.5.3 Invertible Affine Coupling, Schur-Complement Conditional Inference, and Geodesics

Each flow \(f_{\psi_m}\) is a composition of RealNVP affine coupling layers (Dinh et al. 2016; Rezende and Mohamed 2015) with binary mask \(\mathbf{b} \in \{0, 1\}^k\): \[ \mathbf{z}_1 = \mathbf{b} \odot \mathbf{z}, \quad \mathbf{z}_2 = (1 - \mathbf{b}) \odot \left( \mathbf{z} \odot e^{s(\mathbf{z}_1)} + t(\mathbf{z}_1) \right), \quad \mathbf{z}' = \mathbf{z}_1 + \mathbf{z}_2 \tag{2.44}\] with exact analytical inverse: \[ \mathbf{z}_1 = \mathbf{b} \odot \mathbf{z}', \quad \mathbf{z}_2 = (1 - \mathbf{b}) \odot \left( (\mathbf{z}' - t(\mathbf{z}_1)) \odot e^{-s(\mathbf{z}_1)} \right), \quad \mathbf{z} = \mathbf{z}_1 + \mathbf{z}_2 \tag{2.45}\] Modeling the concatenated shared latent coordinates across views as a joint Gaussian: \[ \mathbf{Z}_{\text{joint}} = [\mathbf{Z}_{1,\text{shared}} \parallel \dots \parallel \mathbf{Z}_{M,\text{shared}}] \sim \mathcal{N}(\boldsymbol{\mu}, \Sigma) \tag{2.46}\] yields closed-form conditional cross-view inference for an unobserved view \(B\) given observed view(s) \(A\) via the Schur complement: \[ \boldsymbol{\mu}_{B|A} = \boldsymbol{\mu}_B + \Sigma_{BA} \Sigma_{AA}^{-1} (\mathbf{Z}_{A,\text{shared}} - \boldsymbol{\mu}_A), \qquad \hat{\mathbf{X}}_B = f_{\psi_B}^{-1}(\boldsymbol{\mu}_{B|A}) \mathbf{V}_B^\top \tag{2.47}\]
